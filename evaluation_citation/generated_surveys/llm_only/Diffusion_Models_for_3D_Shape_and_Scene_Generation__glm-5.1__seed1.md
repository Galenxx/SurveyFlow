# 1 Abstract
The generation of 3D shapes and scenes is a fundamental problem in computer vision and graphics, with applications spanning gaming, robotics, and virtual reality. Recently, diffusion models have emerged as a powerful class of generative models, surpassing traditional approaches like GANs and VAEs in terms of sample quality and diversity. This survey provides a comprehensive overview of the rapidly evolving landscape of diffusion models applied to 3D shape and scene generation. We systematically categorize existing literature based on three core dimensions: 3D representations, diffusion methodologies, and evaluation protocols. We first examine the diverse 3D representations employed, including point clouds, voxels, neural radiance fields, and efficient triplane representations, analyzing their trade-offs in fidelity and computational cost. Next, we delve into the diffusion methods themselves, ranging from unconditional generation to text-conditioned synthesis, highlighting the paradigm shift introduced by score distillation sampling that leverages 2D priors for 3D generation. Furthermore, we review the transition from isolated object generation to complex, compositional 3D scenes. Finally, we summarize the evaluation metrics and benchmarks currently used to assess generation quality. By synthesizing these strands, this survey aims to provide researchers with a structured understanding of the current state-of-the-art and to identify critical challenges that remain in the quest for scalable, high-fidelity 3D content creation. As the field continues to evolve at a rapid pace, establishing a clear taxonomy of methods and representations is essential for guiding future research efforts in this highly dynamic domain.

# 2 Introduction
The ability to generate realistic and diverse 3D content is a long-standing goal in computer vision and graphics. Traditionally, 3D asset creation has been a labor-intensive process requiring specialized expertise in modeling software. Generative models offer a promising avenue for democratizing 3D content creation, enabling applications in augmented reality, autonomous driving simulation, and video game design. While 2D image generation has seen remarkable success with the advent of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) and, more recently, diffusion models, the extension of these techniques to the 3D domain introduces unique and formidable challenges.

Unlike 2D images, which possess a regular grid structure, 3D data is inherently irregular and can be represented in numerous ways, such as point clouds, polygonal meshes, voxel grids, and continuous implicit fields. This irregularity complicates the direct application of standard convolutional architectures that underpin most 2D diffusion models. Furthermore, 3D data is often sparse and suffers from a higher dimensionality, leading to prohibitive computational costs for high-resolution generation. Early generative approaches for 3D, primarily based on GANs and Variational Autoencoders (VAEs), struggled with mode collapse, training instability, and limited resolution, failing to achieve the same level of success as their 2D counterparts.

The introduction of denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPMs) has fundamentally reshaped the generative landscape. By learning to reverse a gradual noising process, diffusion models offer a stable training objective and generate samples of unprecedented quality and diversity. The translation of diffusion models to the 3D domain has thus become a highly active area of research. Initial efforts focused on directly applying diffusion frameworks to discrete 3D representations like point clouds, demonstrating significant improvements in generation quality over GAN-based methods. However, generating high-fidelity 3D shapes and complex scenes requires overcoming the computational bottlenecks associated with 3D data.

To address these challenges, the field has witnessed a rapid proliferation of novel methods and representations. Researchers have explored latent diffusion models to decouple the high-dimensional 3D space from the diffusion process, and efficient factorized representations like triplanes to reduce memory footprints. Concurrently, a revolutionary paradigm emerged that bypasses the need for direct 3D diffusion altogether: Score Distillation Sampling (SDS). By leveraging pre-trained 2D diffusion models as a prior to optimize 3D representations, SDS has unlocked unprecedented text-to-3D capabilities, though not without introducing its own set of artifacts and limitations.

This survey aims to provide a structured and comprehensive review of the current state of diffusion models for 3D shape and scene generation. We focus on three critical aspects: the 3D representations utilized, the diffusion methodologies and architectures developed, and the evaluation metrics employed to benchmark progress. By systematically organizing the literature, we seek to highlight the trade-offs between different approaches, from direct point cloud diffusion to 2D-prior-based optimization, and from single-object generation to holistic scene synthesis. The remainder of this survey is structured as follows: Section 3 discusses the 3D representations used in diffusion models; Section 4 delves into the diffusion methods and architectures; Section 5 examines scene generation and compositionality; and Section 6 reviews evaluation metrics and benchmarks. Understanding these dimensions is crucial, as the choice of representation fundamentally constrains the feasible diffusion architectures, while the evaluation metrics dictate the perceived progress in the field. Ultimately, the convergence of scalable 3D representations and robust diffusion frameworks is paving the way for the next generation of 3D content creation tools, making this survey a timely resource for navigating the complex landscape of 3D generative modeling.

# 3 3D Representations for Diffusion Models
The choice of 3D representation is a foundational decision in designing a diffusion model, as it dictates the network architecture, computational complexity, and output fidelity. Early works primarily operated on point clouds due to their simplicity and compatibility with standard set-generating architectures. For instance, (Luo and Hu, 2021) introduced a diffusion model that directly generates 3D point clouds by progressively denoising a set of points, demonstrating that diffusion could outperform GANs in terms of diversity and quality for 3D shape generation. However, point clouds lack topological information and often require post-processing to obtain watertight meshes.

Voxels offer a structured alternative, enabling the use of 3D convolutions. Yet, the cubic scaling of voxel resolution with spatial dimensions severely limits the generation of high-resolution shapes. To overcome this, recent methods have turned to neural implicit representations, such as Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) and Signed Distance Functions (SDFs). These representations parameterize 3D geometry continuously, allowing for high-fidelity rendering at arbitrary resolutions. For example, (Metzer et al., 2022) proposed Latent-NeRF, which generates a latent code representation of a NeRF, combining the high-fidelity benefits of implicit fields with the efficiency of latent diffusion. More recently, triplane representations have emerged as a highly efficient middle ground, factorizing a 3D volume into three orthogonal 2D feature planes, drastically reducing memory costs while maintaining high rendering quality.

# 4 Diffusion Methods and Architectures
Diffusion methods for 3D generation can be broadly categorized into direct 3D diffusion and 2D-prior-based optimization. Direct 3D diffusion involves training a model from scratch on 3D datasets. While effective, this approach is constrained by the limited scale and diversity of current 3D datasets compared to their 2D counterparts. Latent diffusion models have been extensively used to mitigate the computational burden of operating directly in high-dimensional 3D spaces, training the diffusion process in a compressed latent space.

A paradigm shift occurred with the introduction of 2D-prior-based optimization methods, most notably Score Distillation Sampling (SDS). (Poole et al., 2022) introduced DreamFusion, which leverages a frozen pre-trained 2D text-to-image diffusion model to optimize a 3D representation (such as a NeRF). By computing gradients from the 2D diffusion model and projecting them back into 3D, SDS circumvents the need for large-scale 3D training datasets entirely. This approach has unlocked zero-shot text-to-3D generation capabilities. However, SDS-based methods often suffer from issues like the "Janus" problem (multi-faced artifacts) and over-saturation, driving subsequent research into refined distillation techniques and hybrid architectures that combine 2D priors with 3D-aware convolutions to ensure multi-view consistency.

# 5 Scene Generation and Compositionality
While the generation of isolated 3D objects has seen tremendous progress, extending these techniques to full 3D scenes introduces the challenge of compositionality. A 3D scene is not merely a collection of objects; it requires coherent spatial layout, realistic lighting, and occlusion handling. Early scene generation approaches often generated entire scenes as a single monolithic representation, which limited editability and scalability.

To address this, recent methods have focused on compositional scene generation, where a scene is assembled from individually generated objects. (Zeng et al., 2022) demonstrated how latent diffusion models can be applied to generate individual objects that are later composed, but ensuring global scene coherence remains difficult. More advanced techniques employ scene graphs or layout-conditioned diffusion models to guide the spatial arrangement of objects. By conditioning the generation process on bounding boxes or semantic maps, these models can control the placement and scale of individual entities while maintaining a harmonious global appearance. Furthermore, diffusion-based scene generation must handle background elements and ambient lighting, often requiring a combination of object-centric priors and holistic scene-level diffusion processes to achieve photorealistic and navigable 3D environments.

# 6 Evaluation Metrics and Benchmarks
Evaluating the quality and diversity of generated 3D shapes and scenes is a non-trivial task, as traditional 2D metrics like Inception Score (IS) are not directly applicable. The field has largely adopted metrics from the 3D GAN literature, focusing on both fidelity and coverage. For fidelity, the Frechet Inception Distance (FID) is commonly computed on multi-view renderings of the generated 3D assets, comparing the distribution of rendered images to that of real assets. Similarly, the Kernel Inception Distance (KID) is used for a less biased estimate.

For 3D-specific structural evaluation, metrics such as the Minimum Matching Distance (MMD) and Coverage (COV) are employed, which operate directly on point cloud representations extracted from the generated shapes. MMD measures the distance from generated samples to the nearest real samples, while COV assesses the fraction of the real dataset covered by the generated samples. However, as noted by (Shue et al., 2022), evaluating triplane and implicit representations requires careful rendering protocols to ensure fair comparisons. Furthermore, text-to-3D evaluation lacks standardized benchmarks, often relying on qualitative assessments or user studies. The development of robust, geometry-aware, and semantically meaningful evaluation metrics that can capture the intricacies of 3D scenes—such as physical plausibility and text alignment—remains an open and critical area of research.

# 7 Conclusion
The advent of diffusion models has catalyzed a paradigm shift in 3D shape and scene generation, overcoming many of the limitations that plagued earlier generative architectures like GANs and VAEs. This survey has systematically reviewed the current landscape, categorizing existing works by their 3D representations, diffusion methodologies, and evaluation protocols. We have observed a clear evolution in representations, moving from raw point clouds and memory-intensive voxel grids to efficient neural implicit fields and triplane representations. This transition has been crucial in scaling diffusion models to generate high-fidelity 3D assets without exhausting computational resources.

Methodologically, the field is bifurcated into direct 3D diffusion and 2D-prior-based optimization. Direct 3D diffusion models benefit from explicit 3D supervision and geometric consistency but are bottlenecked by the scarcity and limited diversity of 3D training data. In contrast, the introduction of Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) has revolutionized the field by tapping into the vast knowledge encoded in pre-trained 2D text-to-image diffusion models. While SDS-based methods have democratized text-to-3D generation, they frequently introduce multi-view inconsistencies and texture artifacts, highlighting a fundamental trade-off between data scalability and 3D coherence.

Furthermore, the transition from generating isolated objects to complex, compositional scenes remains a formidable challenge. Effective scene generation requires not only high-quality individual assets but also semantically and spatially coherent arrangements. Current approaches leveraging layout conditioning and scene graphs show promise but are still in their nascent stages. Similarly, the evaluation of generated 3D content relies heavily on 2D proxy metrics like FID, which fail to capture the full geometric and topological quality of 3D shapes. The lack of standardized, geometry-aware benchmarks for text-to-3D and scene generation hinders objective comparisons between methods.

In summary, diffusion models have undeniably propelled the field of 3D generation forward, transforming what was once a tedious manual process into an increasingly automated and accessible one. The convergence of scalable 3D representations, sophisticated diffusion frameworks, and powerful 2D priors has laid a robust foundation for the next generation of 3D content creation tools. However, achieving consistent, high-resolution, and physically plausible 3D scene generation remains an ongoing endeavor that requires continued innovation across representations, algorithms, and evaluation metrics.

# 8 Future Directions
As the field of diffusion models for 3D generation continues to mature, several promising research directions emerge. First, the development of large-scale, high-quality 3D datasets is paramount. Current direct 3D diffusion models are severely constrained by the scale and diversity of available 3D assets. Future efforts should focus on curating extensive datasets and developing self-supervised or weakly supervised techniques that can learn robust 3D priors from readily available 2D images and videos, potentially bridging the gap between 2D and 3D data availability.

Second, improving the efficiency and quality of 2D-prior-based optimization is a critical area. While SDS has enabled zero-shot 3D generation, it is computationally expensive and prone to artifacts like the Janus problem and over-smoothing. Future research should explore more efficient distillation techniques, perhaps by integrating 3D-aware attention mechanisms into pre-trained 2D models or developing hybrid diffusion frameworks that iteratively refine both 2D and 3D representations. Additionally, extending these models to handle dynamic 4D scenes—incorporating temporal consistency and motion—is a largely unexplored frontier that will be essential for video and animation applications.

Third, the integration of physical simulation and constraints into the generation process represents a vital step forward. Currently, most generated 3D shapes are purely visual and lack physical properties or structural integrity. Future diffusion models could be conditioned on physical constraints, ensuring that generated objects are not only visually realistic but also functionally plausible and ready for simulation in robotics or engineering contexts.

Finally, the establishment of standardized, comprehensive evaluation benchmarks is urgently needed. The field must move beyond 2D proxy metrics and develop geometric fidelity metrics, semantic alignment scores, and functional plausibility measures. Creating leaderboards and standardized datasets for text-to-3D and scene generation will facilitate fair comparisons and drive systematic progress. As these avenues are explored, the synergy between advanced diffusion models, scalable representations, and robust evaluation will unlock unprecedented capabilities in 3D content creation, paving the way for fully automated, high-fidelity virtual world generation.
