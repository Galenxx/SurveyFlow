# 1 Abstract
The advent of diffusion models has catalyzed a paradigm shift in generative modeling, achieving unprecedented fidelity in 2D image synthesis. Recently, this success has sparked a surge of interest in extending diffusion-based frameworks to 3D shape and scene generation. However, moving from 2D to 3D introduces formidable challenges, including the scarcity of high-quality 3D training data, the computational complexity of 3D representations, and the difficulty of ensuring multi-view consistency. This survey provides a comprehensive overview of the rapidly evolving landscape of diffusion models for 3D generation. We structure our analysis around three core pillars: methods, representations, and evaluation. First, we dissect the diverse 3D representations utilized by diffusion models, ranging from explicit structures like point clouds to implicit neural fields. Second, we categorize the methodological paradigms into native 3D diffusion, which directly operates on 3D data, and 2D-prior-based optimization, which leverages pre-trained 2D diffusion models to guide 3D synthesis. Third, we explore the extension of these techniques from isolated shape generation to holistic 3D scene composition and editing. Finally, we critically assess the current evaluation metrics and benchmarks used to measure fidelity, diversity, and consistency. By synthesizing these dimensions, this survey highlights the transformative potential of diffusion models in 3D vision while identifying critical open problems that warrant future research.

# 2 Introduction
Generative modeling has undergone a revolutionary transformation in recent years, largely propelled by the advent of denoising diffusion probabilistic models. By learning to reverse a gradual noising process, diffusion models have demonstrated an unparalleled capacity to generate high-fidelity, diverse samples, establishing state-of-the-art performance in 2D image and video synthesis. As the 2D domain reaches a degree of maturity, the research community is increasingly turning its attention to the more challenging frontier of 3D generation. The ability to automatically create high-quality 3D shapes and complex scenes holds immense promise for a multitude of applications, including virtual reality, gaming, architectural design, and robotic simulation.

Despite the tremendous potential, extending diffusion models from 2D pixels to 3D structures is far from trivial. The foremost challenge lies in the nature of 3D data itself. Unlike images, which possess a regular grid structure, 3D data is inherently irregular and can be represented in various forms—such as point clouds, polygonal meshes, voxel grids, and implicit neural fields—each carrying distinct computational trade-offs. Furthermore, the availability of large-scale, high-quality 3D datasets pales in comparison to the vast repositories of 2D images available on the internet, exacerbating the data scarcity problem. Finally, ensuring 3D consistency across different viewpoints during generation remains a significant technical hurdle, particularly for methods that rely on stitching together 2D projections.

To navigate these challenges, researchers have developed a diverse array of strategies. Broadly, these can be categorized into two dominant paradigms: native 3D diffusion and 2D-prior-based optimization. Native 3D diffusion involves training diffusion models directly on 3D representations, offering inherent multi-view consistency but suffering from limited scale and resolution due to computational constraints. Conversely, 2D-prior-based methods circumvent the data scarcity issue by leveraging pre-trained 2D diffusion models to guide the optimization of 3D representations, such as Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs), through techniques like Score Distillation Sampling (SDS). The seminal work on Score Jacobian Chaining also demonstrated the potential of lifting pretrained 2D models for this purpose (Wang et al., 2022). While this approach yields impressive visual fidelity, it often grapples with issues like the "Janus" problem and geometric inconsistencies. Recent systems have also demonstrated the ability to generate conditional 3D implicit functions directly from text prompts (Jun and Nichol, 2023).

The transition from generating isolated 3D shapes to generating complex, multi-object 3D scenes represents another critical axis of advancement. Scene generation requires not only modeling the geometry and appearance of individual objects but also capturing plausible spatial layouts, object interactions, and global illumination. Recent works have begun to address this by combining diffusion priors with scene graph representations or employing iterative generation pipelines that place objects within a coherent 3D environment.

The scope of this survey encompasses the most significant developments in 3D diffusion models published in recent years, with a particular emphasis on text-to-3D and image-to-3D generation. We intentionally focus on the intersection of diffusion models and 3D vision, excluding purely discriminative 3D tasks or 2D-only generation. The structure of this survey is as follows: Section 3 discusses the foundational 3D representations utilized in diffusion frameworks. Section 4 categorizes and analyzes the core methods for 3D shape generation. Section 5 extends this analysis to 3D scene generation and manipulation. Section 6 critically reviews the evaluation metrics and benchmarks currently employed in the field. Finally, Sections 7 and 8 offer concluding remarks and outline promising directions for future research.

# 3 3D Representations for Diffusion Models
The choice of 3D representation is a fundamental design decision that dictates the architecture of the diffusion model, the scalability of the training process, and the fidelity of the generated output. In the context of diffusion models, 3D representations are broadly categorized into explicit, implicit, and hybrid approaches.

Explicit representations, such as point clouds and voxel grids, offer intuitive and structured data formats that can be directly processed by standard diffusion architectures, such as U-Nets adapted for 3D. Point clouds, in particular, have emerged as a popular choice due to their lightweight nature and compatibility with existing 3D scanning datasets. For instance, systems generating 3D point clouds from text prompts leverage the simplicity of point clouds to achieve fast inference times, though they often lack the topological information required for high-quality rendering (Nichol et al., 2022). Conversely, voxel grids provide a regular structure but suffer from cubic memory scaling, limiting their resolution.

Implicit representations, most notably Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) and Signed Distance Functions (SDFs), encode geometry and appearance as continuous functions parameterized by neural networks. This allows for infinite resolution and compact storage. Diffusion models operating on implicit representations typically generate the weights of a multi-layer perceptron or a latent code that decodes into a 3D shape. Similarly, SDF-based diffusion models can produce high-fidelity surfaces by applying the denoising process directly to the latent space of SDFs (Cheng et al., 2023). Hybrid representations, such as tri-planes, attempt to combine the computational efficiency of explicit grids with the high fidelity of implicit fields, emerging as a highly efficient substrate for 3D diffusion.

# 4 Diffusion Methods for 3D Shape Generation
Current approaches to 3D shape generation using diffusion models can be broadly dichotomized into native 3D diffusion and 2D-prior-based optimization. Native 3D diffusion models are trained directly on datasets of 3D assets, learning the distribution of 3D shapes to synthesize new instances from noise. While these models guarantee inherent 3D consistency, their scalability is severely constrained by the limited availability and diversity of 3D training data, such as ShapeNet or Objaverse. Consequently, native 3D diffusion models often struggle to match the visual complexity and diversity exhibited by their 2D counterparts.

To overcome the data bottleneck, a revolutionary paradigm shift occurred with the introduction of 2D-prior-based optimization. These methods leverage the vast visual knowledge encapsulated in pre-trained 2D diffusion models, such as Stable Diffusion, to guide the optimization of a 3D representation. The seminal work introducing Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) demonstrated that a 3D representation like a NeRF could be optimized by extracting gradients from a 2D diffusion model, effectively "lifting" 2D priors into 3D (Poole et al., 2022). While SDS-based methods produce highly diverse and detailed 3D assets from text prompts, they are computationally expensive due to the per-instance optimization and are prone to artifacts like the multi-face "Janus" problem, where the model generates multiple front-facing views due to the lack of 3D awareness in the 2D prior. Subsequent methods have sought to improve upon this by employing coarse-to-fine optimization strategies or utilizing more sophisticated 2D priors to achieve high-resolution text-to-3D content creation (Lin et al., 2023).

# 5 Diffusion Methods for 3D Scene Generation
While single-object generation is a crucial stepping stone, the ultimate goal of many 3D vision applications is the generation of entire 3D scenes. Scene generation introduces additional layers of complexity, requiring models to reason about spatial layout, object inter-relationships, global illumination, and coherent backgrounds. Diffusion-based scene generation methods must not only produce high-quality individual assets but also seamlessly integrate them into a unified 3D environment.

One prominent approach to scene generation is the iterative, procedural generation of scenes using 2D diffusion priors. Instead of generating a full scene at once, these methods progressively construct the scene by iteratively painting the scene from different viewpoints and lifting the generated pixels into 3D space. For example, extracting textured 3D meshes from 2D text-to-image models can be achieved by iteratively generating images from chosen viewpoints and fusing them into a coherent 3D mesh representation (Höllein et al., 2023). This allows for the generation of complex, room-scale environments that maintain local geometric consistency.

Beyond generation from scratch, diffusion models are also being utilized for 3D scene editing and manipulation. The ability to modify specific aspects of a 3D scene based on textual instructions is vital for practical content creation. By integrating iterative dataset updates with diffusion-based image editing techniques, it is possible to edit 3D scenes with instructions, ensuring that changes are consistent across all viewpoints (Haque et al., 2023). These scene-level applications highlight the transition of diffusion models from isolated object generators to holistic world-building tools, though maintaining global consistency across large-scale scenes remains an active area of research.

# 6 Evaluation Metrics and Benchmarks
The rapid proliferation of 3D diffusion models has underscored the critical need for robust and standardized evaluation metrics. Evaluating 3D generated content is inherently more complex than evaluating 2D images, as it requires assessing both the visual fidelity of rendered views and the geometric validity of the underlying 3D structure. The community currently relies on a combination of 2D-derived metrics, 3D geometric metrics, and subjective user studies.

To evaluate visual fidelity and text alignment, researchers commonly adapt 2D metrics by rendering the generated 3D assets from multiple viewpoints. The Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) and Kernel Inception Distance (KID) are widely used to measure the distributional similarity between rendered images and real reference datasets. Additionally, CLIP scores are employed to quantify the semantic alignment between the input text prompt and the rendered 3D assets. For instance, evaluating the quality of 3D digital avatars often relies on FID scores calculated over rendered frontal views, alongside specialized metrics for texture and identity preservation (Wang et al., 2023).

However, 2D metrics alone are insufficient, as they can be easily fooled by models that produce visually appealing but geometrically implausible outputs, such as flat surfaces with painted depth. To directly assess 3D geometry, metrics such as Chamfer Distance and Intersection-over-Union (IoU) are utilized, though they require a canonical alignment and a ground-truth 3D mesh, which limits their applicability for large-scale, diverse generation. For methods that optimize NeRFs using 2D priors, evaluating the consistency and quality of the latent representations is also crucial (Metzer et al., 2023). The lack of a comprehensive, standardized benchmark that simultaneously evaluates texture, geometry, and text alignment remains a significant bottleneck in the field, making it difficult to objectively compare the rapidly emerging generation methods.

# 7 Conclusion
The intersection of diffusion models and 3D vision represents one of the most dynamic and transformative frontiers in artificial intelligence. This survey has charted the rapid evolution of diffusion-based 3D generation, from early experiments in native point cloud synthesis to the sophisticated 2D-prior-based optimization frameworks that dominate the current landscape. We have systematically examined the foundational representations—ranging from explicit point clouds to implicit neural fields—that serve as the building blocks for these generative processes. The dichotomy between native 3D diffusion and 2D-lifting methods highlights a fundamental trade-off in the field: native methods offer intrinsic 3D consistency but are bottlenecked by data scarcity and computational limits, whereas 2D-prior methods unlock unprecedented visual diversity but struggle with geometric fidelity and optimization artifacts like the Janus problem. Recent trends indicate a gradual convergence, where native 3D models are being pre-trained on larger datasets like Objaverse, and 2D-prior methods are incorporating stronger 3D biases to regularize the optimization process.

Furthermore, our analysis extended beyond isolated shape generation to encompass the holistic generation and editing of 3D scenes. The transition from object-centric to scene-centric generation marks a critical step toward practical applications in virtual reality and simulation, demanding that future models reason about spatial composition and global illumination. We also critically assessed the state of evaluation in the field, noting a significant reliance on 2D proxy metrics like FID and CLIP scores, which fail to fully capture the geometric integrity of 3D assets. The development of comprehensive 3D benchmarks remains an urgent necessity.

Ultimately, the progress detailed in this survey demonstrates that diffusion models are not merely a transitory trend but a foundational technology for 3D content creation. By synthesizing the disparate threads of representations, methods, and evaluation, we observe a field that is rapidly coalescing around hybrid approaches that combine the strengths of both 3D-native architectures and powerful 2D priors. As these technical barriers are surmounted, the democratization of 3D content creation is becoming a tangible reality, promising to revolutionize how we design, interact with, and experience digital worlds. The continued collaboration between the 2D generative modeling and 3D vision communities will be paramount in navigating the remaining challenges and unlocking the full potential of 3D diffusion models.

# 8 Future Directions
While the progress in diffusion models for 3D shape and scene generation is undeniably impressive, several critical avenues remain open for future exploration. Addressing these challenges will be essential for transitioning from impressive research prototypes to robust, industry-standard tools.

First, the issue of data scarcity and quality continues to loom large. Although datasets like Objaverse have provided a significant boost, they are still orders of magnitude smaller and less diverse than their 2D counterparts, such as LAION-5B. Future research must explore more efficient ways to learn from limited 3D data, potentially through self-supervised learning, synthetic data augmentation, or developing stronger 3D priors that can be fine-tuned with minimal data. Additionally, the community needs better-curated datasets with high-quality textures and physically based rendering properties.

Second, the generation of dynamic 4D content—3D scenes that evolve over time—represents a natural and highly demanded extension. Current diffusion models are largely static, and extending the temporal dimension introduces exponential computational complexity and the challenge of maintaining spatiotemporal consistency. Developing efficient 4D representations and adapting diffusion frameworks to handle temporal dynamics will be crucial for video game and film applications.

Third, there is a pressing need for improved controllability and composability in 3D generation. Existing models often struggle with precise spatial reasoning and fine-grained control over object attributes. Enabling users to dictate exact layouts, proportions, and interactions between objects through intuitive interfaces, such as scene graphs or detailed layout maps, will significantly enhance the utility of these models.

Finally, the establishment of standardized, comprehensive evaluation benchmarks is imperative. As discussed, current metrics are inadequate for capturing the full spectrum of 3D quality, particularly geometry and physical plausibility. Future efforts should focus on creating benchmark suites that evaluate texture, geometry, text alignment, and physical stability in a unified manner, enabling rigorous and fair comparisons across methodologies.
