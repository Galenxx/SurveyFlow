# 1 Abstract

Text-to-SQL generation, the task of translating natural language questions into executable SQL queries, has undergone a transformative evolution with the advent of Large Language Models (LLMs). This survey provides a comprehensive overview of the current landscape of Text-to-SQL in the era of LLMs, focusing on three critical pillars: schema linking, benchmarks, and robustness. We begin by examining how LLMs approach schema linking—the task of aligning natural language utterances with the relevant database schema elements—which remains a fundamental bottleneck in complex, cross-domain databases. We then review the shift in benchmark datasets, from traditional cross-domain datasets like Spider to more realistic and challenging benchmarks such as BIRD, which introduce large-scale, noisy, and domain-specific database scenarios requiring external knowledge. Furthermore, we analyze the robustness of LLM-based Text-to-SQL systems, highlighting their vulnerabilities to linguistic variations, schema complexity, and out-of-distribution queries, emphasizing the need for diagnostic evaluation. By synthesizing recent advancements and identifying persistent challenges, this survey outlines the trajectory from fine-tuned encoder-decoder models to in-context learning, decomposition prompting, and multi-agent collaboration frameworks. Ultimately, we aim to provide researchers with a structured understanding of the paradigm shifts in Text-to-SQL and to illuminate the critical pathways toward building more robust, reliable, and generalizable database querying systems powered by LLMs.

# 2 Introduction

Text-to-SQL serves as a critical bridge between non-technical users and complex relational databases, democratizing data access. For years, the field relied on specialized neural architectures, such as sequence-to-sequence models augmented with attention mechanisms and copy networks, which required extensive task-specific fine-tuning and struggled with generalization across domains. The introduction of pre-trained language models (PLMs) like BERT and T5 marked a significant leap, establishing new state-of-the-art results by leveraging generalized linguistic representations. However, the paradigm shifted entirely with the emergence of Large Language Models (LLMs) such as GPT-4 and Llama. These models possess unprecedented natural language understanding and code generation capabilities, enabling zero-shot and few-shot Text-to-SQL generation without the need for extensive parameter updates. This transition has fundamentally altered the research focus from feature engineering and model architecture to prompt engineering, context optimization, and error mitigation.

Despite their impressive out-of-the-box performance, LLMs face non-trivial challenges in reliable Text-to-SQL generation. Databases are highly structured environments, and generating accurate SQL requires strict adherence to the provided schema. This necessitates precise schema linking—the task of accurately mapping entities and relationships in the user's natural language question to the correct tables and columns, which is particularly challenging when schema elements are ambiguous or the query is complex. Furthermore, the evaluation of Text-to-SQL systems has evolved significantly. Early benchmarks focused primarily on syntactic correctness and simple joins within narrow domains, but modern benchmarks demand complex reasoning, integration of external domain knowledge, and the ability to handle dirty or realistic database values. Finally, while LLMs perform remarkably well on average cases, their robustness under distribution shifts, adversarial inputs, and complex nested queries remains a critical concern for enterprise deployment. A model that achieves high execution accuracy on a benchmark might still fail catastrophically on subtle linguistic variations or unseen database structures.

This survey focuses specifically on the intersection of Text-to-SQL generation and Large Language Models. We deliberately restrict our analysis to three foundational pillars that define the current frontier: schema linking, benchmarks, and robustness. We do not provide an exhaustive review of traditional PLM-based approaches, except where necessary to provide historical context or contrast with LLM methodologies. By narrowing our scope, we aim to provide a deeper, more targeted analysis of the specific hurdles that prevent LLM-based Text-to-SQL systems from achieving flawless reliability in production environments.

The remainder of this survey is structured as follows. Section 3 delves into schema linking, exploring how LLMs handle schema representation, alignment, and the challenges of long context windows when dealing with massive databases. Section 4 surveys the landscape of modern Text-to-SQL benchmarks, contrasting traditional datasets with contemporary large-scale evaluations that incorporate real-world noise, massive schemas, and domain knowledge. Section 5 examines the robustness of LLM-based Text-to-SQL systems, identifying failure modes, diagnostic methodologies, and vulnerabilities to linguistic and structural perturbations. Section 6 reviews advanced LLM methodologies, such as multi-agent collaboration systems and decomposition prompting strategies, specifically designed to overcome these robustness and linking challenges. Finally, Sections 7 and 8 offer concluding remarks and outline promising future research directions in the pursuit of reliable, production-ready, and highly generalizable Text-to-SQL systems.

# 3 Schema Linking in the Era of LLMs

Schema linking remains one of the most critical and challenging components of Text-to-SQL generation. It involves aligning the entities, attributes, and relationships expressed in a natural language question with their corresponding tables and columns in a database schema. In the era of LLMs, schema linking has transformed from a separate, often supervised module into an implicit expectation of the model's in-context learning capabilities. However, simply presenting the entire database schema to an LLM often leads to suboptimal performance, particularly for databases with a large number of tables and columns, due to context length limitations and attention dilution.

To address this, recent approaches have decoupled schema linking from SQL generation. For instance, methods like RESDSQL explicitly separate the schema linking process from the skeleton parsing of the SQL query, demonstrating that forcing a model to first identify relevant schema elements significantly improves generation accuracy (Cao et al., 2023). By framing schema linking as a classification or sequence labeling task before generation, models can filter out irrelevant schema items, thereby reducing the search space for the subsequent SQL synthesis.

Furthermore, the representation of the schema itself plays a vital role in LLM-based schema linking. Prompting strategies now include encoding schema relationships—such as primary and foreign keys—directly into the prompt using natural language descriptions or specialized formatting. Despite these advancements, LLMs still struggle with ambiguous column names or when the natural language phrasing diverges significantly from the schema terminology. The development of robust schema linking mechanisms that can handle implicit references and complex multi-hop relationships within massive schemas is crucial for the next generation of Text-to-SQL systems.

# 4 Evolution of Text-to-SQL Benchmarks

The evaluation of Text-to-SQL systems has historically been anchored by the Spider dataset, which provided a large-scale, cross-domain benchmark focusing on complex SQL queries and database generalization (Yu et al., 2018). While Spider catalyzed significant progress in developing models capable of handling nested queries and diverse joins, it operates under the assumption of clean, perfectly aligned database values and straightforward schema descriptions. As Text-to-SQL systems transition from research prototypes to real-world applications, the limitations of such traditional benchmarks have become apparent, necessitating the creation of more realistic evaluation frameworks.

Modern benchmarks have emerged to bridge this gap, introducing the complexities of real-world database environments. A prime example is the BIRD benchmark, which incorporates large-scale databases with dirty data, extensive domain knowledge, and massive schemas (Li et al., 2023). BIRD challenges models to not only understand the schema but also to reason over realistic data values and external knowledge that might be necessary to formulate the correct query. This shift reflects the understanding that real-world Text-to-SQL requires both schema comprehension and data-aware reasoning.

These new benchmarks expose the fragility of LLMs when faced with messy data distributions and the necessity for external knowledge retrieval. They also highlight the discrepancy between a model's ability to generate syntactically correct SQL and its ability to produce queries that return the correct results in a noisy environment. As benchmarks continue to evolve towards greater realism, they drive the development of more sophisticated retrieval-augmented generation and knowledge-grounded Text-to-SQL methodologies.

# 5 Robustness and Diagnostic Evaluation

While LLMs have achieved impressive execution accuracies on standard benchmarks, their robustness in Text-to-SQL tasks remains a significant concern. Robustness in this context refers to the model's ability to maintain performance when subjected to linguistic variations, paraphrased questions, schema perturbations, or out-of-distribution database structures. In real-world scenarios, users rarely formulate queries in the precise, unambiguous manner seen in training data, making robustness a prerequisite for deployment.

Diagnostic evaluations have revealed that LLMs are highly sensitive to prompt engineering and minor rephrasings of the natural language question. The Dr. Spider benchmark systematically categorizes and evaluates these robustness challenges, demonstrating that state-of-the-art models experience substantial performance drops when confronted with column name synonyms, database dialect changes, or question perturbations (Gan et al., 2021). Such findings indicate that LLMs often rely on spurious correlations and surface-level pattern matching rather than deep semantic understanding of the query and schema.

Addressing these robustness issues requires targeted strategies. Data augmentation and adversarial training have been proposed to immunize models against linguistic variations. Furthermore, self-correction mechanisms, where the LLM iteratively refines its generated SQL based on execution feedback or error messages, have shown promise in mitigating logical errors. However, ensuring consistent and reliable performance across the long tail of potential user inputs and schema designs remains an open problem, demanding continuous refinement of both model architectures and evaluation protocols.

# 6 Advanced Methodologies: Decomposition and Multi-Agent Systems

To overcome the limitations of single-prompt generation, particularly for complex queries requiring multi-step reasoning, the field has increasingly adopted advanced methodologies such as query decomposition and multi-agent systems. These approaches break down the monolithic Text-to-SQL task into manageable sub-tasks, allowing LLMs to focus on specific aspects like schema linking, query drafting, and debugging iteratively.

Decomposition strategies, such as DIN-SQL, instruct the LLM to first generate a natural language plan or decompose the complex query into simpler sub-queries before synthesizing the final SQL (Pourreza and Rafiei, 2023). This step-by-step reasoning mimics human problem-solving and significantly reduces the cognitive load on the model, leading to higher accuracy on nested and complex queries. By explicitly forcing the model to articulate its reasoning chain, decomposition also enhances interpretability and makes error diagnosis more straightforward.

Taking decomposition a step further, multi-agent frameworks like MAC-SQL assign specialized roles to different LLM agents, such as a selector for schema linking, a decomposer for query planning, and a refiner for self-correction (Wang et al., 2024). These agents collaborate through structured communication, iteratively refining the SQL query. The selector narrows down the schema, the decomposer breaks the question into logical steps, and the refiner executes and debugs the query. Such collaborative architectures leverage the strengths of LLMs in role-playing and contextual reasoning, representing a promising direction for building highly robust and scalable Text-to-SQL systems capable of handling enterprise-level complexity.

# 7 Conclusion

The advent of Large Language Models has undeniably revolutionized the field of Text-to-SQL generation, shifting the paradigm from heavily supervised, task-specific architectures to versatile, in-context learning frameworks. This survey has examined the current landscape of LLM-based Text-to-SQL through the critical lenses of schema linking, benchmarks, and robustness, revealing both remarkable advancements and persistent challenges.

Schema linking, once a straightforward alignment task, has evolved into a complex bottleneck when dealing with the massive and ambiguous schemas typical of real-world databases. While LLMs possess an inherent ability to infer relationships from context, their performance degrades without explicit schema filtering and representation strategies. The transition from traditional benchmarks like Spider to realistic, knowledge-intensive evaluations like BIRD has exposed the limitations of evaluating models solely on clean, synthetic data. Modern systems must now navigate dirty data values, massive schemas, and the integration of external domain knowledge to produce accurate SQL queries.

Furthermore, our analysis of robustness highlights a critical vulnerability: high execution accuracy on standard benchmarks does not translate to reliability in the face of linguistic variations or structural perturbations. LLMs remain susceptible to spurious correlations and surface-level patterns, as evidenced by diagnostic evaluations. The shift towards advanced methodologies, such as query decomposition and multi-agent collaboration, represents a necessary evolution. By breaking down complex generation tasks into specialized sub-tasks—schema linking, planning, and refinement—these frameworks mitigate the cognitive overload on individual models and enhance overall system reliability.

In summary, while LLMs have lowered the barrier to entry for basic Text-to-SQL tasks, achieving the robustness and precision required for enterprise-grade database interaction remains an ongoing endeavor. The field is moving away from the pursuit of higher benchmark numbers on simplified datasets towards building resilient, interpretable, and deeply reasoning systems. The integration of self-correction, targeted schema linking, and multi-agent collaboration are pivotal steps in this journey, ensuring that Text-to-SQL systems can reliably bridge the gap between complex human intent and structured database querying.

# 8 Future Directions

As the field of Text-to-SQL generation continues to mature in the era of Large Language Models, several promising research directions emerge. First, the development of dynamic and adaptive schema linking mechanisms is crucial. Future systems must move beyond static schema representations and learn to dynamically retrieve and encode schema elements based on the evolving context of a multi-turn conversation. As users refine their queries, the model should efficiently update its schema understanding without requiring a complete re-evaluation of the entire database structure.

Second, the intersection of Text-to-SQL and data privacy presents a critical frontier. As LLMs are increasingly deployed via cloud-based APIs, transmitting sensitive database schemas and user queries raises significant privacy concerns. Future research should focus on privacy-preserving Text-to-SQL techniques, such as federated learning, differential privacy, or the use of locally deployed, smaller open-source models that can match the performance of proprietary LLMs through specialized fine-tuning and knowledge distillation.

Third, there is a pressing need for more comprehensive and interactive evaluation benchmarks. Current benchmarks primarily assess single-turn, static queries. Future datasets should emulate the interactive, exploratory nature of real-world data analysis, where users iteratively query the database, correct model errors, and update their intent based on intermediate results. Evaluating models on their ability to handle these multi-turn, stateful interactions will provide a more accurate measure of their practical utility.

Finally, the integration of LLM-based Text-to-SQL with broader data analytics workflows offers exciting possibilities. Rather than merely generating SQL, future systems could act as comprehensive data agents, capable of generating code for data visualization, statistical analysis, and automated reporting. By combining Text-to-SQL with tool-use capabilities and multi-modal reasoning, we can envision intelligent agents that not only translate natural language into code but also synthesize actionable insights from the resulting data, fundamentally transforming how humans interact with structured information. The journey towards truly intelligent database interfaces is far from complete, but by addressing these critical avenues—dynamic schema linking, privacy preservation, interactive evaluation, and agentic workflows—the research community can unlock the full potential of LLMs in democratizing data access and analysis.
