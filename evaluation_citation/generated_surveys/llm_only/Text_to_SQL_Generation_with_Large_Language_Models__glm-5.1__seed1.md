# 1 Abstract
Text-to-SQL generation, which translates natural language questions into executable SQL queries, has undergone a transformative evolution with the advent of large language models (LLMs). While traditional models struggled with complex schema understanding and generalization, LLMs offer unprecedented semantic parsing capabilities through in-context learning and chain-of-thought reasoning. However, bridging the gap between ambiguous user queries and rigid database schemas remains a formidable challenge. This survey provides a comprehensive overview of Text-to-SQL generation in the LLM era, focusing on three critical pillars: schema linking, benchmarks, and robustness. We first examine schema linking, detailing how LLMs identify and align relevant database entities with user intents, moving from traditional relation-aware encodings to modern prompt-based linking. Next, we review the evolution of benchmarks, highlighting the transition from sterile, cross-domain datasets to realistic, large-scale evaluations that demand external knowledge and domain reasoning. We then analyze the robustness of LLM-based Text-to-SQL systems, discussing their vulnerability to schema complexity, prompt perturbations, and hallucination, alongside mitigation strategies like decomposition and multi-agent collaboration. By synthesizing recent advancements and identifying persistent bottlenecks, this survey outlines the current landscape of LLM-driven Text-to-SQL and provides a foundation for future research aimed at building reliable, scalable, and robust natural language database interfaces. Ultimately, we aim to equip researchers with a structured understanding of how LLMs are reshaping the Text-to-SQL landscape and the critical milestones required to achieve production-grade reliability.

# 2 Introduction
The intersection of natural language processing and database management has long been epitomized by the Text-to-SQL task, which seeks to democratize data access by allowing users to query databases using natural language. For years, the field was dominated by specialized sequence-to-sequence models and graph neural networks that required extensive task-specific architectural designs and fine-tuning. These conventional approaches often faltered when confronted with complex queries, intricate database schemas, or domain shifts, limiting their applicability in real-world scenarios.

The emergence of Large Language Models (LLMs) has fundamentally altered this landscape. Models such as GPT-4 and Llama have demonstrated remarkable zero-shot and few-shot capabilities, drastically reducing the reliance on heavily curated training data. By leveraging in-context learning and instruction following, LLMs can decompose complex questions, reason over schema relationships, and generate syntactically correct SQL. However, translating this latent capability into consistent, reliable performance introduces a new set of challenges that form the motivation for this survey.

First, schema linking—the process of aligning entities and relationships in the natural language query with the corresponding columns and tables in the database—remains a critical bottleneck. While LLMs possess vast semantic knowledge, they often struggle with the exact matching required by rigid database schemas, especially when database identifiers are cryptic or heavily abbreviated. Second, the evaluation landscape has shifted. Early benchmarks provided clean, well-structured databases, but modern datasets now emphasize real-world messiness, requiring models to integrate external domain knowledge to generate accurate queries. Third, the robustness of LLM-based Text-to-SQL systems is an ongoing concern. LLMs are sensitive to prompt formatting, prone to hallucination, and often fail silently on complex nested queries.

Prior to the LLM era, state-of-the-art systems relied on sophisticated pre-training regimes and complex architectures like graph neural networks to encode schema structure. These models required substantial labeled data for every new database domain, rendering deployment costly and inflexible. The paradigm shift introduced by LLMs replaced much of this architectural complexity with prompt engineering and contextual grounding. Instead of learning schema representations from scratch, LLMs serialize the schema into the prompt, relying on their pre-trained weights to infer relationships. Yet, this serialization introduces its own challenges, such as context window limitations for large schemas and attention dilution over long prompts. Consequently, the research focus has pivoted from designing specialized neural modules to optimizing how schema information is presented, how errors are corrected iteratively, and how models can be fine-tuned efficiently.

This survey captures this pivotal transition, systematically exploring these three dimensions—schema linking, benchmarks, and robustness—to provide a holistic view of the current state of Text-to-SQL with LLMs. We review how the community is addressing schema linking through advanced prompting and decomposition techniques. We trace the evolution of benchmarks from foundational datasets to contemporary, knowledge-intensive evaluations. Furthermore, we analyze the robustness challenges inherent to LLM-driven generation and explore mitigation strategies, ranging from self-correction mechanisms to multi-agent collaboration and targeted fine-tuning of open-source models.

The remainder of this paper is structured as follows: Section 3 delves into schema linking methodologies; Section 4 examines evolving benchmarks; Section 5 explores prompting and decomposition strategies; Section 6 discusses robustness and fine-tuning; Section 7 concludes the survey; and Section 8 outlines promising future directions.

# 3 Schema Linking in the Era of LLMs
Schema linking is the cornerstone of successful Text-to-SQL generation, serving as the bridge between the user's natural language query and the structured database schema. It involves identifying the tables and columns relevant to the user's question and disambiguating references that may be implicit or phrased differently from the schema's metadata. In the pre-LLM era, this was typically addressed using relation-aware encoders that explicitly modeled the edges between question tokens and schema items. For instance, RAT-SQL introduced a relation-aware self-attention mechanism to jointly encode the question and schema, allowing the model to learn structural biases directly from the data (Wang et al., 2019).

With the advent of LLMs, schema linking has shifted from an architectural feature to a prompting and pre-processing challenge. Because LLMs process serialized text, the way a schema is presented in the prompt profoundly impacts linking accuracy. Simply listing schema metadata often leads to attention dilution, causing the model to overlook relevant columns, especially in databases with hundreds of tables. Modern approaches tackle this by explicitly injecting schema links into the prompt. For example, S3 introduces a framework that generates schema links as an intermediate step before SQL generation, effectively decoupling the linking task from the synthesis task and guiding the LLM's attention to the correct database entities (Yang et al., 2023). By structuring the prompt to highlight these links, LLMs can significantly reduce hallucination and improve the precision of the generated SQL.

# 4 Evolving Benchmarks and Evaluation
The trajectory of Text-to-SQL research is inextricably linked to the benchmarks that define it. For years, the Spider dataset stood as the definitive benchmark, driving progress by providing a large-scale, cross-domain evaluation of complex SQL generation (Yu et al., 2018). Spider's contribution was monumental, moving the field beyond single-table, Atis-style datasets to complex, multi-join queries across diverse domains. However, Spider and its derivatives assume clean, well-designed schemas and questions that can be answered solely from the database content.

The emergence of LLMs exposed the limitations of these clean benchmarks, as models quickly achieved high execution accuracies, suggesting the task was "solved." In response, the community has developed new benchmarks that reflect the messy reality of real-world databases. The BIRD benchmark represents a significant leap in this direction, introducing large-scale databases with messy data, domain-specific jargon, and the necessity for external knowledge to form correct queries (J. Li et al., 2024). In BIRD, questions often require reasoning over values not explicitly present in the database schema, demanding that models possess or acquire domain expertise. This shift has fundamentally altered evaluation metrics, emphasizing not just SQL syntax and execution accuracy, but also the efficiency of the generated queries and the model's ability to handle real-world noise and knowledge gaps.

# 5 Prompting and Decomposition Strategies
The zero-shot and few-shot capabilities of LLMs have made prompting the primary mechanism for Text-to-SQL generation. However, naive prompting—simply providing the schema and the question—often fails on complex queries involving nested subqueries or multiple aggregations. To overcome this, researchers have developed sophisticated prompting strategies that decompose the generation process into manageable steps. The C3 framework demonstrates the power of this approach by utilizing ChatGPT's zero-shot capabilities through a combination of clear prompt construction, calibration, and consistency checking, significantly boosting baseline performance without any fine-tuning (Dong et al., 2023).

Taking decomposition further, DIN-SQL reframes Text-to-SQL as a multi-step reasoning problem. It employs decomposed in-context learning, where the LLM is prompted to first identify the relevant tables and columns, then generate the SQL skeleton, and finally fill in the details, followed by a self-correction step to debug any execution errors (Pourreza and Rafiei, 2023). This chain-of-thought-like decomposition aligns well with the autoregressive nature of LLMs, allowing them to focus on one sub-task at a time. By breaking down the complex mapping from natural language to SQL, these strategies mitigate the cognitive load on the model and substantially improve accuracy on challenging datasets.

# 6 Robustness and Fine-Tuning
Despite the impressive performance of proprietary LLMs like GPT-4, their Text-to-SQL generation remains brittle. Models are highly sensitive to prompt formatting, synonym variations, and schema complexity. A comprehensive evaluation of ChatGPT's zero-shot capabilities revealed significant drops in performance when faced with complex schemas or ambiguous queries, highlighting that LLMs are not yet reliable out-of-the-box database interfaces (Chang and Fosler-Lussier, 2023). Furthermore, reliance on proprietary models raises concerns regarding data privacy and inference costs, driving the need for robust, open-source alternatives.

To address these robustness and accessibility issues, the field is increasingly turning to targeted fine-tuning of open-source LLMs. By curating high-quality, task-specific datasets, researchers can instill stronger schema linking and SQL generation capabilities into smaller models. CodeS is a prominent example, building an open-source language model specifically for Text-to-SQL through extensive fine-tuning on synthetic and distilled data, achieving competitive performance with a fraction of the parameters (H. Li et al., 2024). Additionally, multi-agent frameworks like MAC-SQL enhance robustness by delegating different aspects of the task—such as schema linking, SQL drafting, and debugging—to specialized agents, creating a collaborative system that is more resilient to errors than a single monolithic model.

# 7 Conclusion
The transition from specialized neural architectures to Large Language Models has fundamentally reshaped the landscape of Text-to-SQL generation. This survey has traced this evolution through the critical lenses of schema linking, benchmarks, and robustness. We have observed that while LLMs possess unparalleled semantic understanding and generative capabilities, they do not inherently solve the structural challenges of mapping natural language to rigid database schemas. Schema linking remains a pivotal bottleneck, shifting from an encoder-level feature in pre-LLM models to a complex prompting and pre-processing challenge that requires explicit strategies to guide LLM attention. Techniques that explicitly inject schema links or decouple linking from generation have proven essential for mitigating hallucinations.

Furthermore, the evaluation landscape has matured significantly. The era of clean, synthetic benchmarks like Spider has given way to realistic, knowledge-intensive datasets like BIRD, which expose the fragility of LLM-driven systems when confronted with real-world database messiness and domain-specific reasoning. These new benchmarks highlight that generating syntactically correct SQL is insufficient; models must also generate efficient queries and reason over domain knowledge. In response, the community has developed sophisticated decomposition and prompting strategies, such as DIN-SQL, to break down complex generation tasks into manageable, sequential steps. These methods leverage the inherent reasoning capabilities of LLMs, transforming a monolithic translation task into an interpretable pipeline.

Yet, the brittleness of zero-shot prompting and the privacy concerns of proprietary models underscore the necessity of robust, open-source alternatives. Fine-tuned models like CodeS and multi-agent collaborative frameworks like MAC-SQL represent vital steps toward making Text-to-SQL systems more reliable, accessible, and secure. By distilling the capabilities of massive models into smaller, specialized ones, and by distributing cognitive load across multiple agents, the field is actively addressing the robustness gap. Ultimately, while LLMs have dramatically elevated the state-of-the-art, achieving production-grade robustness requires continued innovation in how these models interact with structured data, correct their own errors, and adapt to the diverse and messy realities of real-world databases.

# 8 Future Directions
As the field of LLM-based Text-to-SQL continues to mature, several promising research directions emerge that promise to bridge the gap between academic benchmarks and production-ready systems. First, the development of multi-turn Text-to-SQL systems is critical. Real-world data exploration is rarely a single-shot query; users iteratively refine their questions based on previous results, ask follow-ups, or pivot their analysis. Future systems must maintain contextual coherence over extended dialogues, managing ambiguous references and dynamic schema filtering seamlessly. Current benchmarks largely neglect this conversational complexity, necessitating new datasets and evaluation metrics for multi-turn interactions.

Second, agentic workflows and tool integration will likely define the next generation of Text-to-SQL. By equipping LLMs with the ability to execute queries, inspect error messages, and query data profiles dynamically, systems can move from static generation to interactive, self-correcting loops. This "acting" paradigm allows the model to verify its output against the database and refine its logic, drastically improving reliability. Third, addressing dynamic and evolving schemas presents a significant frontier. In production environments, databases are not static; columns are added, tables are renamed, and schemas are modified. Future research must explore how LLMs can adapt to schema drift without requiring extensive re-fine-tuning, perhaps through dynamic schema indexing or continuous learning mechanisms.

Fourth, the integration of multimodal inputs is an exciting avenue. Users often formulate questions based on visual artifacts like charts, dashboards, or tables in documents. Enabling LLMs to ground their SQL generation in both textual and visual contexts will dramatically expand the accessibility and applicability of data interfaces. Finally, the pursuit of data privacy and efficiency will drive advancements in localized, on-device Text-to-SQL models. As open-source models become more capable through specialized fine-tuning, creating highly compressed, efficient models that can run locally on enterprise servers will alleviate the privacy concerns associated with sending proprietary database schemas to external APIs. Overcoming these challenges will transform Text-to-SQL from a benchmark-driven academic task into a robust, ubiquitous interface for enterprise data management.
