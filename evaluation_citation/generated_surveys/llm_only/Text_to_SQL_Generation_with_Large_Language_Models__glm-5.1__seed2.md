# 1 Abstract
Text-to-SQL generation, which translates natural language questions into executable SQL queries, has undergone a transformative shift with the advent of large language models (LLMs). This survey provides a comprehensive overview of the current landscape of Text-to-SQL, focusing on three critical pillars: schema linking, evolving benchmarks, and system robustness. We begin by examining schema linking, a pivotal process for aligning natural language utterances with database schemas, and detail how LLMs leverage advanced prompting and decomposition strategies to overcome traditional bottlenecks associated with complex database structures. Next, we explore the transition from standard, clean datasets like Spider to more realistic, large-scale benchmarks such as BIRD, which introduce the complexities of dirty data, massive databases, and external knowledge requirements that mirror real-world applications. Furthermore, we analyze the robustness of LLM-based Text-to-SQL systems, identifying vulnerabilities to schema complexity, linguistic variations, and prompt sensitivity, while reviewing mitigation strategies like multi-agent collaboration and self-correction mechanisms. We also review recent open-source model developments tailored specifically for database tasks. By synthesizing these dimensions, this survey highlights the paradigm shift from traditional fine-tuning to in-context learning and agentic frameworks, outlining the remaining challenges in achieving reliable, production-ready Text-to-SQL systems. Ultimately, we aim to provide researchers and practitioners with a structured understanding of recent advancements and the fundamental open problems that define the future of LLM-driven database interaction.

# 2 Introduction
The intersection of natural language processing and database management has long been epitomized by the Text-to-SQL task, which seeks to bridge the gap between human language and structured query languages. By enabling non-expert users to query complex databases using natural language, Text-to-SQL systems democratize data access and significantly enhance productivity across various industries. Historically, this field was dominated by task-specific architectures, such as sequence-to-sequence models, and fine-tuning approaches on relatively small, domain-specific datasets. However, the emergence of Large Language Models (LLMs) like GPT-4 and Llama has fundamentally reshaped the paradigm, shifting the focus from extensive supervised training to sophisticated prompt engineering, in-context learning, and agentic workflows (Katsogiannis-Meimarakis and Koutrika, 2023).

Despite the remarkable reasoning and generation capabilities of LLMs, Text-to-SQL generation remains a highly challenging problem, primarily due to the intricate nature of real-world databases. Three core challenges currently dominate the research landscape: schema linking, benchmark limitations, and system robustness. First, schema linking—the process of mapping entities and relationships in a natural language question to the corresponding columns and tables in a database—is a critical bottleneck. As database schemas grow in size and complexity, LLMs struggle to attend to the relevant schema components, often generating queries that are syntactically correct but semantically flawed due to incorrect table selections or joins. Effective schema linking is thus a prerequisite for high-accuracy Text-to-SQL generation.

Second, the evaluation of Text-to-SQL systems has outpaced traditional benchmarks. While early datasets like Spider provided an essential foundation for cross-domain evaluation, they often relied on clean, well-structured databases and unambiguous questions. Real-world databases, conversely, are characterized by messy data, obscure column names, and massive scale, necessitating the development of next-generation benchmarks that better reflect these conditions. The transition to these realistic benchmarks has revealed significant performance gaps in even the most advanced LLMs, highlighting the discrepancy between controlled evaluation and practical deployment.

Third, robustness remains a critical concern. LLM-based Text-to-SQL systems are notoriously sensitive to prompt phrasing, question ambiguity, and schema formatting. Minor linguistic variations in the user's question can lead to drastically different SQL outputs. Furthermore, the propensity of LLMs to hallucinate schema elements or misinterpret complex join conditions poses significant reliability challenges for deployment in production environments.

This survey aims to provide a structured and comprehensive review of Text-to-SQL generation in the era of LLMs, with a specific focus on the triad of schema linking, benchmarks, and robustness. We explore how recent methodologies have evolved to address the limitations of earlier models, highlighting the shift towards decomposed prompting, multi-agent collaboration, and specialized open-source models. The scope of this survey encompasses the most significant developments from the advent of LLMs through the latest advancements in 2024, providing both a historical context and a forward-looking perspective.

The remainder of this survey is organized as follows. Section 3 delves into schema linking, examining both decoupled and integrated strategies within LLM frameworks. Section 4 discusses the evolution of benchmarks, contrasting traditional datasets with modern, realistic evaluation frameworks. Section 5 investigates the robustness of Text-to-SQL systems, analyzing vulnerabilities and current mitigation strategies. Section 6 reviews the rise of open-source models and specialized frameworks designed to democratize and stabilize Text-to-SQL generation. Finally, Sections 7 and 8 offer concluding remarks and outline promising future research directions.

# 3 Schema Linking in the Era of LLMs
Schema linking is a foundational step in Text-to-SQL, involving the alignment of natural language mentions with their corresponding database schema elements. In the LLM era, the sheer size of real-world databases presents a significant context window and attention challenge. When presented with hundreds of tables and columns, LLMs often suffer from the "lost in the middle" phenomenon, leading to incorrect column selection or missed joins.

To mitigate this, recent approaches have shifted towards decoupling schema linking from SQL generation. Instead of prompting the LLM to generate SQL directly from the full schema, models first filter the schema to a relevant subset. For example, RESDSQL explicitly decouples schema linking from skeleton parsing, employing a cross-encoder to rank and filter schema items before generating the SQL skeleton (Li et al., 2023). This two-step process drastically reduces the search space for the generative model.

Alternatively, in-context learning strategies have been refined to guide the LLM's attention during schema linking. DIN-SQL employs a decomposed prompting strategy where the LLM is first asked to identify the relevant tables and columns step-by-step before writing the query (Pourreza and Rafiei, 2023). By breaking down the schema linking process into intermediate reasoning steps, DIN-SQL significantly improves the model's ability to handle complex schemas, demonstrating that explicit schema linking remains a critical lever for maximizing LLM performance in Text-to-SQL tasks.

# 4 Evolving Benchmarks and Evaluation
The evaluation of Text-to-SQL systems has historically relied on the Spider dataset, which standardized cross-domain evaluation by providing a diverse set of databases and complex query structures (Yu et al., 2018). While Spider catalyzed early progress, its databases are often clean, small, and lack the messiness characteristic of real-world environments. This discrepancy has led to a false sense of maturity in the field, as high accuracy on Spider does not translate to reliable real-world performance.

To bridge this gap, the community has developed next-generation benchmarks that introduce realistic complexities. BIRD is a prominent example, offering a large-scale benchmark grounded in real-world databases that contain dirty data, massive row counts, and obscure schema names (Li et al., 2023). Furthermore, BIRD introduces external knowledge requirements, acknowledging that generating correct SQL often requires domain knowledge not explicitly present in the database schema. The stark performance drop of state-of-the-art LLMs on BIRD compared to Spider underscores the ongoing challenges in handling realistic data distributions (Gao et al., 2023) and highlights the necessity for benchmarks that rigorously test a model's ability to reason over imperfect information.

# 5 Robustness and Generalization
While LLMs exhibit impressive zero-shot and few-shot capabilities, their robustness in Text-to-SQL tasks remains a critical vulnerability. Robustness in this context refers to the model's ability to maintain performance despite linguistic variations in the prompt, paraphrased questions, or structural perturbations in the database schema. Minor rephrasing by a user can lead to entirely different, often incorrect, SQL queries, making LLMs unreliable for enterprise applications.

To enhance robustness, researchers have explored various prompting and architectural strategies. The C3 framework demonstrates that carefully crafted zero-shot prompts, combined with schema filtering and consistency checks, can significantly stabilize ChatGPT's performance across different phrasing of the same question (Dong et al., 2023). By enforcing consistency through multiple sampling and voting, C3 mitigates the inherent randomness of LLM generations.

More recently, multi-agent systems have emerged as a robust alternative to single-prompt approaches. MAC-SQL utilizes a collaborative framework where different LLM agents assume specialized roles, such as schema linking, SQL generation, and error correction (Wang et al., 2024). By decomposing the task and allowing agents to iteratively review and correct each other's outputs, multi-agent frameworks introduce a self-correcting mechanism that dramatically improves the reliability and robustness of the final SQL output, addressing the fragility of monolithic LLM calls.

# 6 Open-Source Models and Frameworks
The dominance of proprietary models like GPT-4 in Text-to-SQL raises significant privacy and deployment concerns, particularly for enterprises handling sensitive data. This has spurred the development of open-source LLMs and frameworks specifically tailored for database tasks. General-purpose open-source models often lag behind their proprietary counterparts in complex reasoning, necessitating targeted adaptation.

CodeS represents a significant step towards building open-source language models explicitly for Text-to-SQL (Hai et al., 2024). By continually pre-training and fine-tuning open-source base models on specialized SQL and schema data, CodeS achieves competitive performance while ensuring data privacy and local deployment capabilities.

Concurrently, specialized prompting frameworks have been developed to maximize the efficiency of open-source models. PET-SQL introduces a prompt-enhanced two-stage framework that systematically optimizes the prompt structure for schema representation and question decomposition (Li et al., 2024). These efforts demonstrate that combining tailored open-source models with structured, systematic prompting frameworks can narrow the gap with proprietary models, offering a viable and secure alternative for production Text-to-SQL systems.

# 7 Conclusion
The advent of Large Language Models has undeniably revolutionized the field of Text-to-SQL generation, shifting the paradigm from rigid, fine-tuned architectures to flexible, prompt-driven, and agentic systems. This survey has examined the current landscape through the critical lenses of schema linking, evolving benchmarks, and system robustness, revealing both remarkable progress and persistent challenges.

Schema linking has emerged as a central battleground in the LLM era. As databases grow in complexity, the inability of LLMs to consistently attend to relevant schema elements remains a primary source of error. The shift towards decoupled strategies, where schema linking is explicitly handled before SQL generation, has proven highly effective. By reducing the search space and guiding the LLM's attention, methods like RESDSQL and DIN-SQL have set new performance standards, underscoring the necessity of structured intermediate steps.

Concurrently, the evolution of benchmarks from the clean, controlled environments of Spider to the messy, realistic databases of BIRD has fundamentally recalibrated our understanding of Text-to-SQL maturity. These modern benchmarks expose the fragility of LLMs when confronted with dirty data, massive schemas, and the need for external knowledge. They serve as a crucial reminder that high accuracy on synthetic or simplified datasets is insufficient for real-world deployment.

Robustness remains the most significant hurdle for enterprise adoption. The sensitivity of LLMs to prompt variations and their propensity for hallucination necessitate sophisticated mitigation strategies. The transition from single-prompt solutions to multi-agent collaborative frameworks represents a promising direction, leveraging self-correction and specialized roles to stabilize outputs. Furthermore, the rise of open-source, specialized models like CodeS and structured frameworks like PET-SQL addresses critical privacy and deployment concerns, democratizing access to high-performance Text-to-SQL systems.

In summary, while LLMs have propelled Text-to-SQL into a new era of capability, achieving reliable, production-grade performance requires moving beyond naive prompting. The integration of explicit schema linking, rigorous evaluation on realistic benchmarks, and robust, multi-agent architectures are essential steps toward building the next generation of database interaction systems.

# 8 Future Directions
As the field of Text-to-SQL continues to evolve in the era of Large Language Models, several promising research directions emerge from the challenges identified in this survey.

First, the development of dynamic and interactive schema linking mechanisms is crucial. Current schema linking approaches are largely static, processing the schema in a single pass before generation. Future systems should explore interactive schema linking, where the model can iteratively query the database to explore schema relationships, verify column contents, and refine its understanding based on intermediate execution results. This would more closely mimic the behavior of human analysts and significantly improve accuracy on complex, nested queries.

Second, there is a pressing need for benchmarks that incorporate multi-modal and conversational Text-to-SQL scenarios. Real-world data querying is rarely a single-turn interaction; it involves clarifying questions, referencing previous queries, and sometimes interpreting visual data alongside tabular schemas. Future datasets must capture this multi-turn, multi-modal complexity to drive the development of truly conversational database agents.

Third, enhancing the efficiency and scalability of robustness mechanisms, such as multi-agent collaboration and self-correction, is vital. While frameworks like MAC-SQL improve reliability, they incur substantial computational overhead due to multiple LLM calls and iterative refinement. Research into more efficient verification mechanisms, perhaps through smaller, specialized critic models or neuro-symbolic verification, could provide the benefits of self-correction without the prohibitive latency and cost.

Fourth, the intersection of Text-to-SQL and data privacy will become increasingly important. As open-source models become more capable, techniques like federated learning and differential privacy must be integrated into Text-to-SQL pipelines to ensure that querying sensitive databases does not leak proprietary information through the model's outputs or fine-tuning processes.

Finally, the integration of Text-to-SQL agents into broader data analysis workflows presents an exciting frontier. Rather than treating Text-to-SQL as an isolated translation task, future systems should act as autonomous data analysts, capable of not only generating SQL but also determining when to write code, visualizing results, and providing narrative summaries. This holistic approach will transform Text-to-SQL from a simple interface into a comprehensive analytical engine, unlocking the full potential of LLMs in enterprise data management.
