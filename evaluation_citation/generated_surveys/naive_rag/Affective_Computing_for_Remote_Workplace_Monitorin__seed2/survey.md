# 1 Abstract

The integration of affective computing into workplace monitoring systems marks a significant shift from traditional biometric tracking focused on physical presence to the sensing and evaluation of workers’ emotional states. Commercially termed Emotional AI, these technologies leverage advances in affective computing to read, monitor, and assess the affective conditions of employees in real time. This survey examines the emerging landscape of Emotional AI in organizational settings, mapping its technological foundations, application domains, and the socio-demographic factors shaping workforce acceptance across different cultural contexts. Our analysis synthesizes current research on the capabilities and limitations of these systems, highlighting critical concerns regarding privacy, consent, and the potential for emotional manipulation. We find that while Emotional AI promises enhanced productivity and well-being monitoring, its deployment raises substantial cross-cultural friction and ethical challenges that remain inadequately addressed in both policy and practice. The survey further identifies key gaps in empirical research on long-term psychological impacts and the need for robust regulatory frameworks. This review underscores the urgent need for interdisciplinary approaches to govern the proliferation of affective monitoring technologies in the workplace.

# 2 Introduction

The modern workplace is undergoing a profound transformation driven by the integration of advanced computational systems into everyday professional activities. Among these developments, the domain of affective computing has emerged as a particularly significant yet contentious field. Affective computing, which focuses on the design of systems capable of recognizing, interpreting, and responding to human emotional states, has expanded its reach from laboratory settings and consumer applications into organizational environments. This expansion has been accelerated by the proliferation of remote and hybrid work models, where traditional forms of managerial oversight have become less feasible. Employers increasingly seek technological solutions that can provide insights into employee well-being, engagement, and productivity across distributed teams. The technological underpinnings of modern workplace monitoring have thus evolved substantially beyond simple presence tracking or keystroke logging. A new generation of biometric devices and software platforms has emerged that can sense, read, monitor, and evaluate the affective state of a worker, a technology commercially recognized as Emotional AI. This shift represents a significant departure from earlier forms of biometric monitoring, which were primarily oriented toward tracking physical presence or task completion rather than interpreting internal emotional conditions.

Despite the growing commercial interest in affective computing for remote workplace monitoring, the field is fraught with unresolved challenges that span technical, social, and ethical domains. A significant challenge lies in the socio-demographic and cross-cultural acceptance of affective monitoring in professional settings, as workforce attitudes toward such technologies vary considerably across different cultural contexts and demographic groups. The deployment of systems designed to assess employee emotional states introduces open problems regarding the potential for cross-cultural friction, given that emotional expression and interpretation are deeply culturally mediated. Furthermore, the sensing modalities employed in these systems, ranging from facial expression analysis and voice tone assessment to physiological signal monitoring, raise substantial concerns about accuracy, consent, and the potential for misuse. The inference pipelines that translate raw sensor data into emotional state classifications remain opaque to most stakeholders, creating barriers to accountability and trust. Additionally, the ethical implications of subjecting workers to continuous emotional surveillance have not been adequately addressed in existing regulatory frameworks, leaving both employers and employees navigating uncertain territory regarding privacy, autonomy, and psychological safety.

This survey paper makes three primary contributions to the understanding of affective computing in the context of remote workplace monitoring. First, it provides a comprehensive and structured overview of the sensing technologies currently deployed or proposed for capturing affective data in remote work environments. This includes an examination of vision-based approaches such as facial expression analysis and eye tracking, audio-based methods including speech emotion recognition and paralinguistic analysis, and physiological sensing techniques that leverage wearable devices to capture heart rate variability, electrodermal activity, and other biomarkers. For each sensing modality, the paper discusses the underlying principles, the types of emotional or affective states that can be inferred, and the practical limitations encountered in real-world deployment scenarios, particularly those exacerbated by the uncontrolled conditions of remote work settings.

Second, this survey critically examines the inference methodologies that transform raw sensor data into actionable insights about worker emotional states. The paper traces the evolution from rule-based and classical machine learning approaches to contemporary deep learning architectures, highlighting the trade-offs between model complexity, interpretability, and generalizability. Special attention is devoted to the challenges of ground truth acquisition in emotion recognition, the problem of intra- and inter-subject variability, and the risks of demographic and cultural biases embedded within training datasets and model outputs. The survey also addresses the emerging trend of multimodal fusion, wherein data from multiple sensing channels are combined to improve inference robustness, and discusses the associated technical and ethical complexities.

The aim of this survey is to provide researchers, practitioners, and policymakers with a systematic understanding of the current state of affective computing technologies as applied to remote workplace monitoring, while foregrounding the ethical considerations that must guide their responsible development and deployment. The remainder of this paper is structured as follows. The next section establishes foundational concepts in affective computing and traces its trajectory from academic research to commercial workplace applications. Subsequent sections are organized around the three pillars identified in the title: sensing technologies are reviewed in detail, followed by an analysis of inference pipelines, and then a dedicated discussion of ethical considerations encompassing privacy, consent, fairness, and psychological impact. The paper concludes by identifying open research challenges and proposing directions for future work that balance technological innovation with the protection of worker dignity and well-being.

# 7 Future Directions

The current landscape of workplace monitoring has shifted from tracking physical presence to interpreting the internal emotional states of employees through Emotional AI, yet significant gaps remain in understanding how these technologies are perceived and accepted across diverse workforces. Existing research has primarily focused on the technical capabilities of affective computing systems, leaving critical questions unanswered about the socio-demographic and cross-cultural dimensions of their deployment. The subjective nature of emotion introduces complexities that earlier biometric monitoring did not face, as emotional interpretation is deeply influenced by cultural norms, individual differences, and contextual factors. Without a comprehensive understanding of how different demographic groups and cultural contexts respond to emotional surveillance, organizations risk implementing systems that generate friction, mistrust, or unintended harm.

Future research should prioritize large-scale, cross-cultural empirical studies that examine workforce attitudes toward Emotional AI across different regions, industries, and demographic segments. Such investigations could reveal how factors like age, gender, cultural background, and professional role shape acceptance or resistance to affective monitoring. Additionally, researchers should explore the psychological and organizational consequences of prolonged exposure to emotional surveillance, including its effects on employee well-being, autonomy, and authentic emotional expression. Another promising direction involves developing and testing frameworks for the ethical design and deployment of these systems, incorporating participatory approaches that give workers a voice in how their emotional data is collected and used. Comparative studies examining alternative models of implementation, such as opt-in versus mandatory monitoring, could also provide valuable insights into preserving employee agency.

Addressing these gaps holds substantial potential to shape both organizational practice and technology design. A deeper understanding of cross-cultural and demographic differences can inform the development of more inclusive and context-sensitive Emotional AI systems, reducing the risk of alienating segments of the workforce. This research could also guide policymakers and industry bodies in establishing standards that protect worker rights while enabling beneficial applications of affective computing. Ultimately, by grounding the deployment of emotional monitoring technologies in empirical evidence about human attitudes and cultural variability, organizations can move toward practices that are not only technologically sophisticated but also socially responsible and aligned with the diverse values of the modern workforce.

# 8 Conclusion

This survey has examined the emerging landscape of affective computing in workplace monitoring, tracing the evolution from traditional biometric tracking focused on physical presence to sophisticated systems capable of sensing, reading, and evaluating worker emotional states. The central finding reveals that Emotional AI represents a fundamental paradigm shift in organizational surveillance, moving beyond objective behavioral metrics into the subjective and deeply personal domain of human affect. This transition introduces unprecedented capabilities for continuous emotional assessment, yet simultaneously raises profound concerns regarding privacy intrusion, psychological autonomy, and the validity of algorithmic interpretations of complex affective states. The analysis further highlights significant socio-demographic and cross-cultural challenges, as workforce attitudes toward emotional monitoring vary considerably across different populations, creating potential friction points that organizations must navigate carefully. The convergence of these factors underscores a critical tension between technological capability and social acceptability that remains largely unresolved in both academic discourse and practical implementation.

The significance of this survey lies in its systematic articulation of the boundaries where affective computing intersects with workplace governance, employee rights, and cultural norms. By mapping the trajectory from earlier biometric monitoring to contemporary Emotional AI applications, this work provides a structured framework for understanding how emotional surveillance technologies are reshaping the employer-employee relationship. The survey contributes to ongoing debates about digital ethics, algorithmic management, and the future of work by foregrounding the often-overlooked dimension of cross-cultural acceptance. As organizations increasingly adopt these technologies without comprehensive regulatory guidance, the synthesis presented here serves as a critical resource for scholars, policymakers, and practitioners seeking to balance innovation with the protection of worker dignity and psychological integrity. The identification of open problems regarding workforce attitudes and cultural friction establishes a foundation for future empirical investigations into the societal implications of emotional monitoring.

The trajectory of affective computing in the workplace demands urgent and sustained attention from multiple stakeholders. Organizations must move beyond purely technical assessments of Emotional AI and engage meaningfully with the ethical, cultural, and psychological dimensions of deploying such systems. Policymakers should prioritize the development of regulatory frameworks that establish clear boundaries around emotional surveillance, ensuring that worker consent, data sovereignty, and algorithmic transparency are not merely aspirational values but enforceable standards. Researchers are called upon to conduct cross-cultural empirical studies that illuminate how diverse populations perceive and respond to emotional monitoring, thereby informing context-sensitive deployment strategies. The path forward requires collaborative dialogue between technology developers, organizational leaders, labor representatives, and civil society to forge governance models that respect human emotional integrity while acknowledging legitimate organizational interests. Without such concerted effort, the promise of affective computing risks being overshadowed by its potential to erode the fundamental trust that underpins healthy workplace relationships.
