# 1 Abstract

Generative modeling of three-dimensional content has rapidly advanced through the development of deep generative architectures, including generative adversarial networks, variational autoencoders, and denoising diffusion models, each offering distinct strategies for representing and synthesizing 3D shapes. This survey systematically examines the landscape of 3D generative modeling, with a particular focus on the interplay between generative paradigms and the choice of shape representation, ranging from point clouds and neural fields to gradient-based surface models and multi-view depth maps. We review key methodologies, highlighting how diffusion-based approaches leverage iterative ancestral sampling to produce high-fidelity geometry from pure noise, while GAN-based systems exploit latent encodings, progressive deconvolutional structures, and spherical representations to generate diverse 3D content. Our analysis reveals that the primary challenges confronting the field involve simultaneously achieving scalability to fine-grained details and faithfulness to target distributions, alongside managing the substantial computational demands of iterative inference processes. Furthermore, we identify open problems in unifying disparate representation strategies into cohesive frameworks that can balance expressiveness, efficiency, and output quality. This survey provides a structured overview of current capabilities and persistent limitations, offering insights to guide future research toward more robust and versatile 3D generative systems.

# 2 Introduction

The generation of three-dimensional digital content has emerged as a cornerstone of modern computer vision and graphics, driven by the increasing demand for realistic virtual environments, digital twins, and immersive media experiences. Over the past decade, the field has witnessed a paradigm shift from manual modeling pipelines to data-driven generative approaches that can automatically synthesize novel shapes and scenes. Early generative frameworks, including generative adversarial networks, variational autoencoders, and normalizing flows, established foundational capabilities for learning complex data distributions and producing plausible 3D outputs. However, these methods often struggle to simultaneously capture fine-grained geometric details and the full diversity of real-world object categories, frequently exhibiting mode collapse or producing overly smooth approximations. The recent emergence of denoising diffusion models has introduced a powerful alternative, offering the ability to explicitly model the underlying data distribution through a gradual denoising process. This principled formulation enables more faithful generation that better reflects the richness and variability inherent in natural 3D content, positioning diffusion models as a transformative technology for shape and scene synthesis.

Despite these advances, the landscape of diffusion-based 3D generation presents several fundamental challenges that hinder widespread adoption and limit output quality. A primary tension exists between scalability—the capacity to generate objects with intricate structural details across diverse categories—and faithfulness, which demands high fidelity to the target distribution without sacrificing diversity. The choice of 3D representation further complicates this balance, as different modalities such as point clouds, implicit neural fields, voxel grids, and multi-view depth maps each impose distinct inductive biases and computational trade-offs. Prior work has explored a wide spectrum of strategies, including learning distributions over gradient fields that model shape surfaces, training generative models on latent encodings of neural fields, directly synthesizing point clouds, and employing progressive architectures within point-based frameworks. The inference process itself introduces practical bottlenecks, with standard diffusion pipelines requiring hundreds to thousands of iterative denoising steps to transform pure noise into coherent 3D structures, resulting in substantial computational overhead that limits real-time applicability and deployment on resource-constrained devices. Furthermore, the lack of standardized evaluation protocols makes it difficult to systematically compare methods across different representations and generation paradigms.

This survey makes two primary contributions to the field of diffusion-based 3D generation. First, we present a comprehensive and structured taxonomy of diffusion models for 3D shape and scene synthesis, systematically categorizing existing methods according to their underlying 3D representations and generation strategies. Our taxonomy spans the major representation paradigms, including point cloud-based approaches that operate directly on unordered point sets, implicit neural representations that model continuous signed distance or occupancy fields, explicit voxel and mesh-based techniques that leverage regular grid structures, and hybrid methods that combine multiple modalities to exploit complementary strengths. For each category, we analyze the architectural design choices, diffusion formulations, and training objectives that distinguish individual methods, providing readers with a clear understanding of the technical landscape and the relationships between seemingly disparate approaches. This organizational framework reveals common design patterns and highlights the trade-offs inherent in different representational choices, offering insights into why certain methods excel in specific scenarios while falling short in others.

Second, we conduct an in-depth examination of the evaluation methodologies employed across the literature, identifying inconsistencies and gaps in current benchmarking practices. We survey the metrics used to assess generation quality, including fidelity measures that quantify similarity to reference shapes, diversity metrics that capture the variability of generated outputs, and task-specific evaluations for downstream applications such as shape completion, single-view reconstruction, and scene-level synthesis. By critically analyzing these evaluation protocols, we uncover limitations in existing benchmarks and propose directions for more rigorous and standardized assessment frameworks. Additionally, we highlight open challenges that remain inadequately addressed, including the generation of complex multi-object scenes with realistic spatial relationships, the efficient sampling of high-resolution 3D content, and the integration of semantic control mechanisms that enable intuitive user-guided generation.

The aim of this survey is to provide researchers and practitioners with a structured overview of the rapidly evolving field of diffusion models for 3D content generation. The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. We begin by establishing the necessary background on diffusion models and 3D representations, ensuring readers have the foundational knowledge required to engage with the surveyed literature. We then present our taxonomy of methods, dedicating individual sections to each major representation category and examining representative works in detail. Following this, we discuss the evaluation landscape, comparing metrics and benchmarking practices across studies. Finally, we identify open challenges and outline promising directions for future research, concluding with a summary of key insights and the broader implications for the field.

# 6 Challenges and Open Problems

Despite the rapid progress in 3D generative modeling, several challenges remain. Li et al. (2026) note that a powerful 3D generative model must balance scalability—the capacity to produce objects with fine-grained details—and faithfulness, meaning the generated shapes should exhibit high fidelity to the target distribution. Meeting both demands simultaneously is nontrivial, particularly as models scale to more complex and diverse object categories. The inference process itself introduces practical hurdles: the dual diffusion framework described by Li et al. (2026) requires iterative ancestral sampling over \(T = 1000\) steps to transform pure Gaussian noise into multi-view depth maps, a computationally intensive procedure that may limit real-time or resource-constrained applications.

Open problems also persist in representation and generation paradigms. Prior approaches have explored diverse strategies, including learning distributions over gradient fields that model shape surfaces (ShapeGF), training GANs on latent encodings of neural fields (IM-GAN, l-GAN), directly generating point clouds (r-GAN), and employing progressive deconvolutional networks within point-cloud GANs (PDGN), as surveyed by Li et al. (2026). However, unifying the strengths of these disparate representations—implicit fields, point clouds, and gradient-based surfaces—into a single framework that jointly optimizes scalability, faithfulness, and computational efficiency remains an open direction for future work.

# 7 Future Directions

The current landscape of 3D generative modeling reveals several persistent limitations that hinder broader adoption and capability. A central tension exists between scalability and faithfulness: models that capture fine-grained geometric details often struggle to generalize across diverse object categories, while more scalable approaches sacrifice fidelity to the target distribution. The inference process itself introduces substantial computational bottlenecks, with diffusion-based pipelines requiring hundreds or thousands of iterative denoising steps to transform pure noise into coherent 3D content, making real-time or resource-constrained deployment impractical. Furthermore, the field remains fragmented across representation paradigms. Implicit neural fields, point clouds, gradient-based surface models, and multi-view depth maps each offer distinct advantages, yet no unified framework successfully integrates their complementary strengths. This fragmentation forces practitioners to accept trade-offs between structural completeness, surface smoothness, topological flexibility, and computational tractability, depending on which representation they adopt.

Future research should pursue several promising directions to address these gaps. One critical avenue involves developing hybrid generative frameworks that dynamically combine multiple 3D representations within a single diffusion or adversarial pipeline. For instance, a coarse-to-fine architecture could leverage multi-view depth diffusion for global structure initialization, then refine surfaces through gradient-field optimization, and finally inject high-frequency details via point-cloud or neural field representations. Such a framework would require novel training objectives that jointly optimize across heterogeneous output modalities while maintaining consistency between them. A second direction targets inference efficiency through progressive distillation and adaptive sampling strategies. Rather than applying a fixed number of denoising steps uniformly, future models could learn to allocate computational budgets based on local geometric complexity, concentrating refinement iterations on regions requiring fine detail while rapidly converging in smooth or planar areas. Additionally, exploring latent-space diffusion formulations that operate in compressed representation spaces—learned jointly across multiple 3D modalities—could dramatically reduce the dimensionality of the generation problem while preserving expressiveness.

The potential impact of these advances extends well beyond academic benchmarks. Efficient and faithful 3D generative models would transform content creation pipelines across gaming, film production, virtual reality, and architectural visualization by enabling artists to rapidly prototype and iterate on complex assets without manual modeling. In robotics and embodied AI, unified frameworks capable of generating diverse, physically plausible 3D shapes could accelerate sim-to-real transfer by populating training environments with realistic object variations. Medical imaging and computational biology stand to benefit from generative models that synthesize anatomically accurate 3D structures for surgical planning or drug discovery. By reconciling the competing demands of scalability, fidelity, and efficiency while bridging fragmented representation paradigms, the next generation of 3D generative models would unlock creative and scientific workflows that remain impractical today.

# 8 Conclusion

This survey has examined the landscape of generative modeling for 3D content, tracing the evolution from foundational architectures—generative adversarial networks, variational autoencoders, and normalizing flows—to the more recent prominence of denoising diffusion models. The analysis reveals that diffusion-based approaches, exemplified by multi-view depth diffusion, offer distinct advantages in explicitly modeling the data distribution and producing diverse, high-fidelity outputs through iterative ancestral sampling. Concurrently, the survey highlights the critical role of 3D representation, documenting a rich spectrum of strategies that includes gradient-field-based surface modeling, neural field encodings, direct point cloud generation, progressive deconvolutional architectures, and spherical representations. Each paradigm presents a unique trade-off between structural fidelity, computational tractability, and compatibility with downstream tasks, underscoring the absence of a universally optimal solution.

The significance of this survey lies in its structured synthesis of a rapidly advancing and increasingly fragmented field. By juxtaposing diffusion-based methods with established GAN-based paradigms and systematically categorizing representation choices, the survey provides a coherent conceptual map for researchers navigating the design space of 3D generative models. It clarifies the fundamental tension between scalability—the ability to capture fine-grained geometric details across diverse object categories—and faithfulness to the target distribution, a dual requirement that remains only partially resolved. Furthermore, the discussion of inference-time computational burdens, particularly the thousand-step denoising process characteristic of diffusion pipelines, foregrounds practical constraints that are often underemphasized in methodological contributions. This integrative perspective enables the identification of cross-cutting challenges that transcend individual architectures.

Moving forward, the field must confront the open problem of unifying disparate representational paradigms into cohesive frameworks that jointly optimize expressiveness, efficiency, and fidelity. The computational intensity of iterative denoising demands innovations in accelerated sampling, model distillation, or hybrid architectures that retain the benefits of diffusion while mitigating inference costs. Equally pressing is the need for rigorous benchmarking protocols that evaluate generative models not only on geometric accuracy but also on diversity, controllability, and real-world applicability in domains such as virtual reality, robotics, and computer-aided design. Progress will depend on sustained interdisciplinary collaboration, the release of large-scale, high-quality 3D datasets, and a collective commitment to addressing the scalability-faithfulness trade-off as a central research objective rather than an incidental limitation.
