# 1 Abstract

The generation of high-quality 3D shapes is a fundamental challenge in computer vision and graphics, driven by the need for scalable and faithful content creation. Denoising diffusion models have recently gained prominence over generative adversarial networks and other paradigms due to their ability to explicitly model complex data distributions. This survey provides a structured overview of diffusion-based 3D generation, with a focus on the evolution of shape representations and the architectural innovations that enable multi-view synthesis. We examine how multi-view depth diffusion models, which operate by iteratively denoising Gaussian noise through ancestral sampling, reduce the dimensionality of the generation space and avoid unstructured outputs, thereby producing high-fidelity shapes. Our analysis further highlights the trade-offs between detail resolution, output fidelity, and computational cost that characterize current methods, and we identify the lengthy sampling chains required for inference as a key bottleneck for real-time applications. By synthesizing recent advances and persistent limitations, this survey offers a comprehensive perspective on the state of diffusion-based 3D generative modeling and outlines promising directions for efficient, high-quality shape synthesis.

# 2 Introduction

The generation of three-dimensional digital content has become a cornerstone of modern computer vision and graphics, with applications spanning virtual reality, robotics, autonomous driving, and creative design. Over the past decade, the research community has devoted substantial effort to developing generative models capable of synthesizing high-quality 3D shapes and scenes. Early approaches relied on a diverse array of generative paradigms, including generative adversarial networks, variational autoencoders, and normalizing flows, each offering distinct trade-offs between sample quality, diversity, and training stability. More recently, denoising diffusion models have emerged as a particularly promising framework for 3D generation. Unlike their predecessors, diffusion models explicitly learn to approximate the underlying data distribution through a gradual denoising process, which endows them with superior mode coverage and the ability to produce outputs that faithfully reflect the diversity of real-world content. This property has made diffusion models especially attractive for the complex and high-dimensional domain of 3D shape and scene synthesis.

Despite these advances, the field of diffusion-based 3D generation faces a set of persistent and interrelated challenges. A fundamental difficulty lies in the choice of 3D representation, which directly governs the quality, structure, and downstream usability of generated content. Prior work has explored a wide spectrum of representations, including point clouds, implicit neural fields, gradient fields that model shape surfaces, spherical projections, and multi-view depth maps, each with its own strengths and limitations. Beyond representation, the generative models themselves must satisfy multiple demanding criteria simultaneously: they must scale to produce objects with fine-grained geometric detail, maintain high fidelity so that generated shapes are both realistic and structurally coherent, and operate within acceptable computational budgets. Current methods often involve inherent trade-offs among these desiderata. Moreover, the standard ancestral sampling procedure employed by diffusion models, which can require hundreds or even thousands of iterative denoising steps, imposes a substantial runtime burden that limits their applicability in real-time or interactive settings. These intertwined issues of representation design, output quality, scalability, and inference efficiency remain central open problems that hinder the broader adoption of diffusion models for 3D content creation.

This survey makes two primary contributions to the study of diffusion models for 3D shape and scene generation. First, it provides a comprehensive and structured taxonomy of the representations employed in diffusion-based 3D generation. We systematically categorize existing methods according to their underlying geometric representations—ranging from explicit formats such as point clouds, meshes, and depth maps to implicit representations including neural radiance fields, signed distance functions, and occupancy networks, as well as hybrid approaches that combine multiple modalities. For each category, we analyze the representational assumptions, the inductive biases they introduce, and their impact on generation quality and downstream applicability. This taxonomy not only organizes a rapidly growing and fragmented literature but also reveals the design principles and trade-offs that govern the choice of representation in diffusion-based pipelines.

The second contribution is a thorough examination of evaluation methodologies and the identification of open challenges and future research directions. We survey the metrics and benchmarks commonly used to assess 3D generative models, discussing their suitability for capturing geometric fidelity, structural plausibility, perceptual quality, and diversity. We highlight the limitations of current evaluation practices, including the lack of standardized protocols and the difficulty of measuring semantic coherence in generated scenes. Building on this analysis, we delineate key open problems, including the need for more efficient sampling strategies, the integration of multi-modal conditioning signals, the generation of dynamic and editable content, and the development of representations that jointly model geometry and appearance at scale. By synthesizing insights from both methodological and evaluative perspectives, we aim to provide a clear roadmap for future research.

The aim of this survey is to offer researchers and practitioners a structured overview of the state of the art in diffusion-based 3D generation, with a particular emphasis on the interplay between representation design, generative modeling, and evaluation. The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. We begin by introducing the foundational concepts of denoising diffusion models and the principal 3D representations used in the literature. We then present our taxonomy of diffusion-based 3D generation methods, grouping them by representation type and discussing their architectures, training strategies, and inference procedures. Subsequently, we review evaluation protocols and metrics, critically assessing their effectiveness and limitations. Finally, we discuss open challenges and promising directions for future work, concluding with a summary of the key insights and trends shaping this rapidly evolving field.

# 3 Background and Foundations


The generation of 3D shapes has been approached through a variety of generative modeling paradigms. Denoising diffusion models have emerged as a prominent framework because, unlike generative adversarial networks (GANs), variational autoencoders (VAEs), and normalizing flows, they explicitly model the data distribution, enabling them to faithfully generate outputs that reflect content diversity (Li et al., 2026). The choice of 3D representation is equally critical for shape generation quality. Prior work has explored diverse strategies, including learning distributions over gradient fields that model shape surfaces, as in ShapeGF, and training GANs over latent variables that encode shapes, exemplified by IM-GAN which models shapes as neural fields, and l-GAN, while r-GAN generates point clouds directly (Li et al., 2026). Other point-cloud-based approaches include PDGN, which introduces progressive deconvolutional networks within a point cloud GAN, and SP-GAN, which employs a spherical representation (Li et al., 2026).

In the specific context of diffusion-based 3D generation, Li et al. (2026) describe an inference strategy for multi-view depth map generation using a Multi-View Depth Diffusion (MVDD) model. The process is initialized from 2D maps of pure Gaussian noise and proceeds via ancestral sampling over \( T = 1000 \) steps, following a standard denoising diffusion probabilistic model update rule where noise is iteratively removed according to a predefined schedule, with the added noise term drawn from an isotropic multivariate normal distribution (Li et al., 2026).

# 4 Methods and Techniques


Li et al. (2026) situate their multi-view depth diffusion (MVDD) approach within a broader landscape of 3D generative techniques. Prior generative adversarial network (GAN)-based methods have explored diverse shape representations: ShapeGF learns distributions over gradient fields that model shape surfaces, IM-GAN models shapes as neural fields and trains GANs over latent variables that encode the shapes, while r-GAN generates point clouds directly (Li et al., 2026). Other point-cloud GANs include PDGN, which introduces progressive deconvolutional networks, and SP-GAN, which employs a spherical representation (Li et al., 2026).

In contrast to these GAN-based paradigms, Li et al. (2026) adopt a denoising diffusion framework for 3D shape generation. Their MVDD inference strategy is initialized with 2D maps of pure Gaussian noise and generates multi-view depth maps through an ancestral sampling procedure. The reverse diffusion step is defined as \(x_{t-1} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_t}} \left( x_t - \frac{1-\alpha_t}{\sqrt{1-\tilde{\alpha}_t}} \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t) \right) + \sqrt{\beta_t}\epsilon\), where \(\epsilon\) follows an isotropic multivariate normal distribution, and this process is iterated for \(T = 1000\) steps (Li et al., 2026).

# 5 Applications and Systems


The generation of 3D shapes and avatars has been advanced through diffusion-based systems that operate on structured representations. Li et al. (2026) introduced MVDD, a multi-view depth diffusion model that represents the first use of a multi-view depth representation within a generative diffusion framework. Their approach initializes generation from 2D maps of pure Gaussian noise and produces multi-view depth maps through an ancestral sampling process over 1000 steps, following a standard denoising schedule where each step applies the update rule \(x_{t-1} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_t}} \left( x_t - \frac{1-\alpha_t}{\sqrt{1-\tilde{\alpha}_t}} \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t) \right) + \sqrt{\beta_t}\epsilon\), with \(\epsilon\) drawn from an isotropic multivariate normal distribution. This multi-view depth representation reduces the dimensionality of the generation space and avoids unstructured outputs, enabling the system to produce high-quality 3D shapes, perform depth completion, and distill 3D prior knowledge for downstream tasks (Li et al., 2026).

Prior generative approaches to 3D shape modeling have explored a variety of representations and architectures. ShapeGF learns distributions over gradient fields that model shape surfaces, while IM-GAN and l-GAN train GANs over latent variables encoding shapes as neural fields. In contrast, r-GAN generates point clouds directly, and PDGN employs progressive deconvolutional networks within a point cloud GAN framework. SP-GAN further explores spherical representations for shape generation (Li et al., 2026). These systems illustrate the diversity of generative strategies—ranging from implicit neural fields to explicit point cloud and depth-based representations—that have been applied to 3D content creation.

# 6 Challenges and Open Problems

Despite the rapid progress in 3D generative modeling, several challenges and open problems remain. Li et al. (2026) note that a powerful 3D generative model must satisfy multiple demanding attributes, including scalability to produce objects with fine-grained details and faithfulness so that generated shapes exhibit high fidelity. Meeting these criteria simultaneously remains difficult, and current methods often involve trade-offs between detail resolution, output fidelity, and computational cost.

A further practical challenge lies in the computational demands of the generation process itself. For instance, Li et al. (2026) describe an inference strategy for multi-view depth map generation that requires iterating an ancestral sampling procedure for \(T = 1000\) steps, starting from pure Gaussian noise. Such lengthy sampling chains, while effective, impose a substantial runtime burden that limits real-time or interactive applications and motivates research into more efficient inference schemes. Addressing these intertwined issues of quality, scalability, and efficiency remains a central open problem for the field.

# 7 Future Directions

The current landscape of 3D generative modeling, particularly within diffusion-based frameworks, reveals several persistent limitations that constrain both practical deployment and further scientific progress. A central challenge is the inherent tension between generation quality and computational efficiency. State-of-the-art denoising diffusion models rely on lengthy ancestral sampling chains, often requiring a thousand or more iterative steps to transform pure Gaussian noise into high-fidelity outputs. This imposes a substantial runtime burden that renders real-time or interactive applications infeasible, limiting the utility of these models in domains such as virtual reality, gaming, and rapid prototyping. Beyond efficiency, existing methods struggle to simultaneously satisfy the demanding requirements of scalability to fine-grained details and unwavering output fidelity. Multi-view depth representations have shown promise in reducing dimensionality and avoiding unstructured outputs, yet the trade-offs between detail resolution, global shape coherence, and computational cost remain poorly resolved. Furthermore, current generative paradigms are typically evaluated on narrow benchmarks and lack systematic investigation into their robustness across diverse object categories, structural complexities, and real-world deployment conditions. These gaps collectively highlight the need for a new generation of 3D generative models that are not only powerful in their representational capacity but also practical in their computational footprint and reliable in their generalization.

Future research should prioritize the development of accelerated inference schemes that preserve the distributional fidelity characteristic of diffusion models while drastically reducing the number of sampling steps. Promising directions include the design of learned distillation techniques that compress the reverse diffusion trajectory into fewer steps, the exploration of adaptive noise schedules that concentrate computational effort on the most critical phases of generation, and the integration of consistency models that enable single-step or few-step sampling without sacrificing output quality. In parallel, hybrid architectures that combine the structured representational advantages of multi-view depth maps with the expressiveness of implicit neural fields could offer a pathway to reconciling detail resolution with global coherence. Such systems might employ a coarse-to-fine strategy where a lightweight diffusion model generates a base multi-view depth structure, and a subsequent refinement network operating on continuous implicit representations adds high-frequency geometric and textural details. Another critical avenue involves expanding the evaluation framework beyond synthetic benchmarks to include stress tests under distribution shift, adversarial perturbations, and diverse real-world scanning conditions. This would require the curation of large-scale, multi-modal datasets that pair depth maps with corresponding meshes, point clouds, and textured renderings across a wide spectrum of object categories. Additionally, incorporating physical plausibility constraints and semantic controllability into the generation process could unlock new applications in engineering design, where generated shapes must not only appear realistic but also satisfy functional requirements such as structural integrity or aerodynamic properties.

The successful pursuit of these research directions would carry transformative implications for both the scientific understanding of 3D generative modeling and its real-world impact. Efficient few-step sampling would democratize access to high-quality 3D content creation, enabling interactive design tools that allow artists, engineers, and educators to generate and manipulate complex shapes in real time. Hybrid representations that bridge structured depth maps and implicit fields could establish a new state of the art in generation fidelity, producing assets indistinguishable from human-crafted or scanned counterparts. Robust generalization capabilities would pave the way for deploying generative models in safety-critical applications such as surgical planning, autonomous navigation simulation, and architectural modeling, where reliability under varied conditions is non-negotiable. By embedding physical and semantic constraints directly into the generative process, future systems could move beyond mere appearance mimicry toward functional content creation, accelerating iterative design cycles in manufacturing and reducing the reliance on costly physical prototyping. Collectively, these advances would shift 3D generative modeling from a primarily academic pursuit into a practical, pervasive technology embedded across creative, industrial, and scientific workflows.

# 8 Conclusion

This survey has examined the landscape of 3D shape generation, with a particular focus on the emergence of denoising diffusion models as a powerful alternative to earlier generative paradigms. The analysis reveals that while generative adversarial networks and variational autoencoders have historically driven progress through diverse representations—including gradient fields, neural fields, point clouds, and spherical coordinates—diffusion-based frameworks offer distinct advantages by explicitly modeling the underlying data distribution. The Multi-View Depth Diffusion model exemplifies this shift, demonstrating that structured intermediate representations such as multi-view depth maps can effectively constrain the generation space, reduce dimensionality, and avoid unstructured outputs. Through an ancestral sampling procedure spanning a thousand denoising steps, this approach achieves high-quality shape generation, depth completion, and the distillation of 3D prior knowledge for downstream applications. Nevertheless, the review also highlights persistent tensions between the competing demands of fine-grained detail, output fidelity, and computational feasibility that characterize the current state of the field.

The significance of this survey lies in its structured synthesis of the conceptual and technical evolution of 3D generative modeling. By tracing the trajectory from implicit neural representations and point-cloud-based generative adversarial networks to contemporary diffusion-based systems, the survey provides a coherent framework for understanding how representational choices and modeling paradigms jointly determine generation quality and applicability. The detailed examination of the Multi-View Depth Diffusion inference process, including its mathematical formulation and iterative denoising schedule, offers concrete insight into both the capabilities and the computational bottlenecks of current methods. This integrated perspective is valuable for researchers seeking to navigate the rapidly expanding literature, identify the fundamental trade-offs that shape methodological decisions, and recognize the open problems that define the frontier of 3D generative research.

Moving forward, the field must confront the intertwined challenges of scalability, fidelity, and efficiency that currently limit the practical deployment of 3D generative models. The computational burden imposed by lengthy diffusion sampling chains, while effective for producing high-quality outputs, remains a significant barrier to real-time and interactive applications. Future research should prioritize the development of accelerated inference schemes, more compact yet expressive 3D representations, and training objectives that explicitly balance detail resolution against computational cost. Progress on these fronts will not only advance the technical capabilities of generative systems but also broaden their accessibility across domains such as computer graphics, virtual reality, and robotic perception. The continued refinement of diffusion-based approaches, informed by the lessons of prior paradigms and driven by the demands of real-world applications, holds the promise of transforming how 3D content is created, shared, and utilized.
