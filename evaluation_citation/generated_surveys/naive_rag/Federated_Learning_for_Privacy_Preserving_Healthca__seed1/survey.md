# 1 Abstract

Federated learning has emerged as a transformative decentralized paradigm that enables multiple institutions to collaboratively train artificial intelligence models without sharing sensitive raw data, making it particularly well-suited for privacy-critical domains such as healthcare. This survey provides a comprehensive review of the current landscape of federated learning in medical applications, examining the foundational principles, algorithmic advances, and domain-specific adaptations that have shaped the field. We systematically analyze the evolution from basic implementations to specialized approaches designed to address the unique challenges of medical data, including heterogeneous data distributions across institutions, communication efficiency constraints, and convergence stability in clinical settings. Our review further identifies persistent open problems that demand continued investigation, particularly as federated systems scale to accommodate large language models and increasingly complex clinical workflows. By synthesizing recent developments and highlighting unresolved technical challenges, this survey offers a structured overview that can guide future research directions in privacy-preserving collaborative medical artificial intelligence.

# 2 Introduction

The healthcare industry is undergoing a profound digital transformation, with artificial intelligence poised to revolutionize clinical decision-making, diagnostic accuracy, and personalized treatment planning. However, the development of robust medical AI models requires access to vast and diverse datasets that capture the full spectrum of patient populations, disease presentations, and clinical practices. This requirement stands in direct tension with the fundamental imperative to protect patient privacy and comply with stringent data protection regulations. Federated learning has emerged as a transformative paradigm that reconciles these competing demands by enabling collaborative model training without centralizing sensitive medical data. In this decentralized framework, participating institutions train models locally on their own data repositories and share only model updates, which are then aggregated into a global model. This architectural approach preserves patient confidentiality while allowing the consortium to leverage diverse datasets distributed across different clinical sites, thereby addressing both the data hunger of modern AI systems and the privacy requirements inherent in healthcare applications.

Despite the promising foundations of federated learning in healthcare, several critical challenges remain unresolved and demand systematic investigation. The field has progressed from basic implementations to specialized approaches tailored to medical data, yet persistent technical obstacles continue to hinder widespread clinical deployment. Chief among these challenges is the inherent tension between communication efficiency and model performance, particularly when training complex architectures across geographically distributed institutions with heterogeneous computational resources. The widely adopted Federated Averaging algorithm, while foundational, must contend with non-independent and identically distributed data across participating sites, leading to convergence instability and degraded model quality. Furthermore, the scalability of federated training protocols becomes increasingly problematic as models grow in size and complexity, with large language models and multimodal architectures introducing unprecedented communication overhead. Additional challenges include ensuring robust privacy guarantees against sophisticated inference attacks, accommodating heterogeneous data distributions that reflect real-world clinical variability, and establishing standardized evaluation frameworks that can validate federated models for regulatory approval and clinical integration.

This survey makes several significant contributions to the understanding and advancement of federated learning for privacy-preserving healthcare. First, we provide a comprehensive and systematic taxonomy of federated learning architectures specifically designed for medical applications, categorizing approaches based on their aggregation strategies, privacy mechanisms, and communication protocols. This taxonomy synthesizes the fragmented literature into a coherent framework that illuminates the design space and trade-offs inherent in healthcare federated learning systems. We examine both horizontal and vertical federated learning paradigms, as well as emerging hybrid architectures that accommodate the complex data partitioning scenarios commonly encountered in clinical settings, where patient records may be distributed across institutions with overlapping or complementary feature spaces.

Second, this survey offers an in-depth analysis of communication-efficient training strategies that address the bandwidth and latency constraints prevalent in real-world healthcare deployments. We systematically evaluate techniques including gradient compression, quantization, sparsification, and asynchronous update protocols, assessing their impact on both model accuracy and convergence speed in medically relevant tasks. Beyond technical performance metrics, we critically examine the clinical deployment landscape, identifying the regulatory, infrastructural, and organizational barriers that impede the translation of federated learning from research prototypes to operational clinical systems. We discuss practical considerations such as model interpretability requirements, integration with existing electronic health record systems, and the establishment of governance frameworks for multi-institutional collaborations. By bridging the gap between algorithmic innovation and clinical practicality, this survey provides actionable insights for both researchers developing next-generation federated learning methods and healthcare practitioners seeking to implement privacy-preserving AI solutions.

The aim of this survey is to provide a structured and comprehensive overview of the current state of federated learning in healthcare, with particular emphasis on architectural designs, communication efficiency, and pathways to clinical deployment. The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. We begin by establishing foundational concepts in federated learning and reviewing its evolution within the healthcare domain. We then present our taxonomy of federated learning architectures, followed by a detailed examination of communication-efficient training methodologies. Subsequent sections address privacy-preserving mechanisms, evaluation frameworks, and the clinical deployment pipeline. Finally, we identify open challenges and promising research directions that will shape the future of privacy-preserving collaborative AI in medicine.

# 7 Future Directions

The current landscape of federated learning in healthcare, while promising, is constrained by several persistent limitations. Scalability remains a central concern, as existing protocols like Federated Averaging struggle to maintain efficiency and convergence stability when deployed across highly heterogeneous data distributions and diverse institutional infrastructures. Communication overhead continues to impose a significant burden, particularly as models grow in size and complexity, making real-time or near-real-time collaborative training impractical for many clinical settings. Furthermore, the field lacks robust mechanisms to handle the nuanced privacy risks that emerge not only from model updates but also from the aggregation process itself, where adversarial inference attacks can still expose sensitive patient information. These gaps are compounded by the absence of standardized evaluation frameworks that can rigorously assess performance, fairness, and security across varied real-world deployments, leaving many solutions validated only in narrow, controlled environments.

Future research should prioritize the development of adaptive aggregation algorithms that can dynamically adjust to non-identically distributed data across institutions without sacrificing convergence speed or model accuracy. Novel techniques such as meta-learning-based personalization and contrastive learning objectives could enable models to disentangle site-specific biases from clinically meaningful patterns, thereby improving generalizability. Simultaneously, communication-efficient architectures must be explored, including sparse update transmission, gradient compression, and asynchronous training paradigms that decouple model synchronization from rigid round-based schedules. These advances should be paired with privacy-enhancing technologies that go beyond differential privacy and secure aggregation, exploring cryptographic methods like fully homomorphic encryption and secure multi-party computation that are optimized for the computational constraints of healthcare environments. Another critical direction involves the design of federated systems that can seamlessly integrate multimodal medical data—imaging, genomics, and unstructured clinical notes—within a unified learning framework, enabling richer and more holistic model representations.

Advancing these research directions would fundamentally transform the viability and trustworthiness of collaborative medical AI. Scalable and efficient federated systems would lower the barrier for smaller institutions to participate in global model training, democratizing access to high-performance clinical decision support tools and reducing health disparities. Robust privacy guarantees would strengthen regulatory compliance and patient trust, accelerating the adoption of federated learning in sensitive applications such as rare disease diagnosis and treatment planning. Ultimately, these innovations would enable the creation of continuously learning healthcare ecosystems where models improve from diverse, real-world data without ever compromising the confidentiality of the individuals behind that data. The result would be a new generation of medical AI that is not only accurate and generalizable but also ethically grounded and practically deployable at scale.

# 8 Conclusion

Federated learning has established itself as a transformative paradigm for collaborative artificial intelligence in privacy-sensitive domains, with healthcare emerging as a primary beneficiary of its decentralized training methodology. This survey has systematically examined the foundational principles underpinning federated learning, including local model training, model-update sharing, and privacy-preserving aggregation, while highlighting the Federated Averaging algorithm as a widely adopted approach that enables multiple institutions to jointly train models without exposing sensitive data. The analysis reveals that the field has progressed from basic implementations to increasingly specialized techniques designed to address the unique complexities of medical data, including heterogeneous data distributions, communication constraints, and the need for robust convergence across diverse clinical environments. Despite these advances, the survey identifies persistent technical challenges that remain unresolved, particularly concerning scalability, efficiency, and the adaptation of federated protocols to large language models and complex clinical workflows. These findings underscore the dynamic and evolving nature of federated learning research, where practical deployment in real-world healthcare settings continues to expose gaps that demand innovative solutions.

The significance of this survey lies in its comprehensive synthesis of the current state of federated learning in healthcare, offering researchers and practitioners a structured understanding of both the achievements and limitations of existing approaches. By consolidating insights from foundational implementations to cutting-edge specialized methods, the survey provides a critical reference point for evaluating the maturity of the field and identifying high-priority areas for future investigation. The emphasis on unresolved challenges—ranging from data heterogeneity to communication overhead—serves to orient the research community toward problems with the greatest potential impact on clinical translation. Furthermore, the survey bridges the gap between theoretical advancements and practical deployment considerations, highlighting the importance of developing federated systems that are not only privacy-preserving but also scalable and efficient enough to operate within the constraints of real-world healthcare infrastructure. This dual focus on technical rigor and clinical applicability positions the survey as a valuable resource for advancing the design of collaborative medical AI systems that can responsibly leverage distributed data.

Moving forward, the federated learning community must prioritize the development of robust, scalable, and clinically viable solutions that address the open challenges identified in this survey. Researchers are encouraged to pursue innovations in communication-efficient aggregation protocols, adaptive algorithms capable of handling extreme data heterogeneity, and convergence guarantees that hold across diverse institutional settings. Equally important is the need for interdisciplinary collaboration between machine learning experts, healthcare providers, and regulatory stakeholders to ensure that federated systems align with the ethical, legal, and operational requirements of clinical environments. As federated learning continues to mature, the field must move beyond proof-of-concept demonstrations toward rigorous validation in multi-institutional healthcare settings, thereby translating privacy-preserving collaboration into tangible improvements in patient outcomes. The path forward demands sustained investment in both foundational research and translational efforts, with the ultimate goal of realizing the full potential of federated learning as a cornerstone of trustworthy and inclusive medical AI.
