# 1 Abstract

Lithium-ion batteries have become indispensable across electric vehicles, stationary energy storage, and portable electronics, yet their progressive capacity fade and internal resistance growth pose significant risks to system reliability, operational safety, and lifecycle cost. Accurately forecasting battery health and remaining useful life is therefore essential to prevent unanticipated performance declines and to enable proactive maintenance. This survey provides a structured examination of data-driven and hybrid prognostic approaches for lithium-ion battery state-of-health estimation, with a focus on methods that integrate machine learning techniques with physical degradation insights. We review the application of Gaussian process regression for capturing nonlinear capacity fade trajectories, highlighting both its flexibility in uncertainty quantification and its limitations in fully characterizing complex aging mechanisms. In parallel, we analyze hybrid frameworks that combine model-based incremental capacity analysis with support vector regression, illustrating the growing trend toward fusing electrochemical domain knowledge with data-driven regressors to improve estimation fidelity and robustness. Our synthesis identifies persistent challenges, including the inherent variability of degradation pathways, limited generalization across operating conditions, and the need for reliable long-term predictions under real-world usage profiles. By consolidating recent advances and delineating open research gaps, this survey offers a comprehensive reference for developing next-generation battery health forecasting systems that are both accurate and practically deployable.

# 2 Introduction

Lithium‑ion batteries have become a cornerstone technology across a broad spectrum of applications, including electric vehicles, stationary grid storage, and portable electronics. Their widespread adoption stems from their high energy density, long cycle life, and declining manufacturing costs. However, as these batteries age, they undergo gradual but irreversible degradation, primarily manifested as capacity fade and internal resistance increase. This aging process directly compromises system reliability, operational safety, and economic viability. Uncontrolled degradation can lead to unanticipated performance declines or even catastrophic failures, which are especially critical in transport and grid energy storage contexts where continuity of operation is paramount. Consequently, the ability to accurately monitor and forecast battery health has emerged as a central research and engineering priority, driving the development of advanced prognostic methods that can be embedded within battery management systems to enable proactive maintenance and safer operation.

Despite significant progress, the accurate estimation of battery state of health and the prediction of remaining useful life remain formidable challenges. The fundamental difficulty lies in the complex, nonlinear, and time‑varying nature of battery degradation, which is influenced by a multitude of interacting factors such as temperature, charge–discharge rates, depth of discharge, and manufacturing variability. Without reliable knowledge of a battery’s rate of capacity loss or its remaining useful life, systems become susceptible to sudden performance drops and unplanned downtime. Prior research has explored a range of techniques to address this uncertainty, from model‑based approaches that incorporate physical insights to purely data‑driven strategies. However, many existing methods still struggle to fully capture the degradation dynamics under real‑world operating conditions. For instance, while certain probabilistic models can characterize nonlinear degradation trends, they often fall short in generalizing across diverse usage profiles and battery chemistries. Similarly, conventional machine learning regressors may lack the physical interpretability needed to ensure robust predictions when faced with limited or noisy data. These limitations underscore a critical open problem: developing forecasting frameworks that are both accurate and reliable across the wide range of scenarios encountered in practice.

This survey makes two primary contributions to the field of lithium‑ion battery prognostics. First, it provides a comprehensive and structured review of machine learning techniques applied to state‑of‑health estimation and remaining‑useful‑life prediction, systematically categorizing the literature according to methodological families. By examining approaches ranging from Gaussian process regression and support vector machines to deep learning architectures, the survey clarifies the strengths and weaknesses of each paradigm in handling the specific challenges posed by battery degradation data. Special attention is given to hybrid methods that integrate physical models with data‑driven regressors, as these represent a promising direction for combining interpretability with predictive power. The review also traces the evolution of key ideas, highlighting how early work on resistance‑based capacity estimation has paved the way for more sophisticated multi‑feature and multi‑stage forecasting strategies.

Second, the survey identifies persistent gaps and emerging trends that shape the future research agenda. It critically assesses the limitations of current methods, including their sensitivity to training data quality, computational complexity, and difficulty in transferring models across different battery types and operating conditions. Furthermore, the paper discusses the growing role of on‑board implementation, real‑time learning, and uncertainty quantification, which are essential for deploying prognostic algorithms in safety‑critical applications. By synthesizing insights from a broad body of work, this survey not only serves as a reference for researchers entering the domain but also offers a roadmap for advancing the state of the art toward more robust, interpretable, and practically deployable battery health forecasting systems.

The aim of this survey is to equip readers with a clear understanding of the current landscape of machine learning for battery state‑of‑health estimation and remaining‑useful‑life prediction, while also outlining directions for future innovation. The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. The next section introduces the fundamentals of lithium‑ion battery degradation and defines the key prognostic targets. Subsequent sections then present a detailed taxonomy of machine learning methods, progressing from classical regression techniques to advanced deep learning and hybrid approaches. Following this, the paper examines cross‑cutting issues such as feature engineering, uncertainty management, and real‑world deployment challenges. The final section synthesizes the findings, highlights open problems, and proposes concrete pathways for future research.

# 3 Background and Foundations


Lithium-ion batteries are a key technology for electric vehicles, stationary storage, and portable electronics, and their aging directly impacts system reliability, safety, and cost (Zhang et al., 2022). Capacity fade and resistance increase reduce usable energy and power, while uncontrolled degradation can lead to unplanned failures (Zhang et al., 2022). Richardson et al. (2017) similarly emphasize that accurately predicting the future capacity and remaining useful life of batteries is necessary because not knowing a battery’s rate of capacity loss or useful life renders the system susceptible to an unanticipated decline in performance. These concerns motivate the development of data-driven and model-based prognostic methods that can forecast battery health over time.

A variety of techniques have been explored to address this challenge. Richardson et al. (2017) note that prior work by Goebel et al. investigated the use of Gaussian processes (GPs) for extrapolating battery internal resistance and subsequently deriving capacity estimates based on a linear relationship between resistance and capacity, demonstrating that GPs could handle the forecasting task. In parallel, Zhang et al. (2022) situate their own contribution within a landscape where existing approaches for battery health forecasting often rely on combining model-based analysis with machine learning regressors, an observation that frames the broader methodological foundations upon which recent state-of-health estimation research is built.

# 4 Methods and Techniques

# 6 Challenges and Open Problems


Accurate battery health forecasting remains hindered by several persistent challenges. Richardson et al. (2017) underscore that the inability to reliably predict a battery’s rate of capacity loss or remaining useful life leaves systems vulnerable to unanticipated performance declines or operational failures. This fundamental uncertainty is compounded by the complex, nonlinear nature of battery degradation. While advanced methods such as Gaussian process regression have been explored for extrapolating internal resistance and deriving capacity estimates, Richardson et al. (2017) note prior conclusions that, although these models can characterize nonlinear degradation data, they still face limitations in fully capturing the phenomenon. Zhang et al. (2022) further highlight that existing forecasting approaches often struggle with the direct impacts of aging, namely capacity fade and resistance increase, which reduce usable energy and power. Uncontrolled degradation, they add, can lead to unplanned failures, pointing to a critical open problem in developing robust methods that can anticipate and mitigate these risks across diverse applications such as electric vehicles and grid storage.

# 7 Future Directions

Current battery health forecasting methods remain constrained by several fundamental limitations that prevent reliable deployment across diverse applications. The inherently nonlinear and time-varying nature of degradation processes is not yet fully captured by existing models, with even flexible approaches such as Gaussian process regression struggling to characterize the complete evolution of capacity fade and resistance growth. A persistent gap exists between model-based techniques that incorporate physical insights and purely data-driven regressors, as hybrid frameworks have not yet achieved the robustness needed to handle the wide variability in operating conditions, usage patterns, and environmental factors encountered in real-world electric vehicles and grid storage systems. Furthermore, the inability to reliably predict the rate of capacity loss leaves systems vulnerable to unanticipated performance declines, and uncontrolled degradation continues to pose risks of unplanned failures that compromise both safety and economic viability.

Future research should prioritize the development of more expressive and adaptive prognostic models that can better capture the multi-scale and multi-physics nature of battery aging. One promising direction involves the integration of deep probabilistic methods, such as deep Gaussian processes or Bayesian neural networks, which can learn hierarchical representations of degradation dynamics while providing well-calibrated uncertainty estimates essential for risk-aware decision-making. These models should be designed to incorporate domain knowledge from electrochemical impedance spectroscopy, incremental capacity analysis, and differential voltage techniques as informative priors or physics-informed loss functions, thereby bridging the gap between physical understanding and data-driven flexibility. Another critical avenue is the creation of transferable and generalizable prognostic frameworks that can adapt across different battery chemistries, form factors, and operational regimes without requiring extensive retraining or recalibration. This could be achieved through meta-learning strategies, domain adaptation techniques, or foundation models pre-trained on large-scale, multi-source battery aging datasets. Additionally, research should explore online learning algorithms that continuously update model parameters as new degradation data become available during operation, enabling battery management systems to refine their forecasts in real time and respond dynamically to evolving health states.

Advancing these research directions would yield transformative impacts on the safety, reliability, and economic efficiency of battery-dependent systems. More accurate and uncertainty-aware forecasts would enable proactive maintenance scheduling, reducing the risk of catastrophic failures in electric vehicles and preventing costly downtime in grid storage installations. Improved generalization across battery types would accelerate the adoption of second-life applications by providing reliable health assessments for repurposed cells, thereby supporting circular economy objectives. Ultimately, robust and adaptive prognostic capabilities integrated into next-generation battery management systems would extend operational lifetimes, optimize charging strategies, and enhance user confidence, directly contributing to the broader electrification of transport and energy infrastructure.

# 8 Conclusion

This survey has examined the landscape of lithium-ion battery health forecasting by synthesizing two complementary perspectives on data-driven prognostics. The analysis reveals that Gaussian process regression offers a principled probabilistic framework capable of characterizing the nonlinear degradation trajectories inherent to battery aging, yet early implementations encountered limitations in fully capturing the evolution of capacity fade and resistance growth over extended operational lifetimes. In parallel, hybrid approaches that integrate model-based incremental capacity analysis with machine learning regressors such as support vector regression demonstrate improved estimation fidelity by embedding physical insights directly into the learning pipeline. Across both paradigms, the fundamental challenge remains consistent: the complex, multifaceted nature of battery degradation resists capture by any single modeling strategy, and the direct consequences of capacity loss and impedance rise—reduced usable energy, diminished power capability, and elevated failure risk—underscore the high stakes of accurate forecasting for applications ranging from electric vehicles to stationary grid storage.

The significance of this survey lies in its structured juxtaposition of two methodological families that represent distinct philosophies in battery prognostics. By tracing the evolution from early Gaussian process-based extrapolation of internal resistance toward contemporary hybrid frameworks that fuse electrochemical signatures with machine learning, the discussion clarifies how the field has progressively moved toward integrating physical interpretability with data-driven flexibility. This synthesis highlights a broader trend in state-of-health estimation research: the recognition that neither purely empirical nor purely model-based approaches alone suffice to address the uncertainty, nonlinearity, and operational variability that characterize real-world battery aging. The survey thus provides researchers and practitioners with a conceptual map of the trade-offs between probabilistic uncertainty quantification and hybrid physical–statistical modeling, informing the selection and development of prognostic techniques tailored to specific application requirements and data availability constraints.

Looking forward, the battery prognostics community must prioritize the development of methods that not only achieve high estimation accuracy under controlled laboratory conditions but also demonstrate robustness across diverse usage profiles, environmental conditions, and cell chemistries encountered in field deployments. Bridging the gap between the expressive power of Gaussian processes and the physical grounding of model-based hybrids represents a promising direction, as does the incorporation of emerging data sources such as in situ electrochemical impedance spectroscopy and real-time operational logs from fleet vehicles. Equally critical is the establishment of standardized benchmarking protocols and open-access aging datasets that span the full diversity of degradation modes, enabling rigorous comparison of forecasting methods and accelerating translational impact. Concerted efforts across academia, industry, and regulatory bodies will be essential to transform battery health forecasting from a research endeavor into a reliable engineering tool that safeguards system performance, extends asset lifetime, and underpins the safe, cost-effective deployment of lithium-ion batteries in the global transition toward electrified energy systems.
