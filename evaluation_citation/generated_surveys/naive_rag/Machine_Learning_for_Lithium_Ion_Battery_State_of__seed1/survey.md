# 1 Abstract

Lithium-ion batteries have become indispensable across electric vehicles, stationary energy storage, and portable electronics, yet their gradual capacity fade and internal resistance increase pose significant challenges to system reliability, safety, and lifecycle cost. Accurate forecasting of battery state of health and remaining useful life is therefore essential to prevent unanticipated performance degradation and unplanned failures. This survey systematically reviews recent advances in data-driven and model-based approaches for battery health prognostics, with a particular focus on the role of Gaussian process regression and its integration with complementary techniques such as support vector regression and incremental capacity analysis. We examine how these methods address the inherent nonlinearity and complexity of battery degradation trajectories, highlighting their respective strengths in capturing aging patterns and quantifying prediction uncertainty. Our analysis reveals that while Gaussian process models offer principled uncertainty estimates and adaptability to diverse operating conditions, challenges remain in achieving robust long-term generalization and in fully characterizing degradation pathways across varied usage scenarios. We also identify promising directions for hybrid frameworks that combine physical degradation insights with flexible machine learning regressors to enhance forecasting fidelity. This survey provides a structured overview of current methodologies, delineates persistent limitations, and outlines future research opportunities for developing more reliable and interpretable battery health forecasting systems.

# 2 Introduction

Lithium-ion batteries have become an indispensable energy storage technology across a broad spectrum of modern applications, ranging from electric vehicles and stationary grid storage to portable consumer electronics. Their widespread adoption is driven by favorable characteristics such as high energy density, long cycle life, and declining manufacturing costs. However, battery performance inevitably degrades over time through complex electrochemical and mechanical aging mechanisms, manifesting as capacity fade and internal resistance increase. This gradual deterioration directly impacts the usable energy and power delivery capability of the battery system, while uncontrolled or unexpected degradation can lead to catastrophic failures, compromising system reliability, operational safety, and economic viability. As these batteries are increasingly deployed in safety-critical and capital-intensive applications, the ability to accurately monitor their health status and predict their future degradation trajectory has emerged as a fundamental requirement for ensuring dependable operation, optimizing maintenance strategies, and enabling timely replacement decisions.

Despite significant research efforts, accurate estimation of battery state of health and prediction of remaining useful life remain challenging problems that have not been fully resolved. The primary difficulty stems from the inherently complex and nonlinear nature of battery degradation processes, which are influenced by multiple interacting factors including operating temperature, charge-discharge rates, depth of discharge, and manufacturing variability. Traditional physics-based models, while providing valuable mechanistic insights, often struggle to capture the full complexity of real-world degradation trajectories without extensive parameterization and calibration efforts. On the other hand, purely data-driven approaches face the challenge of generalizing beyond the specific conditions represented in training data, particularly when confronted with the diverse and evolving operating profiles encountered in practical applications. A critical open problem lies in developing prognostic methods that can not only accurately fit observed nonlinear degradation patterns from historical data but also provide reliable long-term forecasts of future capacity loss and remaining useful life under varying and previously unseen operating conditions. The gap between model flexibility and robust generalization performance continues to motivate intensive research into advanced machine learning techniques tailored for battery health prognostics.

This survey paper makes two primary contributions to the field of battery health estimation and prediction. First, it provides a comprehensive and structured review of machine learning approaches applied to lithium-ion battery state-of-health estimation and remaining-useful-life prediction, systematically categorizing the diverse range of techniques that have been proposed in the literature. The survey covers both classical machine learning methods, such as support vector regression and relevance vector machines, and more advanced approaches including Gaussian process regression, deep learning architectures, and hybrid methods that combine physical models with data-driven techniques. For each category of methods, the underlying principles, modeling assumptions, and typical implementation strategies are examined in detail, providing readers with a clear understanding of the methodological landscape and the evolution of research directions in this domain.

Second, this survey offers a critical comparative analysis of the strengths and limitations of different methodological approaches, with particular attention to their practical applicability and generalization capabilities. By examining how various techniques handle key challenges such as nonlinear degradation patterns, capacity regeneration phenomena, and varying operating conditions, the paper identifies the conditions under which different methods excel or falter. Special emphasis is placed on the trade-offs between model complexity, data requirements, computational cost, and prediction accuracy, providing practical guidance for practitioners seeking to select appropriate methods for specific application scenarios. Furthermore, the survey highlights persistent challenges and open research questions that remain inadequately addressed, thereby identifying promising directions for future investigation and development in battery health prognostics.

The aim of this survey is to serve as a comprehensive reference for researchers and practitioners working on battery health management, offering both a systematic overview of the current state of the art and critical insights to guide future research. The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. The next section introduces the fundamental concepts of battery degradation, including key health indicators and degradation mechanisms. Subsequent sections present detailed examinations of major machine learning approaches, organized by methodological categories, with dedicated discussions of Gaussian process regression, support vector-based methods, deep learning techniques, and hybrid approaches. Following the methodological review, a comparative analysis section synthesizes findings across different approaches, discussing their relative merits and limitations. The paper concludes with a summary of key insights, identification of remaining challenges, and recommendations for future research directions in machine learning for battery state-of-health estimation and remaining-useful-life prediction.

# 3 Background and Foundations


Lithium-ion batteries are a critical technology for electric vehicles, stationary storage, and portable electronics, yet their aging directly impacts system reliability, safety, and cost (Zhang et al., 2022). Capacity fade and resistance increase reduce usable energy and power, and uncontrolled degradation can lead to unplanned failures (Zhang et al., 2022). Richardson et al. (2017) similarly emphasize that not knowing a battery’s rate of capacity loss or useful life renders the system susceptible to an unanticipated decline in performance. These concerns motivate the development of accurate forecasting methods for battery state of health and remaining useful life.

A foundational modeling approach in this domain is Gaussian process (GP) regression. Richardson et al. (2017) note that prior work by Goebel et al. investigated the use of GPs for extrapolating battery internal resistance and subsequently deriving capacity estimates based on a linear relationship between resistance and capacity, demonstrating that GPs could handle the associated prediction challenges.

# 4 Methods and Techniques

In a complementary line of work, Richardson et al. (2017) examined Gaussian process regression for forecasting battery state of health, noting that Gaussian processes are capable of handling the nonlinear data characteristic of battery degradation. Their review of prior work highlighted that Goebel et al. investigated the use of Gaussian processes to extrapolate battery internal resistance and subsequently derive capacity estimates through a linear relationship between resistance and capacity, though they concluded that limitations remained in fully characterizing the degradation trajectory (Richardson et al., 2017).

# 6 Challenges and Open Problems


Accurate prediction of battery state of health remains a critical need across applications ranging from electric vehicles and grid storage to portable electronics, where unanticipated capacity fade or resistance increase can directly compromise system reliability, safety, and cost (Richardson et al., 2017; Zhang et al., 2022). A central challenge lies in the inherent complexity of battery degradation data. Richardson et al. (2017) note that Gaussian process regression has shown capability in handling the nonlinear data patterns characteristic of battery aging, yet earlier investigations concluded that while such models could characterize degradation, significant forecasting difficulties persist. This points to an open problem in developing prognostic methods that not only fit observed nonlinear trends but also generalize reliably to future capacity loss and remaining useful life under diverse operating conditions. Further work is needed to bridge the gap between model flexibility and robust, long-term forecasting performance.

# 7 Future Directions

Despite the progress made in battery state-of-health estimation, several important limitations persist. Current data-driven methods, including Gaussian process regression, often excel at interpolating within observed degradation regimes but struggle to extrapolate reliably into future operating cycles. The nonlinear and path-dependent nature of battery aging, combined with variability across cells, chemistries, and usage profiles, introduces substantial uncertainty that existing models cannot fully capture. Moreover, many approaches rely on simplified assumptions about the relationship between measurable parameters such as internal resistance and capacity, which may not hold under all conditions. The lack of robust generalization across diverse temperature ranges, charge-discharge rates, and aging mechanisms remains a central obstacle, as does the difficulty of incorporating physical degradation knowledge without sacrificing the flexibility that makes machine learning methods attractive.

Future research should prioritize the development of hybrid prognostic frameworks that tightly couple data-driven flexibility with physically grounded degradation models. One promising direction is the integration of Gaussian processes with electrochemical models or incremental capacity analysis features, enabling the model to respect known physical constraints while learning complex nonlinear patterns from operational data. Such approaches could incorporate multi-output formulations that jointly predict capacity fade and resistance growth, capturing their coupled evolution rather than treating them independently. Another important avenue involves advancing uncertainty quantification for long-term forecasts, moving beyond point predictions toward well-calibrated probabilistic estimates of remaining useful life that can inform risk-aware decision-making in safety-critical applications. Additionally, transfer learning and domain adaptation techniques warrant deeper investigation to address cell-to-cell variability and to enable models trained on accelerated aging data to generalize to real-world usage scenarios. The development of standardized benchmarking datasets that span diverse chemistries, form factors, and operating conditions would further accelerate progress by enabling rigorous comparison of forecasting methods.

The successful realization of these research directions would have a transformative impact across multiple application domains. Improved prognostic accuracy and uncertainty awareness would directly enhance the safety and reliability of electric vehicles by enabling timely maintenance and reducing the risk of unexpected failures. In grid-scale storage, robust life predictions would support more effective asset management and economic dispatch strategies, lowering the total cost of ownership and facilitating deeper penetration of renewable energy. For portable electronics, better health forecasting could extend device lifetimes and reduce electronic waste. Beyond these immediate benefits, advancing the scientific understanding of how to fuse physical knowledge with machine learning for long-term forecasting would contribute broadly to the field of prognostics and health management, offering methodologies transferable to other complex engineered systems where degradation is nonlinear, uncertain, and safety-critical.

# 8 Conclusion

This survey has examined the landscape of data-driven and model-based techniques for lithium-ion battery state-of-health estimation and remaining useful life forecasting, with a particular focus on Gaussian process regression and hybrid approaches. The review reveals that capacity fade and internal resistance increase remain the principal degradation indicators across electric vehicle, stationary storage, and portable electronics applications, and that unanticipated performance declines pose direct risks to system reliability, safety, and lifetime cost. Gaussian process regression has emerged as a compelling framework due to its ability to capture the nonlinear and time-varying characteristics of battery degradation data, while combined strategies that integrate physical degradation insights with machine learning regressors have demonstrated improved estimation accuracy. Nevertheless, the analysis indicates that significant challenges persist in achieving robust long-term forecasting, particularly when models must generalize beyond observed training regimes to diverse operating conditions, varying load profiles, and different environmental stressors.

The significance of this survey lies in its systematic synthesis of two complementary methodological streams—purely data-driven Gaussian process forecasting and physics-informed hybrid modeling—within the unified context of battery health prognostics. By tracing the evolution from early Gaussian process applications on internal resistance extrapolation to contemporary hybrid frameworks, the review clarifies both the demonstrated capabilities and the persistent limitations of current approaches. This synthesis is timely given the accelerating deployment of lithium-ion batteries in safety-critical and economically sensitive domains, where the cost of unexpected failure continues to rise. The survey further highlights a critical gap between model flexibility and long-term predictive reliability, providing researchers and practitioners with a structured understanding of where methodological advances are most urgently needed.

Moving forward, the battery prognostics community must prioritize the development of forecasting methods that not only fit observed nonlinear degradation trends but also maintain accuracy over extended prediction horizons under realistic and variable operating conditions. Future research should focus on enhancing model generalization through the incorporation of uncertainty quantification, transfer learning across cell chemistries and form factors, and the integration of multi-modal degradation signals. Equally important is the establishment of standardized benchmarking protocols and publicly available aging datasets that span diverse usage scenarios, enabling rigorous comparison of emerging techniques. Collaborative efforts between the machine learning and electrochemical science communities will be essential to bridge the gap between data-driven flexibility and physical interpretability, ultimately delivering prognostic tools that can ensure the safe, reliable, and cost-effective operation of battery systems at scale.
