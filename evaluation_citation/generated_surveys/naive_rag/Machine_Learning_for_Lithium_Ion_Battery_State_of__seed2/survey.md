# 1 Abstract

Lithium-ion batteries have become indispensable in applications such as electric vehicles, stationary energy storage, and portable electronics, yet their progressive capacity fade and resistance increase pose significant risks to system reliability, safety, and operational cost. This survey provides a comprehensive review of data-driven and hybrid methodologies for battery state-of-health estimation and remaining useful life prediction, with a focus on techniques that address the inherent nonlinearity and uncertainty of cell degradation. We systematically examine probabilistic approaches, including Gaussian process regression, as well as hybrid strategies that integrate model-based feature extraction—such as incremental capacity analysis—with machine learning regressors like support vector regression. Our analysis reveals that while current methods effectively capture short-term degradation trends and provide uncertainty quantification, they often fall short in delivering robust long-term forecasts, particularly under abrupt or accelerated aging conditions. Furthermore, we identify critical gaps in handling diverse failure modes and in generalizing across different cell chemistries and operating environments. This survey consolidates recent advances, delineates persistent limitations, and outlines promising directions for developing more reliable and interpretable battery health forecasting frameworks.

# 2 Introduction

The rapid expansion of electrified transportation, renewable energy storage, and portable electronics has positioned lithium-ion batteries as a cornerstone of modern energy systems. Their high energy density, long cycle life, and declining manufacturing costs have driven widespread adoption across industries that demand reliable and efficient power sources. However, battery performance is intrinsically transient; repeated charge and discharge cycles induce complex electrochemical and mechanical degradation processes that progressively diminish capacity and increase internal resistance. This aging phenomenon directly threatens system reliability, operational safety, and economic viability. As batteries degrade, the usable energy and power they can deliver steadily decline, and in the absence of accurate health monitoring, systems become susceptible to unanticipated failures that can disrupt service, incur substantial costs, and pose safety hazards. Consequently, the ability to precisely estimate the state of health and predict the remaining useful life of lithium-ion batteries has emerged as a critical requirement for ensuring the safe, dependable, and cost-effective operation of battery-powered systems.

Despite significant research efforts, battery health forecasting remains a formidable challenge due to the inherently nonlinear, time-varying, and uncertain nature of degradation mechanisms. Lithium-ion cells exhibit diverse aging trajectories influenced by operating conditions, manufacturing variability, and usage history, making it difficult to capture their evolution with a single modeling paradigm. Two fundamental problems persist in the literature. First, purely data-driven methods, while capable of learning complex degradation patterns from historical data, often struggle to generalize across different battery chemistries, operating regimes, and aging conditions. Their predictive reliability degrades when confronted with scenarios not well represented in training data, and many such methods provide only point estimates without meaningful uncertainty quantification. Second, model-based approaches that rely on electrochemical principles or equivalent circuit representations offer physical interpretability but frequently lack the flexibility to adapt to the idiosyncratic degradation signatures observed in real-world applications. The integration of these two paradigms presents additional challenges, as the optimal manner in which to combine physical knowledge with machine learning remains an open question. Moreover, the need to anticipate not only gradual capacity fade but also abrupt degradation phenomena, such as lithium plating or accelerated side reactions, further complicates the forecasting task and underscores the limitations of current methodologies.

This survey makes several contributions to the understanding and advancement of machine learning techniques for lithium-ion battery state-of-health estimation and remaining-useful-life prediction. First, it provides a comprehensive and structured taxonomy of existing approaches, systematically categorizing methods into purely data-driven techniques, model-based strategies, and hybrid frameworks that fuse physical insight with statistical learning. Within each category, the survey examines the underlying principles, algorithmic foundations, and typical application contexts, enabling readers to grasp the conceptual landscape and identify the relative strengths and weaknesses of different methodological families. Special attention is devoted to the treatment of uncertainty, a critical yet often underexplored dimension in battery prognostics, and the survey highlights how probabilistic methods such as Gaussian process regression address this requirement compared with deterministic alternatives.

Second, this paper offers a critical analysis of the key challenges that continue to impede progress in the field. It examines issues related to data availability, quality, and diversity, including the scarcity of large-scale, publicly accessible aging datasets spanning multiple battery types and operating conditions. The survey further investigates the difficulties associated with feature engineering and selection, discussing how raw operational measurements can be transformed into health-sensitive indicators through techniques such as incremental capacity analysis and differential voltage analysis. Additionally, it addresses the practical considerations of deploying machine learning models in real-time embedded systems, where computational constraints and memory limitations impose stringent requirements on algorithmic complexity. By synthesizing insights from a broad body of work, this survey identifies open problems and promising research directions that can guide future investigations toward more robust, generalizable, and practically deployable battery health forecasting solutions.

The primary aim of this survey is to equip researchers and practitioners with a thorough understanding of the state-of-the-art in machine learning-driven battery health prognostics while delineating the pathways toward overcoming persistent limitations. The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. The next section establishes foundational concepts, including definitions of state of health and remaining useful life, an overview of lithium-ion battery degradation mechanisms, and a discussion of the key performance indicators used to evaluate forecasting accuracy. Subsequent sections systematically review data-driven methods, model-based approaches, and hybrid strategies, respectively, with each section providing detailed technical exposition and comparative assessment. The survey then examines cross-cutting challenges such as data scarcity, feature extraction, uncertainty quantification, and real-world deployment constraints. Finally, the paper concludes by summarizing the current state of the field and outlining a roadmap for future research that can accelerate the transition of machine learning-based battery health forecasting from laboratory studies to widespread industrial adoption.

# 3 Background and Foundations


Lithium-ion batteries are a key technology for electric vehicles, stationary storage, and portable electronics, and their aging directly impacts system reliability, safety, and cost (Zhang et al., 2022). Capacity fade and resistance increase reduce usable energy and power, while uncontrolled degradation can lead to unplanned failures (Zhang et al., 2022). Richardson et al. (2017) similarly emphasize that not knowing a battery’s rate of capacity loss or useful life renders the system susceptible to an unanticipated decline in performance. Accurately predicting the future capacity and remaining useful life of batteries is therefore necessary for the safe and economic operation of systems ranging from transport to grid energy storage (Richardson et al., 2017).

Prior work has explored data-driven approaches to address these forecasting challenges. Richardson et al. (2017) note that Goebel et al. investigated the use of Gaussian processes (GPs) for extrapolating battery internal resistance and subsequently deriving capacity estimates based on a linear relationship between resistance and capacity, demonstrating that GPs could handle the associated prediction tasks. Zhang et al. (2022) highlight that existing approaches for battery health forecasting often rely on combining model-based analysis techniques with machine learning methods, such as support vector regression, to estimate state of health.

# 4 Methods and Techniques

A central challenge in battery health forecasting is handling the nonlinear degradation patterns that lithium-ion cells exhibit over time. Richardson et al. (2017) review early work by Goebel et al., who applied Gaussian process regression to extrapolate battery internal resistance and then derived capacity estimates through a linear resistance–capacity relationship. That investigation demonstrated that Gaussian processes could capture the nonlinear data characteristic of battery degradation, although the authors concluded that the approach had limitations in fully characterizing the evolving health state. The method thus illustrates a probabilistic, data-driven route to state-of-health estimation that explicitly models uncertainty in the forecasts.

In parallel, model-based techniques remain important for extracting health-sensitive features from operational data. Zhang et al. (2022) combine model-based incremental capacity analysis with support vector regression to estimate state-of-health. The incremental capacity analysis serves as a physically grounded preprocessing step that isolates degradation signatures from voltage–capacity curves, while the support vector regression maps those features to capacity estimates. This hybrid strategy leverages both electrochemical understanding and machine learning flexibility, aiming to improve estimation accuracy under realistic aging conditions where capacity fade and resistance increase progressively reduce usable energy and power.

# 6 Challenges and Open Problems

A central challenge in battery health forecasting is the difficulty of capturing the nonlinear and uncertain nature of degradation. Richardson et al. (2017) observed that while Gaussian processes can model the nonlinear trends in battery internal resistance and capacity fade, their ability to characterize future behavior remains limited. This points to a broader open problem: existing prognostic methods may fit observed degradation well but still struggle to provide reliable long-term predictions of remaining useful life, leaving systems vulnerable to unanticipated performance declines (Richardson et al., 2017).

Further challenges arise from the direct impact of aging on system reliability, safety, and cost. Zhang et al. (2022) highlighted that capacity fade and resistance increase reduce usable energy and power, and that uncontrolled degradation can lead to unplanned failures. This underscores the need for forecasting approaches that not only track gradual aging but also anticipate abrupt or accelerated degradation modes—a requirement that current methods do not yet fully satisfy. Addressing these gaps remains an open problem for the field.

# 7 Future Directions

Current forecasting methods for lithium‑ion battery health remain constrained by their limited ability to capture the full nonlinear, non‑stationary, and uncertain nature of cell degradation over long time horizons. While Gaussian process regression and hybrid model‑based machine learning pipelines have shown promise in fitting observed capacity fade and resistance increase, they often struggle to generalize across diverse usage profiles, chemistries, and aging regimes. The reliance on linear or stationary assumptions between resistance and capacity, the difficulty of anticipating abrupt degradation transitions such as knee points or sudden capacity plunges, and the absence of robust uncertainty quantification over multi‑year horizons all leave systems exposed to unanticipated performance declines. Moreover, existing approaches typically treat degradation as a single‑cell, single‑mode phenomenon, failing to account for cell‑to‑cell variability, interactions within modules and packs, and the coupling of multiple aging mechanisms under realistic operating conditions. These gaps highlight the need for prognostic frameworks that are simultaneously more expressive in capturing complex degradation dynamics, more reliable in providing long‑term forecasts with well‑calibrated confidence bounds, and more scalable to fleet‑level and system‑level applications.

Future research should pursue several interconnected directions to overcome these limitations. First, deep probabilistic models that combine the flexibility of neural networks with rigorous uncertainty quantification—such as deep Gaussian processes, Bayesian recurrent neural networks, or normalizing flows—should be developed to model the full trajectory of capacity fade and resistance growth without imposing restrictive functional forms. These models can be designed to learn hierarchical representations of aging that disentangle gradual wear from abrupt failure modes, enabling early detection of accelerated degradation and more trustworthy remaining useful life predictions. Second, physics‑informed machine learning approaches should be advanced to embed electrochemical constraints and domain knowledge directly into data‑driven architectures. By encoding differential equations governing lithium‑ion transport, solid‑electrolyte interphase growth, and active material loss into the learning objective, models can achieve better extrapolation beyond the training envelope and maintain physical consistency in long‑range forecasts. Third, multi‑task and transfer learning frameworks should be explored to leverage data across different cell chemistries, form factors, and duty cycles, reducing the data hunger of purely data‑driven methods and enabling rapid adaptation to new battery designs or usage scenarios. Fourth, the development of fleet‑level prognostic systems that model the joint evolution of thousands of cells operating under heterogeneous conditions will be essential for grid storage and large electric vehicle deployments. Such systems must incorporate hierarchical Bayesian models or graph neural networks that capture both individual cell degradation and the statistical dependencies induced by shared manufacturing batches, thermal environments, and control strategies.

Advancing battery health forecasting along these lines would substantially improve the safety, reliability, and economic viability of lithium‑ion battery systems across transport, grid storage, and portable electronics. More accurate and well‑calibrated predictions of remaining useful life would enable predictive maintenance scheduling that prevents unplanned failures, extends asset lifetimes through optimized operating policies, and reduces warranty costs for manufacturers. In grid storage applications, fleet‑level forecasting would support better dispatch decisions and capacity planning, directly lowering the levelized cost of storage. In electric vehicles, reliable long‑term degradation forecasts would enhance consumer confidence, inform second‑life repurposing decisions, and reduce the risk of range‑related safety incidents. By bridging the gap between laboratory‑derived degradation models and real‑world operational data, the proposed directions would accelerate the transition toward autonomous, self‑aware battery management systems that continuously adapt to evolving health states and operating conditions.

# 8 Conclusion

This survey has examined the landscape of data-driven and hybrid approaches for lithium-ion battery health forecasting, with a focus on capacity fade and remaining useful life prediction. The analysis reveals that Gaussian process regression offers a principled probabilistic framework capable of capturing the nonlinear degradation trajectories characteristic of lithium-ion cells, while support vector regression, when combined with model-based preprocessing such as incremental capacity analysis, provides a complementary hybrid strategy that leverages electrochemical insights to enhance estimation accuracy. Despite these advances, the reviewed works converge on a shared limitation: existing methods often perform well in fitting observed degradation data but exhibit reduced reliability when extrapolating into long-term future horizons or when confronted with abrupt changes in aging behavior. The difficulty of fully characterizing the evolving health state under realistic operating conditions, where capacity loss and resistance growth interact with diverse stress factors, remains a persistent obstacle. Furthermore, the reviewed literature underscores that uncontrolled degradation can precipitate unplanned failures, highlighting the gap between current prognostic capabilities and the demands of safety-critical applications.

The significance of this survey lies in its synthesis of two influential methodological directions—probabilistic machine learning and physics-informed hybrid modeling—within the specific context of battery health forecasting. By juxtaposing the strengths and limitations of Gaussian process-based extrapolation and support vector regression coupled with incremental capacity analysis, the survey clarifies the trade-offs between purely data-driven uncertainty quantification and approaches that embed domain knowledge through feature engineering. This comparative perspective is timely given the accelerating deployment of lithium-ion batteries across electric vehicles, stationary energy storage, and portable electronics, where accurate state-of-health estimation directly influences system reliability, operational safety, and total cost of ownership. The discussion also brings into focus the critical gap between short-term state estimation and long-term remaining useful life prediction, a distinction that carries profound implications for maintenance scheduling, warranty design, and second-life battery applications. By identifying the shared challenge of capturing nonlinear, uncertain, and potentially abrupt degradation modes, this survey provides a structured foundation for researchers seeking to advance beyond the current state of the art.

Moving forward, the battery health forecasting community must prioritize the development of methods that not only interpolate past degradation trends but also generalize robustly to unseen future conditions. This calls for deeper integration of physical degradation models with flexible machine learning architectures, as well as rigorous benchmarking under diverse aging protocols that include realistic load profiles, temperature variations, and calendar aging effects. Equally important is the need for probabilistic frameworks that can express confidence in long-range predictions and detect early signatures of accelerated degradation before failures occur. Collaborative efforts to share high-quality aging datasets across institutions and application domains would accelerate progress by enabling reproducible comparisons and fostering innovation in uncertainty-aware prognostics. Ultimately, closing the gap between current forecasting capabilities and the reliability demands of real-world battery systems is not merely a technical aspiration but a prerequisite for the sustainable and safe expansion of electrified technologies worldwide.
