# 1 Abstract

The emergence of large language models (LLMs) has significantly advanced the development of autonomous agents, intelligent entities capable of perceiving their environment, reasoning, and executing actions to achieve specific goals. Among the core capabilities required for such agents, planning stands out as particularly critical, demanding complex understanding, reasoning, and decision-making processes. This survey provides a comprehensive and structured examination of LLM-based autonomous agents through the lens of planning capabilities, systematically categorizing existing research into distinct methodological directions. We analyze key approaches including rationale-based methods that leverage human-annotated data for explicit output explanation, and chain-of-thought reasoning techniques that enable models to generate transparent, step-by-step reasoning traces. The survey further investigates recent advancements aimed at enhancing the robustness and reliability of these reasoning processes, while also addressing the growing emphasis on evaluating agents within dynamic, multi-agent, and real-world application scenarios. Our analysis reveals persistent challenges in achieving dependable planning in open-ended environments and highlights the need for more transparent reasoning mechanisms. This survey consolidates the current landscape of planning-focused LLM agent research, offering insights that inform the design of more capable and application-oriented autonomous systems.

# 2 Introduction

Autonomous agents represent a significant frontier in artificial intelligence research, defined as intelligent entities capable of perceiving their environment, formulating plans, and executing actions to accomplish specific goals. The emergence of large language models has fundamentally transformed the landscape of agent development, providing unprecedented capabilities in natural language understanding, knowledge representation, and generative reasoning. These models have enabled the creation of agents that can operate across diverse domains, from software engineering and scientific research to complex decision-making tasks that previously required extensive human expertise. As the field has matured, researchers have increasingly recognized that the core challenge in building effective autonomous agents lies not merely in their ability to process language, but in their capacity for sophisticated planning—a capability that demands the integration of complex understanding, multi-step reasoning, and adaptive decision-making in dynamic environments.

Despite remarkable progress in developing LLM-based autonomous agents, several fundamental challenges persist that hinder their practical deployment in real-world applications. The primary difficulty centers on achieving robust and reliable planning capabilities in open-ended environments where conditions are unpredictable and requirements evolve continuously. While current agents can demonstrate impressive reasoning abilities in controlled settings, they often struggle with maintaining consistency across extended planning horizons, adapting to novel situations that deviate from their training distributions, and providing transparent justifications for their decisions. A related challenge concerns the evaluation of these agents, as traditional benchmarks focusing on isolated tasks fail to capture the complexity of real-world deployment scenarios. The field lacks comprehensive frameworks for assessing how well agents perform in dynamic, multi-agent environments that more accurately reflect practical applications. Furthermore, the integration of external tools and resources into the planning process introduces additional complexity, requiring agents to not only reason about abstract goals but also understand when and how to leverage available instruments effectively.

This survey makes two primary contributions to the field of LLM-based autonomous agents. First, it provides a systematic and comprehensive analysis of planning and reasoning methods specifically designed for LLM-based agents, offering the first structured taxonomy that categorizes existing approaches through the lens of planning capabilities. The survey organizes the landscape of current research into coherent methodological categories, examining how different techniques address the fundamental requirements of understanding, reasoning, and decision-making that constitute effective planning. This includes detailed treatment of rationale-based methods that leverage human-annotated data to explain model outputs, chain-of-thought reasoning approaches that enable models to generate step-by-step textual explanations of their reasoning processes, and recent advances aimed at making these reasoning mechanisms more robust and reliable. By framing the analysis around planning as the central capability, the survey illuminates the relationships between seemingly disparate research threads and identifies the underlying principles that govern successful agent design.

Second, this survey offers an extensive examination of tool use and evaluation benchmarks that are essential for translating theoretical planning capabilities into practical applications. The analysis covers how LLM-based agents can effectively incorporate external tools into their planning and execution pipelines, addressing the critical challenge of bridging abstract reasoning with concrete action in real-world contexts. The survey evaluates existing benchmarks across multiple dimensions, including their ability to assess agent performance in professional domains, their capacity to measure adaptability to dynamic conditions, and their effectiveness in approximating the complexity of genuine deployment scenarios. By synthesizing insights from both methodological developments and evaluation frameworks, the survey identifies current limitations and open problems that define the frontier of research in this rapidly evolving field.

The aim of this survey is to provide researchers and practitioners with a structured understanding of the current state of planning and reasoning in LLM-based agents, while also highlighting promising directions for future investigation. The paper is organized as follows. We begin by establishing foundational concepts and formally defining the role of planning within autonomous agent architectures. We then present our taxonomy of planning and reasoning methods, systematically examining each category and its representative techniques. Subsequently, we explore the integration of tool use into agent planning frameworks, analyzing how external resources can enhance agent capabilities. Following this, we review evaluation benchmarks and methodologies, assessing their strengths and limitations in measuring real-world performance. Finally, we discuss open challenges and outline future research directions that hold promise for advancing the field toward more capable, reliable, and practical autonomous agents.

# 7 Future Directions

The current body of research on LLM-based autonomous agents reveals several critical limitations that demand attention. Planning, while identified as a core capability, remains brittle in open-ended and dynamic environments where agents must handle unforeseen obstacles, ambiguous goals, and evolving constraints. Existing chain-of-thought reasoning approaches, though promising for generating textual explanations, often lack robustness and can produce plausible-sounding but logically flawed plans. Rationale-based methods that rely on human-annotated data face scalability challenges, as the cost and effort of curating high-quality annotations limit their applicability across diverse domains. Furthermore, evaluation frameworks have yet to fully capture the complexity of real-world deployment, with most benchmarks focusing on isolated tasks rather than the multi-agent, temporally extended, and resource-constrained scenarios that characterize practical applications. The gap between structured planning taxonomies and the messy reality of deployment-ready systems remains substantial, leaving open questions about how to build agents that plan reliably, adapt gracefully, and explain their decisions in trustworthy ways.

Future research should prioritize the development of planning mechanisms that are inherently robust to environmental uncertainty and capable of graceful failure recovery. This includes exploring methods that integrate formal verification with neural reasoning, enabling agents to detect and correct logical inconsistencies in their own plans before execution. A promising direction involves advancing chain-of-thought reasoning beyond linear explanation generation toward iterative, self-correcting processes where agents can revise their plans based on simulated outcomes or environmental feedback. Equally important is the creation of more realistic evaluation benchmarks that model multi-agent coordination, long-horizon task completion, and the trade-offs between planning depth and computational cost. These benchmarks should measure not only task success but also the transparency, reliability, and safety of the planning process. Another vital avenue is reducing dependence on large-scale human annotation by developing unsupervised or weakly supervised methods for rationale generation, potentially leveraging the generative capabilities of LLMs themselves to bootstrap training data while maintaining quality control. Research should also investigate how planning architectures can be made modular, allowing agents to compose specialized reasoning components for different sub-tasks while maintaining global coherence.

Advancing these research directions would significantly accelerate the deployment of LLM-based agents in high-stakes and complex real-world settings. More robust planning would enable autonomous agents to operate reliably in domains such as healthcare coordination, legal analysis, and scientific research, where errors carry substantial consequences. Improved chain-of-thought and rationale-generation methods would enhance the interpretability of agent decisions, fostering trust among users and making it easier to audit and refine agent behavior. Realistic multi-agent benchmarks would drive the development of systems that can collaborate effectively with humans and other agents, unlocking applications in logistics, emergency response, and collaborative problem-solving. By reducing annotation bottlenecks and enabling agents to learn from fewer examples, the field could democratize access to capable autonomous agents across languages, cultures, and specialized professional domains. Ultimately, these advances would transform LLM-based agents from impressive but fragile demonstrations into dependable tools that augment human capabilities in meaningful and measurable ways.

# 8 Conclusion

The planning capabilities of large language model-based autonomous agents have emerged as a central research concern, reflecting the broader ambition to develop intelligent systems that can perceive, reason, and act with minimal human intervention. This survey has systematically examined the landscape of planning in LLM-based agents, organizing existing methods into coherent categories and highlighting the foundational role that planning plays in enabling complex task execution. The analysis reveals that current approaches to planning can be broadly understood through the lens of rationale-based explanation and chain-of-thought reasoning, both of which aim to make the internal decision-making processes of agents more interpretable and structured. Furthermore, the review underscores a notable shift in evaluation paradigms, with researchers increasingly favoring dynamic, multi-agent environments over static benchmarks to better capture the demands of real-world deployment. Despite considerable progress, the survey finds that ensuring robust and reliable planning in open-ended settings remains an unresolved challenge, as agents must contend with ambiguous goals, evolving contexts, and the inherent limitations of current reasoning mechanisms. These findings collectively point to a field that is rapidly maturing yet still grappling with fundamental questions about how to build agents that plan effectively and transparently.

The significance of this survey lies in its focused treatment of planning as the organizing principle for understanding LLM-based autonomous agents. By positioning planning at the center of the analysis, the survey provides a coherent framework that connects disparate research threads—from explanation generation to benchmark design—under a unified conceptual umbrella. This perspective is particularly valuable as the field moves toward application-oriented systems, where an agent’s ability to formulate, adapt, and execute plans directly determines its practical utility. The structured categorization of methods offered here not only clarifies the current state of the art but also reveals gaps and tensions that might otherwise remain obscured in broader reviews. For researchers and practitioners alike, the survey serves as both a map of existing terrain and a guide for identifying promising directions, thereby facilitating more principled progress in the design of capable and dependable autonomous agents.

Looking ahead, the development of LLM-based agents with robust planning abilities will require sustained attention to several interrelated challenges. Future work must prioritize the creation of reasoning mechanisms that are not only accurate but also resilient in the face of uncertainty and novelty, moving beyond brittle chain-of-thought patterns toward more adaptive and verifiable planning processes. Equally important is the continued refinement of evaluation methodologies that can faithfully assess planning quality in realistic, multi-agent, and long-horizon scenarios. The research community is encouraged to pursue interdisciplinary insights from cognitive science, control theory, and human-computer interaction to enrich the conceptual foundations of agent planning. Ultimately, realizing the full potential of autonomous agents demands a collective commitment to transparency, reproducibility, and rigorous empirical validation, ensuring that advances in planning translate into systems that are both capable and worthy of trust.
