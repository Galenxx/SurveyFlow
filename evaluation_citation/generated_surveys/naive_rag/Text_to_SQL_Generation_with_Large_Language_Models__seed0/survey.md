# 1 Abstract

The generation of structured query language (SQL) from natural language questions is a fundamental capability for enabling intuitive human-database interaction, yet it remains a challenging task due to the inherent complexity of aligning user intent with database structures. Central to this challenge is schema linking, the critical process of identifying the specific tables, columns, and values referenced in a natural language query. This survey provides a comprehensive examination of schema linking techniques within modern text-to-SQL systems, with a particular focus on approaches that leverage the capabilities of large language models. We systematically analyze the core trade-off between maximizing positive gain—ensuring all relevant schema elements are retrieved—and minimizing negative impact—avoiding the introduction of noisy or irrelevant schema information that can degrade generation quality. Our investigation highlights bidirectional schema linking as a foundational strategy for achieving high recall by first generating a preliminary query using the complete schema and then refining the alignment. We further review the integration of data augmentation and supervised fine-tuning methods that predate and complement the current era of large language models. This analysis underscores that robust schema linking remains a pivotal bottleneck, and continued progress is essential for building reliable and precise natural language interfaces to databases.

# 2 Introduction

The ability to query relational databases using natural language has long been a central goal in the field of natural language processing and human–computer interaction. Text-to-SQL generation, the task of automatically translating natural language questions into executable SQL queries, serves as a cornerstone of natural language interfaces to databases, enabling non-expert users to access structured data without requiring technical knowledge of query languages or database schemas. Over the past several years, the emergence of large language models has dramatically reshaped the landscape of text-to-SQL research, pushing the boundaries of what is achievable in terms of generation accuracy, generalization across domains, and adaptability to complex queries. These models, pretrained on vast corpora of code and natural language, have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in understanding user intent and producing syntactically correct SQL. However, despite these impressive advances, the core challenges of reliably grounding natural language questions in the corresponding database structures remain a persistent obstacle to building truly robust and practical text-to-SQL systems.

A fundamental difficulty that continues to hinder progress in this domain is the problem of schema linking—the process of identifying which tables, columns, and database values are referenced in a natural language question. Schema linking constitutes a foundational component of text-to-SQL generation, as errors at this stage propagate downstream and lead to incorrect or incomplete queries. The central tension in schema linking lies in balancing comprehensiveness with precision: incorporating too much schema information can introduce noise and distract the generation model, while incorporating too little risks missing critical elements needed to formulate the correct query. Even state-of-the-art large language models struggle with this trade-off, particularly when dealing with complex databases containing numerous tables and columns, ambiguous question phrasing, or domain-specific terminology. The challenge is further compounded by the diversity of database designs and the variability in how users express their information needs. As a result, schema linking remains a critical bottleneck that limits the reliability and real-world deployment of text-to-SQL systems.

This survey paper makes two primary contributions to the understanding and advancement of text-to-SQL generation with large language models. First, it provides a comprehensive and structured analysis of schema linking techniques, systematically categorizing the strategies that have been proposed to align natural language questions with database schemas. We examine both traditional approaches that rely on supervised fine-tuning and more recent methods that leverage the in-context learning and reasoning capabilities of large language models. Special attention is devoted to bidirectional schema linking strategies, which aim to maximize the positive gain of including relevant schema elements while minimizing the negative impact of irrelevant information. By dissecting the design choices, strengths, and limitations of existing schema linking methods, this survey offers researchers and practitioners a clear roadmap of the current state of the art and the open challenges that remain.

Second, this survey delivers an in-depth examination of the benchmarks used to evaluate text-to-SQL systems and the robustness of these systems under realistic conditions. We critically assess the most widely adopted benchmarks, including their scope, difficulty, and alignment with practical deployment scenarios. Beyond standard evaluation metrics, we investigate how current text-to-SQL models perform when confronted with challenging conditions such as domain shifts, ambiguous questions, and variations in schema representation. The survey highlights the gap between benchmark performance and real-world reliability, emphasizing the need for more rigorous robustness testing. By synthesizing insights from schema linking research, benchmark analysis, and robustness studies, this paper aims to provide a holistic perspective on the current capabilities and limitations of large language model-based text-to-SQL generation.

The primary aim of this survey is to offer a structured and accessible overview of the key developments, persistent challenges, and promising research directions in text-to-SQL generation with large language models. The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. We begin by formalizing the text-to-SQL task and establishing the foundational role of schema linking within the generation pipeline. We then present a detailed taxonomy of schema linking techniques, covering both retrieval-based and generation-based paradigms. Subsequently, we review the major benchmarks employed in the field and analyze their characteristics and limitations. Following this, we examine the robustness of current systems, discussing common failure modes and strategies for improving generalization. Finally, we conclude by identifying open problems and outlining directions for future research that can drive the development of more accurate, reliable, and practical text-to-SQL systems.

This highlights the ongoing need for methods that can balance comprehensiveness with precision in schema linking, as current systems still struggle to reliably isolate the most relevant schema components without introducing noise or missing critical relationships.

# 7 Future Directions

The primary limitation in current text-to-SQL systems centers on the persistent challenge of accurate schema linking. Despite the adoption of bidirectional linking strategies and the use of complete database schemas to maximize recall, existing frameworks still struggle to strike an optimal balance between comprehensiveness and precision. The reliance on full-schema preliminary query generation, while effective at reducing incomplete results, inevitably introduces noise by incorporating irrelevant tables and columns. This noise can propagate through subsequent processing stages, leading to syntactically correct but semantically misaligned SQL queries. Furthermore, current approaches largely assume static and well-structured database schemas, offering limited robustness when confronted with ambiguous natural language questions, highly normalized schemas with numerous relationships, or domain-specific terminology that diverges from the language model’s pretraining data. The absence of mechanisms to dynamically assess and filter schema relevance at a granular level remains a critical gap, as does the lack of interpretability in how linking decisions are made and refined throughout the generation pipeline.

Future research should pursue several complementary directions to address these shortcomings. A promising avenue involves developing multi-stage schema linking mechanisms that progressively refine the set of candidate schema elements. Rather than committing to a single bidirectional pass, future systems could employ iterative filtering that alternates between schema expansion and contraction, using intermediate query representations and confidence scores to prune irrelevant elements while recovering missed connections. This could be coupled with learned relevance estimators that predict the likelihood of each schema component being necessary for the final query, trained on execution feedback or human annotations. Another important direction is the integration of database value awareness directly into the linking process, enabling systems to recognize when natural language terms correspond to specific cell values rather than structural schema elements. Additionally, future work should explore schema linking in multi-turn and conversational settings, where user intent unfolds across multiple interactions and the relevant schema context shifts dynamically. Developing benchmarks and evaluation protocols that specifically measure the precision-recall trade-off in schema linking, separate from end-to-end query accuracy, would provide clearer signals for progress in this area.

Advancing schema linking capabilities carries substantial implications for the broader adoption and reliability of natural language interfaces to databases. More precise schema linking would directly reduce the error rate of generated SQL, particularly for complex queries involving multiple joins, nested subqueries, and aggregation operations where irrelevant schema inclusion often leads to catastrophic failures. This improvement would lower the barrier for non-expert users across domains such as healthcare analytics, business intelligence, and scientific data exploration, where incorrect query results can have significant consequences. Enhanced interpretability in the linking process would also foster greater user trust, as systems could transparently indicate which database elements were identified as relevant and why. From a research perspective, robust schema linking methods would enable text-to-SQL systems to scale to enterprise databases with hundreds of tables, a regime where current approaches degrade substantially. Ultimately, solving the precision-recall dilemma in schema linking would transform these systems from useful prototypes into dependable tools capable of handling the ambiguity and complexity inherent in real-world data querying scenarios.

# 8 Conclusion

This survey has examined the critical role of schema linking in text-to-SQL generation, highlighting it as a foundational yet persistently challenging component of natural language interfaces to databases. The analysis reveals that schema linking operates at the intersection of natural language understanding and database structural awareness, serving as the essential bridge that maps user questions to relevant tables, columns, and values. A central insight emerging from this review is the characterization of schema linking as a fundamental trade-off between positive gain and negative impact, where the inclusion of relevant schema elements must be carefully balanced against the introduction of noise from irrelevant components. The bidirectional schema linking approach, which first generates a preliminary SQL query using the complete database schema to achieve high recall before refining the alignment, represents a promising direction for managing this trade-off. Furthermore, the survey underscores that despite the transformative advances brought by large language models, schema linking remains a significant bottleneck that limits the reliability and accessibility of text-to-SQL systems for non-expert users.

The significance of this survey lies in its systematic examination of schema linking as a distinct and critical subproblem within the broader text-to-SQL landscape. By framing schema linking in terms of measurable trade-offs rather than treating it as a monolithic preprocessing step, this work provides researchers and practitioners with a structured lens through which to evaluate and design future solutions. The discussion situates schema linking within the evolving context of code generation and large language models, bridging classical supervised fine-tuning approaches with contemporary LLM-based methodologies. This perspective is particularly valuable as the field navigates the transition from traditional pipeline architectures to end-to-end neural systems, where the boundaries between schema linking and query generation are increasingly blurred. The survey also draws attention to the complementary roles of data augmentation and schema linking techniques, emphasizing that progress in one area can amplify or mitigate challenges in the other.

Moving forward, the text-to-SQL community must prioritize the development of schema linking methods that achieve robust precision without sacrificing recall, particularly in complex multi-table scenarios and domain-specific applications. Researchers are encouraged to explore hybrid approaches that combine the broad coverage of LLM-based preliminary query generation with targeted schema refinement mechanisms, potentially drawing inspiration from advances in retrieval-augmented generation and structured reasoning. Equally important is the establishment of standardized evaluation metrics that separately assess schema linking quality, enabling more granular diagnosis of system failures and facilitating targeted improvements. As natural language interfaces become increasingly central to data accessibility, solving the schema linking challenge will be instrumental in democratizing database querying and empowering users across technical and non-technical domains alike.
