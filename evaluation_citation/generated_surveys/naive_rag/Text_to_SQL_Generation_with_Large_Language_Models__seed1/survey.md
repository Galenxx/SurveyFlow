# 1 Abstract

Schema linking—the process of mapping natural language references to relevant tables, columns, and values within a database—remains a central challenge in text-to-SQL systems, even with recent advances driven by large language models. This survey examines the dual role of schema linking in modern text-to-SQL frameworks, focusing on approaches that aim to maximize its positive contributions while mitigating the noise introduced by irrelevant schema elements. We systematically review strategies for aligning user questions with database schemas, including bidirectional schema linking techniques, preliminary query generation using complete schemas, and complementary methods such as code language models and data augmentation. Our analysis reveals that achieving high recall through comprehensive schema coverage must be carefully balanced against the risk of incorporating extraneous information that degrades query accuracy. We further identify persistent open problems in determining which schema components are truly pertinent to a given natural language question, highlighting the unresolved tension between completeness and precision. This survey provides a structured overview of current schema linking methodologies and outlines key directions for future research in building more robust text-to-SQL systems.

# 2 Introduction

Text-to-SQL generation, the task of automatically translating natural language questions into executable SQL queries, has emerged as a pivotal technology for bridging the gap between non-expert users and relational databases. By enabling users to interact with data using everyday language, Text-to-SQL systems democratize access to structured information and reduce reliance on specialized programming skills. The rapid advancement of large language models has significantly transformed this field, pushing the boundaries of what automated query generation can achieve. These models, with their remarkable capacity for understanding natural language and generating code, have opened new frontiers in building more accurate and robust Text-to-SQL systems. However, the inherent complexity of mapping ambiguous human language to precise, structured database queries continues to pose substantial challenges, making Text-to-SQL a vibrant and evolving research area at the intersection of natural language processing, databases, and artificial intelligence.

Among the various technical obstacles in Text-to-SQL generation, schema linking stands out as a particularly critical and persistent bottleneck. Schema linking refers to the process of identifying and aligning the relevant database elements—such as tables, columns, and specific values—that are referenced or implied in a user’s natural language question. The difficulty lies in accurately determining which portions of a potentially large and complex database schema are pertinent to a given query while simultaneously ignoring irrelevant elements that could introduce noise and degrade performance. Even with the impressive capabilities of large language models, current systems struggle to strike an optimal balance: overly inclusive schema linking can overwhelm the model with extraneous information, while overly conservative linking risks missing essential database components, leading to incomplete or incorrect SQL generation. This fundamental tension between maximizing recall and maintaining precision remains largely unresolved, and the dual nature of schema linking—as both an enabler and a potential source of error—demands careful and systematic investigation.

This survey makes several key contributions to the understanding and advancement of Text-to-SQL generation with large language models. First, it provides a comprehensive and structured analysis of schema linking techniques, categorizing existing approaches based on their underlying strategies, such as bidirectional linking, in-context learning, and retrieval-augmented methods. By systematically examining how different frameworks handle the alignment between natural language and database schemas, we illuminate the trade-offs inherent in each approach and identify the conditions under which they succeed or fail. This analysis goes beyond mere description to offer critical insights into the mechanisms that drive effective schema linking and the common pitfalls that undermine it.

Second, this paper delivers an extensive review of benchmarks and evaluation methodologies used in the Text-to-SQL domain, with particular attention to their coverage of schema linking challenges and robustness testing. We examine widely adopted benchmarks such as Spider, WikiSQL, and BIRD, assessing their strengths and limitations in capturing real-world complexities, including domain diversity, query hardness, and schema variability. Furthermore, we highlight the growing importance of robustness as an evaluation dimension, discussing how current systems perform under distribution shifts, adversarial perturbations, and ambiguous natural language inputs. By synthesizing findings across multiple benchmarks and robustness studies, this survey provides a holistic view of the state of the field and pinpoints critical gaps that future research must address.

The primary aim of this survey is to offer researchers and practitioners a clear and thorough roadmap of Text-to-SQL generation with large language models, centered on the intertwined themes of schema linking, benchmarks, and robustness. The remainder of this paper is structured as follows. We begin by establishing foundational concepts and formally defining the Text-to-SQL task and the schema linking problem. We then present a detailed taxonomy and critical review of schema linking techniques, followed by an in-depth examination of major benchmarks and their evaluation protocols. Subsequent sections explore robustness challenges and the methods proposed to address them, before concluding with a discussion of open problems and promising directions for future research. Through this structure, we aim to equip readers with both a deep understanding of current capabilities and a clear vision for advancing the field.

# 7 Future Directions

The primary limitation in current text-to-SQL systems stems from the unresolved tension between comprehensive schema coverage and the noise introduced by irrelevant database elements. While bidirectional schema linking strategies have improved recall by considering the complete schema, they inevitably incorporate extraneous tables and columns that can mislead query generation models. This noise becomes particularly problematic in databases with hundreds of tables or complex relational structures, where the sheer volume of schema information overwhelms the model’s ability to discern truly relevant elements. Additionally, existing approaches struggle with ambiguous natural language references that could map to multiple schema components, and they lack robust mechanisms for handling domain-specific terminology that diverges from standard schema naming conventions. The current framework’s reliance on generating a preliminary query using the full schema, while effective for recall, introduces computational overhead and latency that may prove impractical for real-time applications or resource-constrained deployments.

Future research should explore adaptive schema linking mechanisms that dynamically adjust the scope of schema consideration based on query complexity and database characteristics. Rather than adopting a fixed strategy of using the complete schema or a heavily pruned subset, systems could learn to determine the optimal breadth of schema coverage for each individual query. This might involve developing lightweight classifiers that estimate query difficulty and schema relevance before committing to a particular linking strategy. Another promising direction involves integrating iterative refinement processes where an initial broad schema linking step identifies candidate elements, followed by targeted verification stages that filter out false positives using confidence scoring and cross-validation against query context. Furthermore, researchers should investigate the use of database value embeddings and semantic indexing techniques that allow systems to match natural language terms to schema elements even when naming conventions differ significantly, potentially through learned mappings between colloquial expressions and formal schema identifiers.

The proposed directions could fundamentally transform how text-to-SQL systems handle the schema linking bottleneck, enabling more reliable and efficient natural language interfaces to databases. Adaptive schema linking would reduce computational costs while maintaining or improving accuracy, making these systems viable for deployment in latency-sensitive environments such as interactive analytics dashboards and voice-activated database querying. Improved handling of ambiguous references and domain-specific terminology would broaden the accessibility of text-to-SQL systems to non-technical users across diverse industries, from healthcare to finance, where specialized vocabularies are common. By resolving the coverage-noise tradeoff, future systems could support more complex analytical queries spanning multiple tables without sacrificing precision, ultimately bringing the goal of seamless natural language database interaction closer to practical reality.

# 8 Conclusion

This survey has examined the critical role of schema linking in text-to-SQL systems, highlighting it as a persistent bottleneck that significantly affects the accuracy and reliability of natural language interfaces to relational databases. The analysis reveals that even with the substantial progress driven by large language models, the fundamental challenge of correctly aligning user questions with relevant database schemas and values remains unresolved. A central insight emerging from the reviewed literature is the inherent tension between comprehensive schema coverage and the introduction of noise from irrelevant elements. The bidirectional schema linking framework discussed throughout this survey represents a deliberate attempt to navigate this tension by first generating a preliminary SQL query using the complete database schema, thereby maximizing recall and minimizing the risk of incomplete results, while simultaneously seeking to mitigate the negative impact of extraneous schema information. This dual-objective strategy underscores a broader trend in the field: the shift from simplistic, one-directional linking methods toward more nuanced, iterative approaches that balance the benefits of broad coverage against the costs of increased complexity and potential confusion.

The significance of this survey lies in its focused synthesis of schema linking as a distinct and pivotal subproblem within the broader text-to-SQL research landscape. By consolidating discussions around bidirectional techniques, code language models, and data augmentation strategies, this work provides a structured understanding of how schema linking can be both an enabler and an impediment to query generation. It clarifies the mechanisms through which schema linking contributes to system performance and delineates the conditions under which it introduces errors, thereby offering researchers a clearer conceptual foundation for designing next-generation systems. Furthermore, the survey contextualizes schema linking within the evolving paradigm of treating text-to-SQL as a code generation task, connecting it to advancements in pretrained models and prompting strategies that extend beyond traditional database querying.

Moving forward, the research community must continue to address the unresolved tension between schema coverage and precision. Future efforts should prioritize the development of more adaptive schema linking mechanisms that can dynamically assess the relevance of schema elements in context, potentially through reinforcement learning or confidence-based filtering. Additionally, the creation of more diverse and challenging benchmarks that explicitly test schema linking under ambiguous, multi-turn, or cross-domain scenarios will be essential for driving meaningful progress. Ultimately, advancing schema linking is not merely a technical optimization but a necessary step toward making database interaction genuinely accessible to non-expert users, thereby unlocking the full potential of text-to-SQL systems in real-world applications.
