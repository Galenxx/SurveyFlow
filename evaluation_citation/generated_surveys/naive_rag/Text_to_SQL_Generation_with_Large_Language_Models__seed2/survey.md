# 1 Abstract

Schema linking is a fundamental challenge in text-to-SQL systems, requiring the accurate alignment of natural language questions with relevant database elements such as tables, columns, and values. This process directly determines how effectively a system can translate user intent into executable SQL queries, yet it remains a critical bottleneck despite recent advances driven by large language models. This survey provides a comprehensive examination of schema linking methodologies within text-to-SQL frameworks, exploring the two primary strategies employed in the field and reviewing how contemporary approaches leverage code language models and data augmentation techniques to enhance performance. We analyze a bidirectional schema linking framework that first generates a preliminary SQL query using the complete database schema to achieve high recall, then refines the alignment to minimize negative impacts from irrelevant schema elements. The survey further investigates the inherent tension between recall and precision in schema linking, highlighting the challenges posed by full-schema contexts and the ongoing need for robust strategies that balance comprehensive coverage with targeted relevance. Our analysis synthesizes current research directions and identifies open problems that continue to shape the evolution of schema linking in text-to-SQL systems.

# 2 Introduction

Text-to-SQL generation, the task of automatically translating natural language questions into executable SQL queries, has emerged as a transformative technology in the era of large language models. By enabling non-expert users to interact with relational databases without requiring specialized programming knowledge, text-to-SQL systems promise to democratize data access across industries ranging from healthcare and finance to education and business intelligence. The rapid advancement of large language models has dramatically reshaped this landscape, offering unprecedented capabilities in understanding natural language semantics and generating syntactically correct code. However, the core challenge of bridging the gap between ambiguous human language and the rigid structural requirements of database schemas remains a fundamental obstacle that continues to attract significant research attention.

Despite the impressive progress facilitated by large language models, text-to-SQL systems continue to grapple with several persistent problems. The most critical among these is schema linking—the process of accurately identifying which tables, columns, and database values are referenced in a natural language question. This task proves particularly challenging when dealing with complex database schemas containing hundreds of tables, ambiguous column names, or domain-specific terminology that may not align with the user's natural language expressions. Additionally, the robustness of text-to-SQL systems remains a significant concern, as models often exhibit brittleness when faced with paraphrased questions, unseen database structures, or queries requiring multi-step reasoning across related tables. The evaluation landscape further complicates progress, as existing benchmarks vary widely in their coverage of real-world scenarios, linguistic phenomena, and database complexities, making it difficult to assess true system capabilities and generalize findings across studies.

This survey makes several key contributions to the understanding and advancement of text-to-SQL generation with large language models. First, we provide a comprehensive and systematic analysis of schema linking techniques, categorizing existing approaches into distinct methodological families and examining their theoretical foundations, practical implementations, and empirical performance. Unlike prior surveys that treat schema linking as a peripheral concern, we position it as the central challenge around which successful text-to-SQL systems must be designed. Our analysis reveals the inherent trade-offs between recall and precision in schema linking strategies, the role of bidirectional linking mechanisms in reducing incomplete schema identification, and the impact of full-schema versus partial-schema contexts on downstream query generation accuracy.

Second, this paper offers a critical examination of existing benchmarks and evaluation methodologies, highlighting their strengths, limitations, and gaps in coverage. We analyze how different benchmark designs influence the perceived performance of text-to-SQL systems and identify scenarios where current evaluation protocols may fail to capture important dimensions of system capability, such as robustness to linguistic variation, adaptability to schema evolution, and performance under resource-constrained conditions. Furthermore, we synthesize findings across diverse studies to identify promising research directions, including the integration of schema linking with query generation in end-to-end architectures, the development of more realistic and challenging benchmarks, and techniques for improving model robustness through targeted data augmentation and training strategies. By connecting methodological innovations with evaluation practices, this survey aims to provide researchers and practitioners with a holistic understanding of the field's current state and future trajectory.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. We begin by establishing foundational concepts and formally defining the text-to-SQL task, including its key sub-components and the role of large language models in advancing the state of the art. We then present a detailed taxonomy and analysis of schema linking techniques, examining both traditional approaches and recent innovations that leverage the capabilities of large language models. Following this, we survey the landscape of benchmarks and evaluation methodologies, providing a comparative analysis of their characteristics and discussing their implications for measuring progress. We subsequently investigate the robustness challenges facing current text-to-SQL systems, exploring sources of brittleness and reviewing proposed mitigation strategies. Finally, we conclude by identifying open problems, synthesizing lessons learned, and outlining promising directions for future research that can drive the next generation of reliable and generalizable text-to-SQL systems.

# 7 Future Directions

While significant progress has been made in schema linking for text-to-SQL systems, several critical limitations persist. Current approaches often struggle to balance the trade-off between recall and precision when aligning natural language questions with database schemas. The use of complete database schemas to generate preliminary SQL queries can introduce noise and computational overhead, particularly in databases with hundreds of tables and thousands of columns. Moreover, existing methods frequently fail to handle ambiguous references, domain-specific terminology, and complex multi-hop relationships that span multiple tables. The reliance on bidirectional schema linking, while effective for achieving high recall, does not fully address scenarios where the initial preliminary query is fundamentally flawed or where the schema contains semantically overlapping elements. Additionally, current frameworks lack robust mechanisms for adapting to evolving database schemas and for providing interpretable explanations of their linking decisions, which limits their practical deployment in dynamic enterprise environments.

Future research should explore the integration of query-aware schema pruning techniques that dynamically reduce the search space before performing schema linking, thereby mitigating the noise introduced by full-schema contexts. One promising direction involves developing hierarchical linking strategies that first identify relevant tables at a coarse level and then progressively refine column-level and value-level alignments, which could significantly improve both efficiency and precision. Another avenue worth investigating is the incorporation of domain-adaptive pretraining and few-shot learning paradigms that enable schema linking modules to quickly adapt to new databases and specialized vocabularies without extensive retraining. Furthermore, researchers should investigate the use of graph neural networks and structured attention mechanisms to better capture the inherent relational structure of database schemas, allowing systems to reason about foreign key relationships and join paths during the linking process. The development of confidence-calibrated linking models that can explicitly signal uncertainty and request clarification when encountering ambiguous references would also represent a substantial step toward more reliable and user-trustworthy systems.

Advancing schema linking capabilities along these directions would have far-reaching implications for the accessibility and reliability of text-to-SQL systems. More precise and efficient linking mechanisms would enable non-expert users across healthcare, finance, and scientific research to interact with complex databases using natural language without requiring deep technical knowledge of the underlying schema. Improved handling of schema complexity and ambiguity would reduce the error rates in generated queries, fostering greater trust in automated data retrieval systems and accelerating data-driven decision-making processes. The development of interpretable and uncertainty-aware linking models could also facilitate human-in-the-loop workflows where domain experts can efficiently validate and correct system outputs, creating a virtuous cycle of continuous improvement. Ultimately, these advances would help bridge the gap between the expressive power of natural language and the structural rigor of relational databases, democratizing access to data and enabling a broader population to derive insights from structured information repositories.

# 8 Conclusion

In conclusion, this survey has examined the central role of schema linking in text-to-SQL systems, highlighting it as a critical bottleneck that directly determines the accuracy with which natural language questions are translated into executable database queries. The analysis reveals that despite significant advances driven by large language models, the challenge of correctly identifying relevant tables, columns, and database values remains a persistent obstacle. The reviewed work demonstrates that existing strategies can be broadly categorized into approaches that either prioritize high recall through comprehensive schema coverage or emphasize precision through selective alignment. A particularly promising direction involves bidirectional schema linking, which operates in both the forward direction—mapping question terms to schema elements—and the backward direction—verifying that identified schema components genuinely reflect user intent. Furthermore, the survey underscores the value of generating preliminary SQL queries using complete database schemas as an initial step, followed by refinement processes that iteratively narrow the focus to only those schema elements most pertinent to the query. This dual-phase methodology represents an important conceptual shift from treating schema linking as a standalone preprocessing task to viewing it as an integral, iterative component of the query generation pipeline.

The significance of this survey lies in its systematic synthesis of schema linking methodologies within the broader text-to-SQL landscape, offering researchers and practitioners a structured understanding of the trade-offs inherent in different alignment strategies. By framing schema linking as a problem of maximizing positive gain while minimizing negative impact, the survey provides a valuable conceptual lens through which to evaluate and compare existing approaches. This perspective is particularly timely given the rapid proliferation of large language models and their increasing application to database interaction tasks. The survey also highlights the interconnection between schema linking and related techniques such as data augmentation and code-oriented language model pretraining, thereby situating schema linking within a comprehensive ecosystem of mutually reinforcing methods. For practitioners building production text-to-SQL systems, the insights distilled here offer practical guidance on designing robust linking mechanisms that can handle the complexity and ambiguity of real-world database schemas.

Moving forward, the field must continue to address the fundamental tension between recall and precision in schema linking, particularly as databases grow in scale and complexity. Future research should explore more sophisticated bidirectional linking mechanisms that can dynamically adapt to varying query types and schema structures, potentially leveraging advances in attention mechanisms and graph-based reasoning. Equally important is the development of standardized evaluation benchmarks that specifically isolate and measure schema linking performance, enabling more targeted progress in this critical sub-task. The research community is encouraged to investigate hybrid approaches that combine the complementary strengths of retrieval-based and generation-based methods, as well as techniques that incorporate user feedback to iteratively refine schema alignment. Ultimately, solving the schema linking challenge will be essential to realizing the full potential of text-to-SQL systems as truly accessible interfaces for non-expert database users, democratizing data access across domains and skill levels.
