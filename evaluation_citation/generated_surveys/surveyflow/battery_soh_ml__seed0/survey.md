# 1 Abstract

The accurate estimation of battery state of health and remaining useful life is essential for ensuring the safety, reliability, and economic viability of lithium-ion batteries in both first- and second-life applications, yet the complex, coupled degradation mechanisms under varying usage conditions pose significant modeling challenges. This survey systematically reviews recent advances in data-driven and hybrid modeling approaches that bridge physics-based electrochemical-thermal-degradation models with machine learning techniques for battery health estimation. Key contributions include the analysis of Gaussian process regression frameworks enabling population-level generalization across cells, hybrid error-correction strategies that enhance the flexibility of first-principles models across full lifespan discharge dynamics, and reinforced graph-based physics-informed neural networks that integrate spatio-temporal learning with adaptive weight modulation to track degradation trajectories with quantified uncertainty. The survey further highlights the critical role of comprehensive, physically consistent synthetic datasets in training and validating such models under diverse cycling and calendar ageing conditions, while identifying the interplay between usage parameters and coupled degradation mechanisms as a key factor influencing model generalizability. These developments collectively advance the capability to predict battery degradation with improved interpretability, robustness, and uncertainty awareness, supporting efficient screening and management strategies for both new and retired cells.


# 2 Introduction

The rapid proliferation of electric vehicles, portable consumer electronics, and grid-scale energy storage systems has established lithium-ion batteries as a cornerstone of modern energy infrastructure. As these electrochemical storage devices age through repeated charge–discharge cycles and calendar time, their performance gradually deteriorates, manifesting as capacity fade and internal resistance growth. This degradation directly compromises system reliability, operational safety, and economic returns, making accurate assessment of battery health an indispensable component of contemporary battery management strategies. The ability to reliably estimate the state of health and predict the remaining useful life of lithium-ion cells is essential not only for ensuring safe operation during first-life applications but also for enabling the emerging practice of repurposing retired batteries in less demanding second-life roles, where economic viability hinges on rapid and trustworthy health screening. These imperatives have driven intensive research efforts spanning multiple modeling paradigms, from traditional physics-based electrochemical models to modern data-driven machine learning techniques, each offering distinct advantages and facing characteristic limitations in capturing the complex, coupled degradation phenomena that govern battery ageing.

Despite considerable progress in both physics-based and data-driven approaches, several fundamental challenges continue to impede the development of robust and generalizable battery health estimation frameworks. A central difficulty lies in the inherent complexity of lithium-ion battery degradation, which involves multiple interacting mechanisms—including solid-electrolyte interphase growth, lithium plating, active material loss, and electrolyte decomposition—that evolve differently depending on usage conditions such as temperature, C-rate, depth-of-discharge, and rest state-of-charge. Purely data-driven models, while capable of learning degradation patterns from operational data without explicit physical equations, often function as black boxes that lack interpretability and may produce unreliable extrapolations when confronted with operating regimes outside their training distribution. Conversely, physics-based electrochemical-thermal-degradation models offer mechanistic insight but require extensive parameterization and frequently struggle to generalize across the diverse usage histories encountered in real-world applications. The transferability of predictive models across different cells, chemistries, and ageing stages remains severely limited, and the integration of physical knowledge with data-driven learning continues to present open methodological questions. Furthermore, the scarcity of comprehensive, high-quality degradation datasets that span wide operating envelopes and explicitly capture coupled degradation modes constrains both model development and rigorous benchmarking.

This survey makes three principal contributions to the field of battery health estimation and lifetime prediction. First, it delivers a structured and comprehensive review of machine learning techniques applied to lithium-ion battery state-of-health estimation and remaining-useful-life prediction, systematically categorizing approaches into purely data-driven methods and emerging hybrid strategies that incorporate physics-based modeling. The survey examines the strengths and limitations of Gaussian process regression frameworks, neural network architectures, and reinforcement learning-based methods, with particular emphasis on their capacity to address the challenges of degradation modeling under diverse operating conditions. By analyzing how different paradigms handle population-level generalization, uncertainty quantification, and extrapolation beyond training data, the paper provides a clear comparative perspective that illuminates the relative merits of each approach for specific application scenarios, including both first-life monitoring and second-life screening.

Second, this survey synthesizes recent advances at the intersection of physics-based degradation modeling and data-driven learning, highlighting how hybrid error-correction frameworks and physics-informed neural architectures bridge the longstanding gap between mechanistic interpretability and empirical flexibility. The paper examines innovative approaches such as reinforced graph-based spatio-temporal learning with adaptive weight modulation, which demonstrates the capacity to track complex degradation trajectories while maintaining physical consistency. It further analyzes how high-fidelity simulated datasets generated from electrochemical-thermal-degradation models can serve as foundational resources for training and validating hybrid models across wide operating regimes. By mapping the connections between mechanistic parameterization, data-driven error correction, cross-cell generalization, and learning-based trajectory tracking, the survey clarifies how each methodological strand addresses specific limitations of the others and contributes to a more integrated modeling paradigm.

The aim of this survey is to equip researchers and practitioners with a clear, organized overview of the current landscape of machine learning for battery health estimation while identifying open challenges and promising directions for future investigation. The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 introduces the fundamental concepts of lithium-ion battery degradation and formally defines the state-of-health and remaining-useful-life estimation problems. Section 3 reviews purely data-driven machine learning approaches, including Gaussian process regression and neural network-based methods. Section 4 discusses physics-based electrochemical-thermal-degradation models and their role in generating mechanistic degradation insights. Section 5 examines hybrid and physics-informed frameworks that combine physical knowledge with data-driven learning. Section 6 analyzes available datasets and evaluation protocols used in the literature. Section 7 identifies key challenges and outlines future research directions, and Section 8 concludes the survey.

## 3.1 Data-Driven Machine Learning for Battery Cycle Life Prediction

Data-driven machine learning (ML) approaches have gained increasing attention for battery state and health estimation (Zhang et al., 2022). Liu and Gao (2025) note that ML modeling has been applied successfully in this domain, and they emphasize that the first step of any battery modeling effort should be defining the application precisely. Building on this, they outline a tutorial for interpretable and accurate data-driven cycle life prediction, though the specific modeling architectures and results are described in subsequent sections of their work.

## 3.2 Gaussian Process Regression for SOH and RUL Estimation

Gaussian process regression (GPR) has been recognized as a prominent machine learning technique for state-of-health estimation. Zhang et al. (2022) note that GPs represent a key class of regressors in this domain and reference prior work by Li et al. (2020) that combined incremental capacity analysis with GPR for SOH estimation. In that prior study, single-cell estimation was performed using the first 55% of the cycles as training data to predict subsequent degradation. However, Zhang et al. (2022) shift the focus toward a different scenario, indicating that their own work pursues an alternative estimation setting. The available evidence does not provide further details on GPR-based RUL estimation or additional GPR implementations beyond this referenced work.

## 3.3 Pulse Test Data Prediction and Generalization for Battery Reuse

The reuse of retired batteries depends on the ability to predict discharge dynamics from pristine conditions through end-of-life, and hybrid modeling strategies have been proposed to improve the generalizability of such predictions. Liu and Gao (2025) describe an approach in which a data-driven model learns the prediction error of a previously trained first-principles model, thereby increasing the flexibility and prediction accuracy of the physics-based component while enhancing overall generalizability. This hybrid error-correction framework supports the transfer of discharge behavior predictions across a battery’s full lifespan, which is essential when pulse test data from early life must be extrapolated to later degradation states.

Generalization across individual cells is addressed by regression methods that map early cycling data to state-of-health and remaining-useful-life estimates for previously unseen batteries. Zhang et al. (2022) introduce a Gaussian process regression network (GPRn) that combines single training cell Gaussian processes and is designed to estimate state of health from one charging cycle of a new cell, given cycling data from beginning-of-life to end-of-life for multiple similar cells. By leveraging population-level degradation patterns, such methods reduce the need for extensive pulse characterization of each candidate cell, supporting efficient screening for second-life applications.

# 4 Physics-Informed Neural Networks for Remaining Useful Life and Degradation Modeling

## 4.1 Electrochemical-Thermal-Degradation Modeling for SOH and RUL Prediction

Physics-based electrochemical-thermal-degradation models offer a route to predict state-of-health (SOH) and remaining useful life (RUL) by capturing the coupled degradation mechanisms that govern battery ageing. Madabattula (2026) developed a consistent parameterisation of such a model to link calendar and cycle ageing, arguing that a unified physics-based framework providing insights into degradation modes across coupled usage conditions was not previously available. The study examined variations in rest state-of-charge (10%, 60%, 100%), C-rate (0.1C, 0.3C, 1C), depth-of-discharge (50%, 70%, 90%), and temperature (10 °C, 25 °C, 40 °C), and found that the model predicted sub-linear degradation trajectories under gentle operation, linear trajectories under moderate conditions, and contributions from both positive and negative electrodes to the total capacity loss (Madabattula, 2026). These insights into the coupled effects of usage conditions on degradation mechanisms can support model validation across different operating modes and improve cell-level consistency checks for SOH and RUL prediction.

Complementary data-driven approaches incorporate degradation trajectory information to enhance predictive accuracy. Pour et al. (2025) employed a reinforced graph-based physics-informed neural network with dynamic weights, demonstrating that the model could consistently follow the overall SOH degradation trend across multiple battery batches, with uncertainty quantification that narrowed toward end-of-life. The combination of physics-based degradation parameterisation and learning-based trajectory tracking illustrates the growing emphasis on models that remain faithful to the underlying electrochemical-thermal ageing behaviour while delivering practical SOH and RUL estimates.

## 4.2 Reinforced Graph-Based Physics-Informed Neural Networks for SOH Estimation

Pour et al. (2025) introduced the Reinforced Graph-based Physics-informed neural networks enhanced with Dynamic weights (RGPD) framework to address the challenge of capturing complex degradation interactions in battery state-of-health (SOH) estimation. The architecture integrates graph-based spatio-temporal learning to model degradation dynamics, employs soft actor-critic (SAC) to adaptively modulate hidden representations before temporal attention, and leverages Q-learning as part of its reinforcement mechanism (Pour et al., 2025). A key motivation for such physics-informed data-driven approaches is the availability of high-fidelity simulated degradation data. Madabattula (2026) generated a comprehensive dataset from an electrochemical-thermal-degradation model, covering 81 cyclic ageing use cases, 9 calendar ageing cases, and 3 standard cycling conditions, with the explicit purpose of training physics-informed data-driven models (Madabattula, 2026). This dataset, which links usage conditions to coupled degradation mechanisms, provides the kind of physically consistent degradation trajectories that can validate models under different operating modes and support the development of frameworks like RGPD (Madabattula, 2026).

# 5 Future Directions

Current efforts in battery state-of-health estimation and remaining-useful-life prediction remain constrained by a persistent disconnect between physics-based electrochemical-thermal-degradation models and data-driven learning approaches. Physics-based frameworks capture coupled degradation mechanisms across diverse usage conditions but demand extensive parameterization and exhibit limited generalizability when operating regimes deviate from calibration data. Data-driven methods, including Gaussian process regression networks and reinforced graph-based neural architectures, achieve strong predictive accuracy on specific cell populations yet rely heavily on large, high-quality training datasets and offer limited physical interpretability, which restricts their deployment in safety-critical applications. A critical gap exists in unifying these paradigms so that degradation trajectories remain physically consistent while adapting to cell-to-cell variability and previously unseen usage profiles. Furthermore, most existing methods focus on single-cell or single-batch estimation, leaving unresolved the challenge of transferring predictions across heterogeneous cell chemistries, form factors, and second-life application scenarios without extensive retraining or requalification testing. The absence of standardized benchmarks spanning multiple degradation modes and operating envelopes further impedes rigorous comparison and validation of emerging hybrid frameworks.

Future research should prioritize the development of tightly coupled hybrid frameworks that embed electrochemical-thermal-degradation constraints directly into the learning objectives of data-driven models. One promising direction involves designing physics-informed neural architectures that consume comprehensive simulated degradation datasets spanning wide operating envelopes during pretraining, then adapt to field data from target cells through transfer learning or meta-learning strategies. Such frameworks would enforce adherence to known degradation kinetics while learning residual discrepancies attributable to manufacturing variability or unmodeled ageing phenomena, thereby maintaining physical consistency across a battery’s entire lifespan. A second direction concerns population-level generalization through hierarchical probabilistic models that share statistical strength across cells while preserving individual degradation signatures. Extending Gaussian process regression networks to incorporate physics-based mean functions and structured multi-output kernels could enable simultaneous state-of-health and remaining-useful-life prediction for entire battery packs, explicitly accounting for cell-to-cell interactions and thermal gradients. Additionally, research should explore active learning and experimental design methods to identify minimal diagnostic protocols—such as targeted pulse sequences or partial charge curves—required to calibrate hybrid models for new cells, thereby reducing characterization costs in second-life screening pipelines. The co-design of synthetic degradation datasets with the architectures intended to consume them represents another essential avenue, ensuring that training data capture the full complexity of coupled ageing mechanisms under realistic usage variability.

Advancing these research directions would substantially impact the economic viability and safety of battery reuse ecosystems. Physics-informed hybrid models capable of generalizing across chemistries and usage histories would lower the barrier to qualifying retired electric vehicle batteries for grid storage applications, reducing the need for costly full-cycle testing and accelerating deployment timelines. Improved population-level estimation would enable fleet operators and aggregators to make informed decisions about when to retire, repurpose, or replace individual modules based on probabilistic remaining-life forecasts rather than conservative fixed thresholds. Ultimately, tighter integration of degradation physics with adaptive data-driven learning promises more trustworthy state-of-health estimates that maintain accuracy from pristine conditions through end-of-life, supporting circular economy goals and enabling robust, scalable management strategies for both first- and second-life battery systems.


# 6 Conclusion

This survey has systematically examined the converging trajectories of data-driven and hybrid modeling approaches for battery state-of-health and remaining-useful-life estimation, revealing a field in transition from isolated methodological developments toward integrated frameworks. The analysis has shown that Gaussian process regression methods, particularly when structured as networks that leverage population-level degradation patterns, offer effective generalization across individual cells and reduce the characterization burden for second-life screening. Physics-based electrochemical-thermal-degradation models provide mechanistic interpretability by capturing the coupled effects of rest state-of-charge, C-rate, depth-of-discharge, and temperature on degradation trajectories, generating physically consistent insights that purely data-driven methods cannot independently guarantee. Hybrid error-correction strategies that embed data-driven learning within first-principles models enhance predictive flexibility across a battery’s full lifespan, while reinforced graph-based physics-informed neural networks with dynamic weight modulation demonstrate the capacity to track complex degradation dynamics with calibrated uncertainty quantification. Underpinning these advances is the growing availability of high-fidelity simulated degradation datasets that span wide operating envelopes and explicitly link usage conditions to coupled degradation modes, enabling the training and validation of models under diverse and extrapolative scenarios.

The significance of this survey lies in its synthesis of two paradigms that have often progressed along parallel tracks: physics-based degradation modeling and data-driven health estimation. By mapping the interconnections between mechanistic parameterization, hybrid error correction, cross-cell generalization, and learning-based trajectory tracking, this review clarifies how each methodological strand addresses specific limitations inherent in the others. It demonstrates that first-principles understanding of coupled degradation mechanisms serves not as an alternative to machine learning but as a foundational resource for generating physically consistent training data, constraining model predictions, and validating estimates under conditions that deviate from observed training distributions. The survey further underscores the practical importance of these integrated approaches for emerging battery reuse ecosystems, where rapid and reliable estimation from limited early-life data is essential for economic viability and safety assurance.

The path forward demands a deliberate convergence of physics-based simulation pipelines with data-driven model development cycles, such that degradation datasets and learning architectures are co-designed rather than sequentially developed. Researchers should prioritize the establishment of standardized benchmarking protocols that span multiple battery chemistries, form factors, and usage histories, enabling rigorous assessment of the true generalizability claimed by hybrid frameworks. Equally critical is the continued advancement of uncertainty quantification methods that maintain reliability under the distribution shifts inevitably encountered in real-world deployment. Progress along these directions will accelerate the translation of laboratory-validated estimation techniques into robust, field-ready tools that support safer battery operation, extended service lifetimes, and the efficient repurposing of retired cells, thereby contributing to the broader goals of energy sustainability and circular economy in electrochemical energy storage systems.
