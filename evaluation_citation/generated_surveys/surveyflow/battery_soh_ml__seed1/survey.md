# 1 Abstract

The accurate estimation of battery state of health and prediction of cycle life are critical for ensuring the safety, reliability, and economic viability of lithium-ion battery systems across applications ranging from consumer electronics to grid storage. This survey provides a comprehensive review of data-driven and hybrid modeling approaches for battery cycle life prediction, capacity fade forecasting, and remaining useful life estimation. We examine the application of Gaussian process regression frameworks for both state-of-health estimation of unseen cells and probabilistic capacity fade forecasting, highlighting methods that leverage single-cell aging models and cycling data from beginning-of-life to end-of-life. The survey further explores the integration of physics-informed neural networks with reinforcement learning mechanisms to capture complex degradation interactions, as well as hybrid strategies that combine first-principles electrochemical-thermal-degradation models with data-driven error correction to enhance prediction accuracy and generalizability. Key contributions include a structured analysis of how operating conditions influence degradation trajectory shapes, the role of monotonicity and smoothness priors in enforcing physical consistency, and the emerging use of simulated degradation datasets for training and validating physics-informed models. This review synthesizes recent advances to offer a consolidated perspective on the state-of-the-art methodologies and their implications for robust battery life prediction.


# 2 Introduction

The rapid proliferation of lithium-ion battery technology across transportation, consumer electronics, and grid-scale energy storage has established these electrochemical systems as indispensable components of modern infrastructure. As batteries undergo repeated charge and discharge cycles, their performance gradually deteriorates through a confluence of electrochemical, thermal, and mechanical degradation mechanisms, manifesting as capacity fade and internal resistance growth. The ability to accurately assess battery state of health and predict remaining useful life has therefore become paramount for ensuring operational safety, optimizing maintenance strategies, enabling second-life applications, and supporting the economic viability of battery-dependent systems. Traditional physics-based models, while grounded in first-principles understanding of degradation phenomena, often struggle to capture the full complexity and variability of real-world aging behavior across diverse operating conditions. The emergence of large-scale battery cycling datasets, coupled with significant advances in computational capabilities, has catalyzed a paradigm shift toward data-driven methodologies. Machine learning techniques now offer unprecedented opportunities to model the nonlinear, time-varying, and condition-dependent nature of battery degradation, promising more accurate and scalable prognostic capabilities than conventional approaches alone can provide.

Despite the considerable progress achieved through machine learning applications in this domain, several fundamental challenges continue to impede the development of truly robust and trustworthy battery health prognostics. Battery degradation trajectories are shaped by intricate interactions among multiple stress factors, including temperature, charging and discharging rates, depth of discharge, and rest periods, resulting in highly condition-specific aging patterns that defy simple generalization. Data-driven models trained on limited operating regimes frequently exhibit degraded performance when confronted with unseen usage conditions, raising serious concerns about their reliability in real-world deployments where operational diversity is the norm rather than the exception. Moreover, purely data-driven approaches lack inherent physical constraints and may generate predictions that violate established electrochemical principles, such as non-monotonic capacity fade profiles or spurious oscillations in health indicators, thereby undermining their credibility for safety-critical decision-making. An additional persistent difficulty lies in extracting meaningful degradation signatures from early-life cycling data, where the subtle onset of capacity fade is easily masked by measurement noise and cell-to-cell manufacturing variability. These interconnected challenges underscore the pressing need for methodologies that not only harness the flexibility and scalability of machine learning but also integrate physical knowledge, enforce degradation consistency, and provide principled uncertainty quantification.

This survey makes two principal contributions to the field of machine learning for lithium-ion battery state-of-health estimation and remaining-useful-life prediction. First, it delivers a comprehensive and structured examination of the predominant data-driven methodologies that have been developed for these prognostic tasks, encompassing Gaussian process regression frameworks, deep learning architectures, and hybrid physics-informed neural networks. For each methodological category, the survey systematically analyzes the underlying modeling principles, the types of input features and health indicators employed, and the strategies devised to accommodate battery-to-battery variability and diverse operational conditions. Particular emphasis is placed on approaches that explicitly address the critical challenge of generalization across different aging regimes and that incorporate probabilistic outputs to characterize prediction uncertainty, thereby enabling more informed decision-making in practical battery management systems.

Second, this paper provides a critical analysis of emerging research directions that seek to bridge the persistent gap between purely data-driven and physics-based modeling paradigms. It investigates how physics-informed machine learning frameworks enforce degradation priors such as monotonicity and smoothness to ensure physical consistency, how reinforcement learning mechanisms can adaptively modulate model representations in response to evolving degradation dynamics, and how simulated degradation datasets derived from electrochemical-thermal models can be strategically leveraged for training and validating predictive algorithms. The survey further examines the role of pulse test data and incremental capacity analysis in extracting health-relevant features from operational measurements, as well as the specific challenges associated with early-life cycle life prediction where degradation signals remain subtle. By synthesizing findings from recent studies, identifying methodological trade-offs, and delineating open research questions, this work seeks to provide a consolidated perspective that can guide future efforts toward more robust, interpretable, and physically consistent battery health prognostics.

The remainder of this survey is organized to provide a systematic exploration of the field. The following section establishes foundational concepts of battery degradation and defines the key metrics employed for state-of-health estimation and remaining-useful-life prediction. Subsequent sections present detailed reviews of Gaussian process-based methods, deep learning architectures, and physics-informed approaches, critically examining their respective strengths, limitations, and domains of applicability. The survey then addresses practical considerations including data requirements, feature engineering techniques, and evaluation protocols commonly adopted in the literature. The paper concludes by synthesizing the current state of the field and outlining promising directions for future research, with particular emphasis on improving model generalizability across chemistries and operating conditions, incorporating adaptive physical constraints, and enabling reliable prognostic capabilities from early-life data to support the emerging circular battery economy.

## 3.1 Data-Driven Cycle Life Prediction for Battery State and Health Estimation


Data-driven approaches, particularly those grounded in machine learning (ML), have gained increasing attention for battery state estimation tasks (Zhang et al., 2022). As Liu and Gao (2025) note, such modeling efforts should ideally begin with a precise definition of the target application. Within this domain, a central challenge is the accurate prediction of cycle life, which serves as a critical indicator of battery health and remaining useful life. The successful application of ML techniques to broader state-of-health (SOH) estimation problems provides a foundation for extending these methods to the specific task of cycle life forecasting (Liu & Gao, 2025).

## 3.2 Gaussian Process Regression for Unseen Battery Cell SOH Estimation

A Gaussian process regression approach tailored for state-of-health estimation of previously unseen battery cells was introduced by Zhang et al. (2022). Their method, termed GPRn, combines Gaussian process models trained on individual cells to map a single charging cycle of an unseen cell to its SOH and remaining useful life, leveraging cycling data from beginning-of-life to end-of-life of multiple similar cells. The work further includes a comparison of GPRn performance against other standard machine learning techniques (Zhang et al., 2022).

## 3.3 Forecasting Capacity Fade and RUL with Gaussian Process Regression


Gaussian process regression (GPR) has been applied as a probabilistic regression method for forecasting capacity fade and remaining useful life (RUL) in lithium-ion batteries. Zhang et al. (2022) note that Pang et al. (2021) use GPR as the regression method for RUL estimation, training single-cell aging models on cycles from beginning-of-life to a specified capacity threshold. The same source observes that alternative data-driven approaches, such as bidirectional gated recurrent units, have also been employed for RUL estimation using incremental capacity analysis features (Zhang et al., 2022). In a broader context of hybrid modeling, Liu and Gao (2025) describe a strategy in which a data-driven model learns the prediction error of a previously trained first-principles model, thereby increasing the flexibility and prediction accuracy of the physics-based model while improving generalizability. Although this hybrid error-correction framework is discussed in relation to predicting battery discharge dynamics from pristine to end-of-life conditions, it illustrates a complementary paradigm to standalone GPR-based forecasting.

# 4 Physics-Informed Neural Networks with Electrochemical-Degradation Models for RUL

## 4.1 Electrochemical-Thermal-Degradation Model Parameterization for RUL

Accurate parameterization of electrochemical-thermal-degradation models is essential for producing physically plausible degradation trajectories that underpin reliable remaining useful life (RUL) predictions. Madabattula (2026) emphasizes that coupled degradation mechanisms in lithium-ion batteries defy simple prediction, much like complex, interdependent social behaviors, and demonstrates that the model’s predicted degradation trajectories vary systematically with operating conditions—exhibiting sub-linear trends under gentle use and linear trends under moderate conditions—when parameterized across rest states of charge (10%, 60%, 100%), C-rates (0.1C, 0.3C, 1C), depths of discharge (50%, 70%, 90%), and temperatures (10°C, 25°C, 40°C). Such condition-dependent trajectory shapes directly inform RUL estimation by constraining how capacity fade evolves over time.

The physical consistency of these parameterized trajectories can be further enforced through degradation priors that align with expected battery behavior. Pour et al. (2025) note that health indicators should deteriorate monotonically with usage, degradation trajectories must avoid unrealistic abrupt oscillations, and predicted RUL should approach zero at the end-of-life condition. Incorporating monotonicity and smoothness as general degradation priors during model training helps ensure that the electrochemical-thermal-degradation model’s outputs remain physically grounded, thereby improving the trustworthiness of RUL forecasts derived from the parameterized model.

## 4.2 Reinforced Graph-Based Physics-Informed Neural Network for SoH Estimation

**4.2 Reinforced Graph-Based Physics-Informed Neural Network for SoH Estimation**

Pour et al. (2025) introduced the Reinforced Graph-based Physics-informed neural networks enhanced with Dynamic weights (RGPD) framework to address the challenge of capturing complex degradation interactions in battery state-of-health estimation. The framework integrates graph-based spatio-temporal learning to model degradation dynamics, employs soft actor-critic (SAC) to adaptively modulate hidden representations before temporal attention, and incorporates Q-learning as a reinforcement mechanism. A key motivation for physics-informed approaches in this domain is the availability of simulated degradation data grounded in electrochemical-thermal-degradation models. Madabattula (2026) generated and made publicly available simulated SoH and degradation mode analysis data covering 81 cyclic ageing use cases, 9 calendar ageing cases, and 3 standard cycling conditions, specifically intended for training physics-informed data-driven models. This type of dataset provides the degradation trajectories and coupled degradation mechanism insights that can support the validation and consistency checking of models like RGPD under diverse operational modes (Madabattula, 2026).

# 5 Machine Learning for Sustainable Battery Remanufacturing and Recycling

## 5.1 Charge Status and Residual Value Prediction for Battery Remanufacturing

The work of Tao (2024) addresses the prediction of charge status and residual value for battery remanufacturing through the use of pulse test data. The study outlines a framework where charge status prediction from pulse tests is examined, followed by residual value prediction derived from the same test methodology. The research further includes an analysis of generalization risk, which evaluates the robustness and applicability of the predictive models across different scenarios.

# 6 Future Directions

The accurate prediction of battery cycle life and state of health remains constrained by several fundamental limitations that hinder the deployment of data-driven models in real-world applications. Current machine learning approaches, including Gaussian process regression and deep learning architectures, often rely on single-cell aging patterns and struggle to generalize across diverse chemistries, operating conditions, and usage histories without extensive retraining. Physics-informed neural networks, while offering improved physical consistency, are limited by the fidelity and breadth of simulated degradation data, and their ability to capture coupled degradation mechanisms under extreme or rare operating scenarios remains unproven. The enforcement of degradation priors such as monotonicity and smoothness is typically implemented through static constraints that do not adapt to the evolving nature of electrochemical aging processes. Furthermore, the validation of predictive models across the complete battery lifecycle, from pristine cells to remanufacturing stages, remains underexplored, leaving critical gaps in understanding model robustness for second-life applications and non-standard usage patterns. The lack of standardized benchmark suites that combine experimental and simulated degradation data further complicates fair comparisons across methodologies and impedes the identification of truly generalizable approaches.

Future research should prioritize the development of hybrid modeling frameworks that seamlessly fuse data-driven learning with multi-physics degradation models, enabling more accurate cycle life forecasts under previously unseen conditions. A particularly promising direction involves designing adaptive physics-informed architectures that dynamically adjust the influence of physical constraints based on the operational regime and the degree of model confidence, rather than applying uniform priors throughout the prediction horizon. Multi-task learning paradigms that jointly predict capacity fade, internal resistance growth, and degradation mode evolution could capture the coupled nature of aging mechanisms more faithfully than single-output models, providing a more holistic view of battery health. To address data scarcity and improve generalizability, federated learning strategies across battery testing facilities and real-world fleet deployments should be explored, allowing models to learn from distributed, heterogeneous datasets without compromising data privacy. The integration of online learning capabilities represents another critical frontier, enabling models to update their predictions continuously as new operational data become available. Extending predictive frameworks to encompass the entire battery value chain, including rapid state assessment from pulse tests and residual value estimation for remanufacturing, would bridge the gap between laboratory-based aging studies and practical circular economy applications. Additionally, closer collaboration between the battery modeling and machine learning communities is essential to establish physically meaningful evaluation metrics that go beyond root-mean-square error and capture the practical reliability of state-of-health and cycle life forecasts in real-world battery management systems.

The proposed directions hold significant potential to transform battery management and decision-making across multiple industries. More robust and generalizable cycle life predictions would enable proactive maintenance scheduling, reduce unexpected downtime in grid storage and electric vehicle fleets, and optimize warranty and leasing models through improved risk assessment. By incorporating remanufacturing-oriented predictions, the research could accelerate the adoption of second-life battery applications, directly supporting sustainability goals and reducing raw material demand. Adaptive physics-informed models would enhance safety by providing earlier and more reliable warnings of accelerated degradation or failure onset. The development of standardized benchmark suites would accelerate methodological progress by enabling rigorous and reproducible comparisons, while federated learning approaches would unlock the value of distributed data sources without compromising proprietary information. Collectively, these advances would lower the total cost of ownership for battery systems, strengthen confidence in long-term energy storage investments, and facilitate a more resilient and circular battery economy that aligns with global sustainability objectives.


# 7 Conclusion

This survey has systematically examined the landscape of data-driven and hybrid modeling approaches for lithium-ion battery cycle life prediction, capacity fade forecasting, and remaining useful life estimation. The main findings reveal a clear trajectory from standalone machine learning methods, such as Gaussian process regression for probabilistic state-of-health estimation, toward physics-informed neural network architectures that embed electrochemical degradation knowledge directly into the learning process. Gaussian process frameworks offer principled uncertainty quantification and can generalize across cells by leveraging end-of-life cycling data, yet their dependence on single-cell aging models and fixed capacity thresholds constrains adaptability to diverse degradation trajectories. Physics-informed neural networks, particularly those enhanced with graph-based spatio-temporal learning and reinforcement mechanisms, address these limitations by enforcing monotonicity and smoothness priors, thereby ensuring physically consistent predictions even under previously unseen operating conditions. The integration of simulated electrochemical-thermal-degradation data, which captures coupled degradation mechanisms across varied temperatures, C-rates, and depths of discharge, provides a critical bridge between first-principles understanding and data-driven flexibility. Furthermore, hybrid error-correction strategies, where data-driven models learn the residuals of physics-based predictions, demonstrate a promising avenue for improving generalizability without sacrificing physical interpretability.

The significance of this survey lies in its structured synthesis of two rapidly evolving paradigms that are frequently treated in isolation within the battery prognostics literature. By juxtaposing purely data-driven regressors with physics-informed architectures, the survey clarifies the fundamental trade-offs between model complexity, data requirements, physical consistency, and predictive accuracy. It highlights how the availability of publicly accessible simulated degradation datasets enables rigorous benchmarking and validation of state-of-health estimation models across diverse cycling and calendar aging scenarios, fostering reproducibility and accelerating methodological progress. The discussion of reinforcement learning as a dynamic weight modulation mechanism within graph-based networks underscores the potential for autonomous adaptation to varying degradation modes, a capability that traditional regression models inherently lack. This synthesis provides researchers and practitioners with a conceptual roadmap for selecting and designing estimation frameworks tailored to the specific constraints of their application, whether the priority is uncertainty quantification, physical plausibility, or scalability across battery chemistries and usage profiles.

Looking forward, the field must prioritize the development of standardized benchmark suites that integrate experimental and simulated degradation data to enable fair and transparent comparisons across methodologies. The integration of multi-physics degradation models with online learning capabilities represents a critical frontier, allowing predictive models to continuously update their forecasts as new operational data become available during real-world deployment. Greater emphasis should be placed on generalization across battery chemistries, form factors, and aging conditions, moving decisively beyond cell-specific training paradigms that limit practical applicability. Finally, closer collaboration between the battery modeling and machine learning communities is essential to establish physically meaningful evaluation metrics that extend beyond conventional error statistics, capturing the practical reliability and safety implications of state-of-health and cycle life forecasts in operational battery management systems. By pursuing these directions, the research community can accelerate the transition toward robust, interpretable, and trustworthy battery health prognostics that support the safety, sustainability, and economic viability of next-generation energy storage systems.
