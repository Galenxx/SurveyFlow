# 1 Abstract

Accurate estimation of battery state of health and remaining useful life is essential for ensuring the safety, reliability, and economic viability of lithium-ion battery systems across applications ranging from electric vehicles to grid storage. This survey provides a comprehensive review of recent advances in data-driven and hybrid modeling approaches for battery cycle life prediction, with a particular focus on methodologies that integrate machine learning with physical degradation principles. We examine the evolution from purely data-driven models, which often exhibit limited generalization beyond their training domains, to reinforced graph-based physics-informed neural networks that embed electrochemical dynamics directly into learning architectures. Key findings highlight the critical importance of precisely defining the prediction application as a foundational step, the significant impact of calendar aging on lifetime projections, and the limitations of models trained exclusively on high C-rate cycling data. The review further reveals how coupled electrochemical-thermal-degradation frameworks can disentangle the interplay between cycling and calendar aging mechanisms, demonstrating that rest state of charge and depth of discharge jointly govern the dominant degradation mode. By synthesizing these developments, this survey underscores the growing necessity of integrating physical insight with data-driven flexibility to achieve robust, interpretable, and generalizable battery life predictions.


# 2 Introduction

The rapid global transition toward electrified transportation and renewable energy storage has placed lithium-ion batteries at the center of modern energy systems. Their high energy density, long cycle life, and declining manufacturing costs have made them the dominant technology for electric vehicles, consumer electronics, and grid-scale storage. However, as these batteries age, their performance degrades through complex electrochemical and thermal processes, leading to capacity fade and increased internal resistance. Accurate estimation of the state of health and prediction of remaining useful life are therefore critical for ensuring operational safety, optimizing maintenance schedules, and enabling second-life applications. In recent years, the proliferation of large-scale battery testing data and advances in computational methods have driven a paradigm shift from traditional physics-based models toward data-driven approaches, particularly those rooted in machine learning. These methods offer the ability to capture nonlinear degradation patterns directly from operational data without requiring exhaustive knowledge of internal electrochemical mechanisms, thus opening new avenues for real-time, adaptive battery health management.

Despite the promise of machine learning techniques, several fundamental challenges persist in the domain of battery state-of-health estimation and remaining-useful-life prediction. A primary concern is the limited generalization capability of purely data-driven models when applied to unseen cell types, operating conditions, or aging protocols that differ from those represented in the training data. Many existing approaches rely on observing a substantial portion of a cell’s lifetime before making reliable predictions, which restricts their practical utility in scenarios where only early-cycle data are available. Furthermore, the exclusion of calendar aging effects in many machine learning pipelines can produce overly optimistic lifetime projections, as degradation during storage or idle periods is often neglected. The interpretability of black-box models also remains a significant obstacle, since stakeholders in safety-critical applications require not only accurate predictions but also an understanding of the underlying degradation mechanisms driving those predictions. These interconnected issues highlight the need for hybrid frameworks that integrate physical knowledge with data-driven learning, as well as rigorous validation across diverse usage scenarios.

This survey makes several contributions to the literature on machine learning for battery health estimation. First, it provides a comprehensive and structured review of state-of-the-art data-driven methodologies, encompassing classical regression techniques, deep learning architectures, and emerging hybrid models that fuse mechanistic understanding with machine learning. By systematically categorizing these approaches according to their input features, model structures, and output objectives, the survey clarifies the landscape of available techniques and their respective strengths and limitations. Special attention is devoted to methods that address the critical challenge of generalization, including transfer learning, domain adaptation, and physics-informed neural networks that embed governing equations directly into the learning process. The review also examines the role of feature engineering, highlighting how domain knowledge—such as incremental capacity analysis and differential voltage analysis—can be leveraged to improve model accuracy and interpretability.

A second major contribution is the critical assessment of evaluation practices and data requirements that underpin robust model development. The survey analyzes the implications of training data composition, including the effects of cycling protocols, temperature conditions, and depth-of-discharge ranges on model performance. It further discusses the often-overlooked distinction between cycle aging and calendar aging, synthesizing insights from electrochemical-thermal-degradation studies to illustrate how operating conditions govern the relative dominance of these two degradation modes. By drawing connections between physics-based simulation frameworks and data-driven modeling pipelines, the survey identifies opportunities for generating synthetic training data that capture coupled degradation mechanisms. Additionally, the review addresses the emerging topic of battery remanufacturing and second-life assessment, where rapid state-of-health and residual value predictions from limited pulse test data are of growing industrial relevance.

The aim of this survey is to equip researchers and practitioners with a clear understanding of the current capabilities, open challenges, and future directions in machine learning-based battery health estimation. The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 introduces the fundamental concepts of lithium-ion battery degradation and defines the state-of-health and remaining-useful-life estimation problems. Section 3 presents a taxonomy of data-driven approaches, progressing from classical machine learning methods to advanced deep learning and hybrid physics-informed architectures. Section 4 examines critical issues of generalization, interpretability, and data requirements, drawing on both experimental and simulation-based studies. Section 5 discusses emerging application domains, including fast screening for remanufacturing and second-life deployment. Finally, Section 6 concludes with a summary of key findings and an outlook on promising research directions.

# 4 Physics-Informed Neural Networks for Calendar and Cycle Ageing

## 4.1 Electrochemical-Thermal-Degradation Modeling for Calendar and Cycle Ageing

Madabattula (2026) developed an electrochemical-thermal-degradation model that links calendar and cycle ageing through a consistent parameterisation. The model reveals how operating conditions govern the relative contributions of these two ageing modes. At a low state of charge of 10%, calendar ageing effects are far lower than cycling effects, and the cycle-life for 50% depth-of-discharge (DoD) cases at both 25°C and 40°C extends beyond 12 years (Madabattula, 2026). In contrast, 90% DoD cases consistently exhibit shorter life than 50% DoD cases across all temperatures. At 40°C with a 100% rest state of charge, the cell experiences the shortest life—ranging from 0.9 to 2 years—due to the dominant contribution of calendar ageing (Madabattula, 2026). When the rest state of charge is reduced to 10% at the same 1C rate and 40°C temperature, the cycle-life lengthens substantially to between 10 and 14 years as a result of diminished calendar ageing effects (Madabattula, 2026). These findings demonstrate that an electrochemical-thermal-degradation framework can capture the interplay between calendar and cycling degradation, showing how temperature, DoD, and rest state of charge jointly determine whether calendar or cycling mechanisms dominate lifetime.

## 4.2 Reinforced Graph-Based Physics-Informed Neural Networks for SoH and RUL Prediction

**4.2 Reinforced Graph-Based Physics-Informed Neural Networks for SoH and RUL Prediction**

Pour et al. (2025) proposed a reinforced graph-based physics-informed neural network enhanced with dynamic weights for state-of-health (SoH) and remaining useful life (RUL) estimation. Their framework builds on the concept of Deep Hidden Physics Models, which employ deep neural networks to jointly learn the system solution and the structure of its underlying dynamics directly from observed data (Pour et al., 2025). In a complementary direction, Madabattula (2026) developed a consistent parameterisation of an electrochemical-thermal-degradation model that links calendar and cycle ageing in lithium-ion batteries. This physics-based approach provides insights into the coupled effects of usage conditions on degradation mechanisms to predict SoH and RUL (Madabattula, 2026). The simulated SoH and degradation mode analysis data generated under 81 cyclic ageing use cases, 9 calendar ageing cases, and 3 standard cycling conditions have been made available online for training physics-informed data-driven models (Madabattula, 2026). Madabattula (2026) notes that a physics-based model offering such degradation mode insights was not previously available in the literature, and the resulting insights can help validate battery models under different modes of operation for consistency checking and cell improvement.

# 6 Future Directions

The current landscape of battery state and health estimation reveals several persistent limitations that constrain the reliability and applicability of existing models. Data-driven approaches, while powerful, often struggle to generalize beyond the specific cell types and operating conditions represented in their training data, leading to brittle predictions when confronted with unseen usage profiles or chemistries. The exclusion of calendar aging effects in many lifetime prediction frameworks produces overly optimistic projections that fail to capture real-world degradation, where storage conditions and rest periods significantly influence overall battery life. Furthermore, the integration of physics-based insights with machine learning remains incomplete, as most hybrid models lack consistent parameterization across calendar and cycling degradation modes, and the available training datasets rarely encompass the full diversity of aging scenarios encountered in practice. The interpretability of complex models also remains a concern, with even structured approaches offering limited transparency into the underlying electrochemical degradation mechanisms that govern capacity fade.

Future research should prioritize the development of unified modeling frameworks that seamlessly couple calendar and cycling degradation within data-driven architectures. Such frameworks would leverage consistent electrochemical-thermal-degradation parameterizations to generate rich synthetic datasets spanning diverse operating conditions, including variations in temperature, depth-of-discharge, rest state-of-charge, and C-rate. These datasets could then serve as training foundations for physics-informed neural networks that inherently respect thermodynamic and kinetic constraints while learning from observed data. A particularly promising direction involves extending graph-based and reinforced learning architectures to incorporate degradation mode analysis, enabling models to not only predict state-of-health and remaining useful life but also attribute capacity loss to specific mechanisms such as solid-electrolyte interphase growth, lithium plating, or active material loss. Additionally, research should address the generalization challenge by designing transfer learning strategies that allow models trained on laboratory cycling data to adapt to field-deployed batteries experiencing irregular usage patterns and mixed calendar-cycling aging. The development of pulse-test-based diagnostic protocols for rapid state assessment in remanufacturing contexts represents another valuable avenue, particularly when combined with uncertainty quantification to characterize generalization risk across heterogeneous cell populations.

The proposed directions hold substantial potential to transform battery management across multiple application domains. Unified calendar-cycling models would enable more accurate lifetime projections for electric vehicles and grid storage systems, where batteries routinely experience extended idle periods interspersed with varying load profiles. Physics-informed architectures with degradation mode attribution would empower engineers to diagnose failure mechanisms in real time, facilitating targeted interventions that extend operational life and improve safety. Transferable models capable of adapting to unseen cells would accelerate battery remanufacturing and second-life assessment by enabling rapid, non-destructive state-of-health estimation from minimal diagnostic data. Collectively, these advances would reduce the reliance on costly and time-consuming laboratory aging campaigns, lower barriers to battery reuse and recycling, and support the development of more resilient energy storage systems aligned with sustainability objectives.


# 7 Conclusion

This survey has systematically examined the landscape of data-driven and hybrid modeling approaches for battery state-of-health estimation and remaining useful life prediction, revealing several consistent and interconnected findings. A central observation is that purely data-driven models, while capable of capturing complex nonlinear degradation patterns from large-scale cycling data, exhibit fundamental limitations in generalization when confronted with unseen cell chemistries, operating conditions, or aging protocols that diverge from their training distributions. The reviewed literature demonstrates that such models frequently neglect calendar aging effects, leading to overly optimistic lifetime projections that fail to reflect real-world degradation where storage conditions and rest periods significantly influence capacity fade. In contrast, hybrid frameworks that integrate physical degradation principles into machine learning architectures offer a compelling path forward. The coupling of electrochemical-thermal-degradation models with physics-informed neural networks enables the disentanglement of cycling and calendar aging mechanisms, revealing how rest state-of-charge, depth-of-discharge, and temperature jointly govern the dominant degradation mode. Furthermore, the emergence of reinforced graph-based architectures that embed governing equations directly into learning objectives illustrates the growing capacity of these models to achieve both predictive accuracy and mechanistic interpretability, while the availability of simulated degradation datasets spanning diverse aging scenarios provides a critical resource for training and validating such approaches.

The significance of this survey resides in its synthesis of two converging paradigms that are reshaping the field of battery lifetime prognostics. By juxtaposing traditional data-driven cycle life prediction with emerging physics-informed and graph-based neural network methodologies, the discussion illuminates a critical transition away from black-box empiricism toward architectures that encode domain knowledge without sacrificing the flexibility and scalability afforded by deep learning. This synthesis not only clarifies the persistent challenges of generalization, interpretability, and data representativeness but also identifies actionable pathways for overcoming them. The survey underscores the foundational importance of precisely defining the prediction application as a first modeling step, highlights the dangers of training exclusively on high C-rate cycling data, and draws attention to the often-overlooked interplay between calendar and cycling degradation. These insights carry particular weight given the accelerating global deployment of lithium-ion batteries in electric vehicles and grid storage, where accurate lifetime estimation directly impacts operational safety, economic viability, and sustainability outcomes.

Moving forward, the research community must prioritize the development of unified prognostic frameworks that seamlessly couple calendar and cycling degradation within data-driven architectures. Concerted efforts are needed to generate and openly share high-fidelity degradation datasets that span diverse operating regimes, encompassing variations in temperature, depth-of-discharge, rest state-of-charge, and C-rate. Model developers should embrace hybrid architectures that embed electrochemical constraints and thermal dynamics directly into learning objectives, thereby improving robustness under distribution shifts and enabling generalization to unseen cells and usage profiles. Equally critical is the adoption of rigorous validation protocols that test predictive performance under realistic, multi-modal aging conditions, including the irregular usage patterns characteristic of field-deployed batteries. By fostering closer collaboration between domain experts in electrochemistry and practitioners in machine learning, the field can advance toward interpretable, transferable, and ultimately trustworthy prognostic tools. Such tools will be essential for enabling real-time degradation mode diagnosis, rapid state assessment in remanufacturing contexts, and the safe, efficient utilization of battery systems throughout their entire life cycle.
