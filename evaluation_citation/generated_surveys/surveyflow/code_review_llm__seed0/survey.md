# 1 Abstract

The automation of code review and program repair has advanced rapidly with the emergence of large language models, yet challenges remain in adapting these systems to evolving guidelines, sensitive data constraints, and the need for reliable evaluation. This survey systematically examines recent developments in fine-tuning, prompt engineering, multi-agent frameworks, and continual learning strategies for code review and automated program repair. We analyze how fine-tuned models such as PLBART and CodeT5 leverage review context to improve repair accuracy, and we explore the role of prompt engineering and iterative feedback loops in multi-agent architectures. Our review further highlights critical limitations of surface-level similarity metrics like BLEU for evaluating functional correctness and presents evidence from industrial deployments where online metrics such as resolution rates and actionable-comment proportions offer more meaningful assessments. Additionally, we identify the growing importance of continual instruction tuning and self-evolving models as promising directions for overcoming the infeasibility of static fine-tuning on proprietary data and for integrating dynamic review guidelines. This survey provides a comprehensive synthesis of current methodologies and evaluation practices, offering insights into the future trajectory of adaptive, scalable code review automation.


# 2 Introduction

The automation of software engineering tasks has emerged as a central research frontier in the era of large language models, with code review representing one of the most resource-intensive and quality-critical activities in modern development workflows. Code review serves as a gatekeeping practice that ensures code correctness, maintainability, and adherence to project standards, yet it demands substantial human effort and expertise. The advent of large language models with strong code comprehension capabilities has opened new possibilities for automating aspects of this process, from detecting defects to generating actionable review comments. Researchers have increasingly explored how pre-trained models can be adapted to understand the nuanced relationship between code changes and natural language feedback, drawing on advances in fine-tuning strategies, prompt engineering, and multi-agent coordination. This growing body of work reflects a broader shift toward leveraging artificial intelligence to augment human reviewers and streamline software quality assurance pipelines.

Despite significant progress, several fundamental challenges persist in the development and deployment of automated code review systems. A primary concern lies in the evaluation of generated code and review comments, where traditional surface-level similarity metrics such as BLEU have been shown to correlate poorly with functional correctness, raising questions about the validity of commonly reported performance figures. Beyond offline benchmarks, the translation of research advances into industrial practice introduces additional obstacles, including the infeasibility of fine-tuning on proprietary codebases due to data sensitivity, the frequent neglect of project-specific review guidelines in current approaches, and the dependence of retrieval-augmented methods on rich historical data that may be unavailable for new projects. Furthermore, the static nature of conventional fine-tuning limits the ability of models to adapt to evolving requirements, motivating the exploration of continual learning paradigms. These interconnected problems underscore the need for a comprehensive examination of the techniques, evaluation frameworks, and deployment strategies that define the current landscape of large language model-based code review automation.

This survey makes three primary contributions to the understanding and advancement of automated code review using large language models. First, it provides a systematic taxonomy of the technical approaches that have been applied to this domain, encompassing fine-tuning methodologies, prompt engineering techniques, and multi-agent architectures. By organizing these methods into a coherent framework, the survey clarifies the relationships between different research directions and highlights the progression from adapting individual models to orchestrating cooperative agent systems. The analysis covers how fine-tuned models such as PLBART and CodeT5 have been optimized on review-specific datasets, how carefully crafted prompts enable models to incorporate review context into repair generation, and how agent-based frameworks simulate the iterative feedback loops characteristic of human review processes.

Second, this survey offers a critical examination of evaluation practices and industrial deployment experiences, bridging the gap between laboratory benchmarks and production realities. It synthesizes evidence from both offline evaluations and large-scale online studies, including deployments at major technology companies, to assess the practical viability of current approaches. The survey analyzes the limitations of similarity-based metrics and catalogues alternative evaluation strategies that prioritize functional correctness and developer actionability. It further documents the operational metrics that have emerged from industrial deployments—such as code resolution rates and proportions of human-actionable comments—providing a more complete picture of how automated review systems perform when integrated into real engineering workflows. This dual focus on techniques and evaluation establishes a foundation for identifying the most promising directions for future research.

The aim of this survey is to provide researchers and practitioners with a structured overview of the state of the art in large language model-based code review automation, while also identifying open problems and emerging opportunities. The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. The survey first establishes foundational concepts and categorizes the principal technical approaches, including fine-tuning strategies, prompt-based methods, and multi-agent frameworks. It then turns to the critical issue of evaluation, examining both traditional metrics and their limitations, as well as the online metrics used in industrial settings. Subsequent sections address the challenges of industrial deployment, including data sensitivity, guideline adherence, and the potential of continual instruction tuning as an adaptive alternative to static fine-tuning. The survey concludes by synthesizing key insights and outlining directions for future work that can advance the field toward more robust, adaptable, and practically deployable code review automation systems.


# 3 Fine-Tuning and Prompt Design for LLM-Driven Code Review

## 3.1 Federated and Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning for Code Review

Research on fine-tuning for code review has demonstrated that adapting pre-trained models can substantially improve automated program repair. Paul et al. (2023) fine-tuned PLBART and CodeT5 on the Review4Repair dataset, finding that both models significantly outperformed prior baselines that were trained with both buggy code and its corresponding code review. On this dataset, the fine-tuned PLBART model achieved a 9.91% improvement, while CodeT5 exhibited slightly better overall performance, which the authors attribute to its stronger natural language and programming language comprehension capabilities (Paul et al., 2023). Beyond full fine-tuning, the potential for role-specific adaptation has also been noted in multi-agent frameworks. Khanzadeh (2025) suggests that individual agents, such as a Debugger, could be fine-tuned on data specific to their role—for instance, fine-tuning a dedicated bug-fixing model to become more adept at reading tracebacks and patching code—to improve efficiency within cooperative software development systems.

## 3.2 Retrieval-Augmented Generation and Review Context for Program Repair

The application of large language models to program repair has opened new avenues for leveraging natural language context to guide fix generation. Paul et al. (2023) highlight that prompt engineering enables researchers to craft clear and concise prompts that elicit coherent and relevant responses from models, underscoring a need for diverse techniques to advance automated bug fixing. Their work specifically explores using different pre-trained models and LLMs to repair code based on code reviews, directly incorporating review context into the repair process.

In multi-agent frameworks, review-like feedback can also drive iterative repair. Khanzadeh (2025) describe a Debugger agent that uses an LLM to analyze failures—such as reading an error traceback or spotting a logical mistake—and then proposes a fix by generating a patch or revised code within an iterative test-and-fix loop. This approach mirrors the use of contextual feedback, akin to review comments, to refine repairs.

## 3.3 Multi-Agent Frameworks and Prompt Engineering for Code Refinement

Prompt engineering has opened a new path for automated code repair, where researchers develop methods to craft clear and concise prompts and use large language models to obtain coherent and relevant responses (Paul et al., 2023). This approach reflects a growing need for diverse techniques to advance the state-of-the-art in repairing buggy code (Paul et al., 2023). In parallel, multi-agent frameworks have emerged to coordinate specialized agents on software development tasks. Khanzadeh (2025) introduces AgentMesh, an architecture in which a set of specialized agents communicate and cooperate to accomplish software development automation. Each agent is implemented as an autonomous module—specifically, a Python class wrapping an LLM prompt—that accepts some form of input and contributes to the overall task (Khanzadeh, 2025).

# 4 Benchmarks and Metrics for Code Generation Assessment

## 4.1 Benchmarks and Performance Metrics for Code Generation Evaluation

A central concern in code generation evaluation is the validity of the performance metrics employed. Paul et al. (2024) observe that while similarity metrics have long been the standard approach for evaluating NLP models, their suitability for code generation has been increasingly questioned. Specifically, they note that Kulal et al. (2019) found that BLEU—a widely used similarity metric—fails to assess functional correctness, and that Hendrycks et al. (2021) further demonstrated an inverse relationship between BLEU scores and functional correctness (Paul et al., 2024). These findings underscore a critical limitation of surface-level similarity metrics for capturing the true quality of generated code.

# 5 Industrial-Scale Deployment of LLM-Based Code Review

## 5.1 Large-Scale Online Evaluation of LLM Code Review Automation

Recent work has moved beyond offline benchmarks to examine how LLM-based code review automation performs in production settings. Pornprasit and Tantithamthavorn (2024) report on RovoDev Code Reviewer, a system deployed at Atlassian that automatically generates review comments for code changes. In a large-scale online evaluation covering a one-week period in July 2025, the system achieved a code resolution rate of 38.70%, compared with human-written comments (Pornprasit & Tantithamthavorn, 2024). The same study provides aggregate quality-check statistics, showing that after generation 22% of pull requests contained human-actionable comments (%PR_HAC), while after a quality check this dropped to 7%, alongside shifts in other actionable-comment metrics (Pornprasit & Tantithamthavorn, 2024). These figures offer a rare glimpse into how LLM-generated review comments are received and acted upon in a real industrial workflow.

The practical viability of deploying LLMs at scale in review-related pipelines is further supported by Kang et al. (2025), who validate their MoE-CL approach within large-scale industrial content compliance review systems. While their evaluation centers on content compliance rather than code review, the work demonstrates sustained self-evolution capabilities in production AI deployments (Kang et al., 2025). Together, these studies illustrate a growing body of evidence that LLM-based review automation can be evaluated not only through offline accuracy but also through online metrics such as resolution rates and actionable-comment proportions in live engineering environments.

## 5.2 Self-Evolving LLMs via Continual Instruction Tuning

Continual instruction tuning is a paradigm that dynamically adapts large language models through sequential task-specific instruction tuning, allowing them to incrementally incorporate new knowledge from evolving instructions while retaining performance on previously learned tasks (Kang et al., 2025). This approach is particularly relevant in domains where data, requirements, or guidelines shift over time, as it avoids the need for costly full retraining.

In the context of code review automation, the limitations of static fine-tuning motivate the need for more adaptive strategies. Pornprasit and Tantithamthavorn (2024) note that fine-tuning LLMs on proprietary or user-generated content is often infeasible in industrial settings due to the sensitivity of customer code and metadata. Furthermore, review guidelines—which are critical for guiding inexperienced reviewers—are frequently ignored in current LLM-powered code review approaches, and retrieval-augmented generation methods depend on rich historical data that may not exist for new projects. These constraints highlight the potential value of self-evolving LLMs that can continually adapt to new review guidelines and project-specific contexts without requiring access to sensitive historical data.

# 7 Future Directions

The current landscape of automated code review and program repair remains constrained by several interrelated limitations that impede both research progress and practical deployment. Static fine-tuning paradigms cannot adapt to evolving review guidelines or project-specific contexts without costly retraining cycles, while industrial adoption is severely restricted by the infeasibility of fine-tuning on proprietary codebases due to data sensitivity concerns. Retrieval-augmented generation methods, though promising, depend on rich historical review data that is unavailable for new or small-scale projects. The evaluation crisis represents an equally pressing gap: widely adopted surface-level similarity metrics such as BLEU have been demonstrated to correlate inversely with functional correctness, yet the field continues to lack standardized, practical metrics that capture real-world utility, developer actionability, and downstream productivity improvements. Furthermore, while multi-agent frameworks have introduced role-specific coordination for software development tasks, research on how individual agents can be optimally fine-tuned for specialized responsibilities within cooperative systems remains nascent, leaving substantial performance gains unrealized. The disconnect between offline benchmark performance and modest production outcomes, evidenced by resolution rates hovering below forty percent in industrial deployments, underscores the urgency of addressing these foundational weaknesses.

Future research should prioritize the development of self-evolving models through continual instruction tuning pipelines that enable incremental adaptation to shifting review guidelines and project-specific requirements without requiring access to sensitive historical data. Such architectures would allow models to incorporate new knowledge sequentially while preserving performance on previously learned tasks, directly addressing the tension between model improvement and data privacy that currently blocks industrial adoption. A second critical direction involves advancing role-specific fine-tuning within multi-agent frameworks, where specialized agents such as debuggers, reviewers, and testers are trained on carefully partitioned datasets that reflect their distinct responsibilities. Investigating optimal strategies for designing agent-specific prompts, coordinating knowledge sharing across agents, and harmonizing individual learning processes could substantially improve the efficiency and accuracy of collaborative repair systems. Additionally, the field must confront the evaluation crisis by developing multidimensional assessment frameworks that measure functional correctness, practical actionability, and downstream developer productivity rather than surface-level textual similarity. Longitudinal studies conducted in industrial settings that track resolution rates, comment acceptance proportions, and time-to-merge over extended periods would provide invaluable evidence about real-world efficacy and help align research incentives with practical outcomes.

Pursuing these directions could fundamentally transform how automated code review and repair systems are built, evaluated, and deployed. Self-evolving models would lower the barrier to industrial adoption by eliminating the conflict between continuous improvement and data privacy, enabling organizations to benefit from adaptive automation without exposing proprietary code or customer metadata. Role-specific fine-tuning in multi-agent architectures could yield more reliable and context-aware repairs by leveraging the complementary strengths of specialized agents, potentially reducing the manual effort required in code review cycles and accelerating development velocity. Improved evaluation methodologies would steer the field toward solutions that genuinely enhance developer workflows rather than optimizing for misleading proxy metrics, fostering greater trust in automated review systems among practitioners. Collectively, these advances could accelerate the maturation of automated program repair from a promising research area into a dependable, scalable component of real-world software engineering practice, ultimately reducing the cognitive burden on human reviewers while improving code quality across the industry.


# 8 Conclusion

This survey has examined the evolving landscape of automated code review and program repair, revealing several interconnected findings that collectively define the current state of the field. The analysis demonstrates that fine-tuning pre-trained models such as PLBART and CodeT5 on code review datasets yields substantial improvements in repair accuracy, with CodeT5 exhibiting particular strength due to its dual proficiency in natural and programming language understanding. Beyond full model fine-tuning, role-specific adaptation within multi-agent frameworks has emerged as a promising direction, enabling specialized agents to handle distinct tasks such as debugging and patch generation through targeted training and iterative feedback loops. The survey further identifies a critical methodological shift in evaluation practices, as traditional surface-level similarity metrics like BLEU have proven inadequate for assessing functional correctness, with evidence showing an inverse relationship between BLEU scores and actual code quality. Industrial deployments of large language model-based review systems have begun generating real-world evidence of viability, with production metrics such as code resolution rates of 38.70% and actionable comment proportions offering concrete measures of practical utility that offline benchmarks fail to capture. Additionally, the limitations of static fine-tuning in dynamic, privacy-sensitive environments have motivated interest in continual instruction tuning as a pathway toward self-evolving models capable of adapting to shifting guidelines without compromising data security.

The significance of this survey lies in its synthesis of disconnected yet complementary research threads into a coherent narrative about the maturation of automated code review technologies. By bridging foundational work on fine-tuning with emerging paradigms in multi-agent collaboration, prompt engineering, and continual learning, the survey provides researchers and practitioners with a structured understanding of both the achievements and the unresolved challenges in the field. The critical examination of evaluation metrics addresses a persistent methodological weakness that has implications for the reproducibility and comparability of future studies, particularly as the community increasingly recognizes that optimizing for similarity scores can lead to misleading conclusions about model performance. Moreover, the inclusion of industrial case studies grounds the discussion in practical realities, underscoring the gap between laboratory benchmarks and production requirements, where factors such as data sensitivity, guideline adherence, and the availability of historical data fundamentally constrain deployment options. This integration of academic and industrial perspectives offers a holistic view that is essential for guiding the next generation of research and development efforts toward solutions that are not only technically sophisticated but also practically deployable.

As the field advances, several imperatives emerge that demand concerted attention from both the research community and industry practitioners. Researchers must prioritize the development of evaluation frameworks that capture functional correctness and real-world utility rather than relying on convenient but misleading similarity metrics, potentially through longitudinal studies that track resolution rates, comment acceptance, and developer productivity over extended periods. The exploration of continual and self-evolving architectures should be accelerated to address the dynamic nature of software development environments and the legitimate constraints around data privacy, enabling models to incrementally incorporate new review guidelines without requiring access to sensitive proprietary code. Multi-agent systems warrant deeper investigation, particularly regarding how specialized agents can be optimally coordinated and how their individual learning processes can be harmonized to achieve reliable, context-aware repairs that reduce manual effort in code review cycles. Finally, closer collaboration between academia and industry is essential to ensure that innovations translate into tools that genuinely enhance developer productivity and code quality, rather than optimizing for proxy metrics that fail to reflect the complex realities of software engineering practice. The path forward demands not only technical ingenuity but also a commitment to rigorous, context-aware evaluation that aligns research incentives with practical outcomes.
