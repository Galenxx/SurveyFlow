# 1 Abstract

Diffusion models have revolutionized generative modeling across multiple domains, yet their application to three-dimensional shape generation remains particularly challenging due to fundamental differences between 2D pixel grids and the diverse geometric representations used to describe 3D structures. This survey provides a comprehensive review of recent advances in diffusion-based 3D shape generation, systematically examining how probabilistic denoising frameworks have been adapted to accommodate point clouds, voxels, neural fields, and structured latent spaces. We categorize existing approaches by their underlying data representations and architectural innovations, spanning point-voxel hybrid models that combine structural regularity with geometric flexibility, latent vecset diffusion methods employing sparse attention mechanisms for scalable training, hierarchical point diffusion techniques leveraging compact encodings, CAD-specific diffusion priors, and structure-aware multi-diffusion frameworks that explicitly model shape components, symmetries, and assembly processes. Our analysis reveals a clear trajectory toward methods that leverage compact latent representations, incorporate geometric priors, and decompose the generation process into semantically meaningful sub-problems to improve both computational efficiency and output quality. This survey consolidates the current state of the field while highlighting the critical role of representation choice and structural inductive biases in advancing diffusion-based 3D content creation.


# 2 Introduction

The generation of three-dimensional digital content has become a cornerstone of modern computer vision, graphics, and robotics, with applications spanning immersive virtual environments, autonomous driving simulations, and computer-aided design. Traditionally, the creation of high-quality 3D shapes and scenes has relied on labor-intensive manual modeling or on specialized capture hardware, limiting scalability and accessibility. In recent years, deep generative models have emerged as a powerful alternative, enabling the synthesis of diverse and realistic 3D data directly from learned priors. Among these, diffusion models have attracted particular attention due to their ability to produce high-fidelity samples and to flexibly handle a variety of data modalities. Originally developed for image synthesis, diffusion models operate by gradually corrupting data with noise and learning to reverse this process, thereby transforming a simple noise distribution into a complex data distribution. Extending this success to the 3D domain, however, introduces a distinct set of challenges that stem from the fundamental geometric and structural properties of 3D representations.

Adapting diffusion models from 2D images to 3D data is far from straightforward. Unlike pixel arrays, which are defined on regular grids, 3D shapes and scenes can be represented in fundamentally different forms, including point clouds, voxel grids, meshes, implicit neural fields, and structured latent codes. Each representation entails a unique interplay of geometric elements, spatial relationships, and invariances that must be carefully accounted for in the probabilistic formulation of the diffusion process. A primary difficulty is that a naive extension of image-based diffusion to pure voxel or point representations often yields unsatisfactory results, as the model struggles to capture fine geometric details and global coherence. Moreover, the computational demands of operating directly on high-resolution 3D data can be prohibitive, motivating the development of more efficient and representation-aware architectures. Additional complexities arise when generating structured objects, such as those with part-based decompositions or symmetry constraints, where the generative model must respect both local part geometry and global assembly rules. These challenges have spurred a rapidly growing body of research that explores diverse representations, conditioning mechanisms, and diffusion formulations tailored to the 3D domain.

This survey provides a comprehensive and structured overview of diffusion models for 3D shape and scene generation, systematically examining the methods, representations, and evaluation strategies that define this emerging field. The first major contribution is a detailed taxonomy of 3D representations employed in diffusion-based generation. We cover point-voxel hybrid approaches that combine the structural regularity of voxels with the flexibility of point clouds, latent vecset methods that encode shapes into compact sets of feature vectors for efficient diffusion, and neural field-based representations that use implicit functions decoded from latent codes. We further discuss hierarchical and part-aware representations that explicitly model structural decompositions, as well as latent spaces designed for structured data such as CAD models. For each representation category, we analyze how the diffusion process is formulated, what architectural choices are made, and how geometric properties are preserved or exploited.

The second contribution is an in-depth analysis of the diffusion architectures and conditioning strategies that have been developed to address the unique demands of 3D generation. We examine how attention mechanisms, particularly spatial sparse attention, have been adapted to handle the irregular and high-dimensional nature of 3D latent representations, enabling scalable training and inference. We also review conditioning paradigms, including image-guided generation, shape completion from partial observations, and multi-modal control, highlighting how external signals are integrated into the denoising process. In addition, we discuss multi-stage and multi-diffusion frameworks, such as quartets of diffusions that separately model shape latents, symmetries, parts, and assembly, and analyze how reducing or merging diffusion stages affects generation quality and efficiency. Throughout, we connect design decisions to their impact on sample fidelity, diversity, and structural coherence.

The aim of this survey is to offer researchers and practitioners a clear and organized reference for understanding the landscape of diffusion-based 3D generation. We structure the paper as follows. We begin by introducing the foundational principles of diffusion models and their adaptation to 3D data. We then present our taxonomy of 3D representations and detail the corresponding diffusion formulations. Subsequent sections examine architectural innovations, conditioning mechanisms, and evaluation protocols. Finally, we discuss open challenges and promising directions for future research, aiming to guide the development of next-generation 3D generative models.


# 3 Point-Voxel Diffusion for 3D Shape Generation

## 3.1 Point-Voxel and Particle-Based Diffusion

A straightforward extension of diffusion models to pure voxel or pure point representations has proven insufficient for high-quality 3D shape generation. Cheng et al. (2022) demonstrated this failure experimentally and introduced Point-Voxel Diffusion (PVD), a denoising diffusion probabilistic model designed specifically for 3D point clouds. PVD adopts a point-voxel representation that combines the strengths of both modalities, enabling superior generative performance and impressive shape completion compared to deterministic encoder-decoder baselines. The model is formulated for unconditional shape generation and further adapted with a modified objective for shape completion from partial observations.

The underlying principle of such diffusion-based approaches rests on linking the distribution of 3D coordinates—whether in point clouds or meshes—to a noise distribution through a forward and reverse diffusion process. Wan et al. (2025) highlight this conceptual foundation, noting that unlike pixel data, 3D data involves a distinct interplay of geometric properties that must be accounted for in the probabilistic formulation.

## 3.2 Direct Diffusion on Mesh and 3D Representations

Extending the success of diffusion models from 2D image generation to 3D representations presents unique technical challenges. Cheng et al. (2022) note that while probabilistic denoising diffusion approaches produce highly realistic 2D images, their extension to 3D is technically demanding. A key difficulty lies in the fundamental differences between image and 3D data structures. Wan et al. (2025) observe that, unlike pixel data, 3D data is characterized by the interplay between geometric elements, and they draw an analogy linking the distribution of coordinates in point clouds or meshes to a noise distribution through the diffusion process, which forms the basis of their probabilistic methodology for 3D shape generation.

# 4 Latent Sparse-Attention Diffusion for Scalable 3D

## 4.1 Sparse Attention Diffusion for Latent Vecsets

In the context of latent vecset diffusion, sparse attention mechanisms have been introduced to improve computational efficiency. Wu et al. (2025) propose a Spatial Sparse Attention mechanism that operates by partitioning key and value tokens into spatially coherent blocks according to their geometric relationships, followed by blockwise token selection to achieve significant acceleration. A straightforward approach to this partitioning involves treating the latent tokens as a one-dimensional sequence and dividing them into fixed-length blocks (Wu et al., 2025). This mechanism is integrated into an image-conditioned diffusion transformer designed to enhance both training and inference efficiency (Wu et al., 2025). In parallel, other work has explored diffusion priors over latent representations of structured data. Alam and Ahmed (2024) develop a diffusion prior for CAD latents, where the denoising model is built upon an MLP-ResNet architecture and can optionally accept an image latent as input.

## 4.2 Latent Diffusion for CAD and SDF Representations

A line of work leverages neural fields for shape representation, using compact latent codes to enable scalable generation. Wu et al. (2025) describe how 3DShape2Vecset pioneered diffusion-based shape synthesis by encoding 3D shapes into a latent vecset via a Variational Autoencoder; this latent representation can be decoded into neural signed distance functions (SDFs) or occupancy fields and rendered through Marching Cubes. The latent vecset is then modeled with a diffusion process to produce diverse 3D shapes. Subsequent methods extended this pipeline: CLAY adopted Diffusion Transformers, while TripoSG further improved fidelity through rectified flow transformers and hybrid supervision (Wu et al., 2025).

For computer-aided design, Alam and Ahmed (2024) introduced a CAD Diffusion Prior that operates on CAD latents and can optionally condition on image latents. Their denoising model employs an MLP-ResNet architecture, and the system uses a contrastive loss to jointly learn the latent representation of the CAD image with the latent representation of the CAD data.

## 4.3 Hierarchical Latent Point Diffusion

Hierarchical latent point diffusion methods leverage compact latent representations to enable scalable 3D shape generation. Wu et al. (2025) note that these approaches benefit from neural fields for shape representation, using compact latent codes that support scalable generation. A foundational example is 3DShape2Vecset, which pioneered diffusion-based shape synthesis by employing a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) to encode 3D shapes into a latent vecset that can be decoded into neural signed distance functions (SDFs) (Wu et al., 2025). The same work observes that implicit latent-based methods often depend on VAEs with asymmetric 3D representations, which can reduce training efficiency and typically require hundreds of GPUs (Wu et al., 2025).

In the context of conditional generation, Alam and Ahmed (2024) describe a standard diffusion probabilistic model applied to a CAD latent space. Their approach concatenates a noised CAD latent with an image latent during the denoising process, using a projection mechanism to guide the generation (Alam & Ahmed, 2024). While this work focuses on image-conditioned CAD generation rather than hierarchical point diffusion per se, it illustrates the broader paradigm of operating diffusion processes directly on structured latent representations.

# 5 Structure-Aware Multi-Modal Diffusion for 3D Avatars

## 5.1 Dual Diffusion for Multi-Modal 3D Avatar Generation

The dual-diffusion variants explored by Zhou et al. (2026) provide insight into how reducing the number of diffusion processes affects structured 3D generation. They define two duet configurations: Duet Var. 1 removes both the shape latent and symmetry diffusions, while Duet Var. 2 removes the symmetry diffusion and merges the part and assembler diffusions into a single full-shape diffusion (Zhou et al., 2026). These are contrasted against a solo baseline that uses a single diffusion to generate full point clouds directly. The authors note that the shape latent, symmetry, and assembler diffusions operate on low-dimensional representations, and that the majority of training time is spent on the part diffusion, which is accelerated through an efficient transformer-based diffusion applied to fundamental domains (Zhou et al., 2026).

## 5.2 Structure-Aware Diffusion with Part and Symmetry Guidance

Zhou et al. (2026) introduced the Quartet of Diffusions, a structure-aware point cloud generation framework that explicitly leverages part and symmetry guidance. Their pipeline employs four diffusion models to learn the distributions of shape latents, symmetries, semantic parts, and assemblers that assemble the full point cloud from parts. By explicitly modeling these structural components, the approach aims to produce generated shapes with coherent part decompositions and symmetry properties.

# 6 Future Directions

The current landscape of diffusion-based 3D generation, while advancing rapidly, remains constrained by several fundamental limitations. Existing methods are largely siloed within single-modality representations, such as point clouds, voxels, or latent codes, with no demonstrated integration of multi-view depth map diffusion despite its clear potential for capturing fine geometric detail and resolving occluded structures. Furthermore, the most scalable approaches, including hierarchical latent point diffusion and vecset-based methods, depend on computationally expensive variational autoencoders with asymmetric 3D representations, often requiring hundreds of GPUs for training and thereby restricting accessibility to well-resourced institutions. Structure-aware frameworks that model part decompositions and symmetries introduce promising inductive biases, yet their reliance on multiple sequential diffusion processes inflates both training complexity and inference latency. Finally, conditional generation capabilities remain narrow, with image-conditioned CAD generation representing only a fraction of the potential modalities, and no evidence exists for conditioning on textual descriptions, sketches, or physical constraints in a unified manner.

Future research should pursue the integration of multi-view depth map diffusion with existing point-voxel and latent representations to create a unified framework that captures both global structure and local surface detail. Such a hybrid approach could leverage depth maps rendered from multiple viewpoints as intermediate representations during the denoising process, enabling the model to reason about occluded geometry and thin structures that remain challenging for point-based or volumetric methods alone. Concurrently, efforts should focus on developing more efficient training paradigms for latent diffusion models, potentially through shared encoder architectures, progressive growing strategies, or knowledge distillation from pretrained 2D foundation models, thereby reducing the computational barrier to entry and democratizing access to high-quality 3D generation. Another critical direction involves extending structure-aware generation to support finer-grained semantic control, where users can specify not only part decompositions and symmetries but also functional relationships, material properties, and kinematic constraints. This would require designing new conditioning mechanisms capable of ingesting heterogeneous input modalities—text, images, sketches, and physical specifications—and mapping them into a shared latent space for coherent shape synthesis. Additionally, exploring the unification of multiple diffusion stages into more compact, jointly trained formulations could preserve the benefits of structural guidance while substantially reducing training overhead and inference time.

The proposed directions carry substantial potential impact for both research and application. A unified multi-view and latent diffusion framework could significantly advance the fidelity and structural plausibility of generated 3D content, enabling applications in automated content creation for gaming, virtual reality, and film production where high-quality assets are currently labor-intensive to produce. More efficient training paradigms would lower the barrier to entry, allowing smaller research labs and independent creators to experiment with and build upon state-of-the-art 3D generation models. Extending conditional control to encompass functional and physical constraints would bridge the gap between generative modeling and computer-aided design, making these tools viable for engineering workflows where manufacturability, assembly constraints, and material considerations are paramount. Collectively, these advances would move the field closer to a general-purpose 3D generative framework capable of producing not just visually appealing shapes, but functionally valid and semantically meaningful 3D content across diverse domains.


# 7 Conclusion

This survey has systematically examined the recent advances in diffusion-based three-dimensional shape generation, revealing a clear trajectory from direct extensions of two-dimensional diffusion models toward more sophisticated, structure-aware frameworks. The main findings indicate that naive applications of diffusion processes to pure voxel or point representations are insufficient for capturing the complex geometric and structural properties inherent to three-dimensional data. Instead, hybrid representations such as point-voxel formulations, hierarchical latent encodings, and neural field-based vecsets have emerged as effective strategies for achieving high-fidelity generation. The evidence further demonstrates that explicitly modeling structural priors, including part decompositions, symmetry relationships, and assembly mechanisms, significantly enhances the coherence and plausibility of generated shapes. Computational efficiency remains a central concern, with innovations such as spatial sparse attention mechanisms and compact latent representations enabling scalable training and inference. Across the surveyed methods, a unifying principle is the careful adaptation of the diffusion paradigm to respect the interplay between geometric elements, moving beyond pixel-based analogies to embrace the distinctive characteristics of three-dimensional data.

The significance of this survey lies in its structured synthesis of a rapidly evolving field, providing a coherent narrative that connects foundational probabilistic formulations with cutting-edge architectural innovations. By delineating the progression from unconditional point cloud generation to conditional, part-aware, and latent-space diffusion models, this work offers researchers a clear conceptual map of the design space. The critical analysis of representational choices, training efficiency trade-offs, and structural modeling strategies serves as a practical guide for future investigations. Moreover, the survey highlights underexplored territories, such as the limited integration of multi-view depth map diffusion within current literature, pointing to gaps that warrant further exploration. This consolidation of knowledge is essential for avoiding redundant efforts and for identifying the most promising directions for advancing the state of the art in generative three-dimensional modeling.

Looking forward, the field stands at an important juncture where the integration of structural reasoning, computational scalability, and multimodal conditioning will define the next generation of diffusion-based three-dimensional synthesis. Researchers are encouraged to pursue unified frameworks that seamlessly combine the strengths of hybrid representations with explicit geometric and semantic priors, while maintaining practical training requirements. The development of more efficient attention mechanisms and latent compression techniques will be critical for democratizing access to high-quality three-dimensional generation. Furthermore, expanding the scope of conditional generation to encompass richer input modalities and real-world partial observations promises to unlock transformative applications in computer-aided design, robotics, and virtual content creation. As the community continues to push the boundaries of what diffusion models can achieve in three dimensions, sustained attention to the fundamental interplay between data structure and probabilistic formulation will remain paramount.
