# 1 Abstract

Diffusion models have rapidly become a leading paradigm for three-dimensional shape generation, providing a robust probabilistic framework that addresses the inherent complexity of irregular geometric data. This survey presents a structured and comprehensive overview of recent advances in diffusion-based 3D generation, systematically examining methods across point cloud, mesh, and implicit neural representations. The analysis highlights key methodological innovations, including hybrid point-voxel formulations that balance geometric fidelity with processing efficiency, hierarchical and sparse latent spaces that disentangle global structure from local details, and structure-aware conditioning mechanisms that incorporate part-level semantics and symmetry constraints. We further explore the integration of multi-modal guidance through images, sketches, and depth cues, demonstrating how latent diffusion formulations and dual-model architectures enable flexible and controllable shape synthesis. The survey identifies a clear convergence toward latent-space approaches that achieve scalable, high-quality generation, while also revealing critical gaps in standardized evaluation and unified multi-representation frameworks. By synthesizing fragmented research contributions into a coherent taxonomy, this work provides a foundational reference for understanding the design choices, trade-offs, and future trajectories in diffusion-based 3D content creation.


# 2 Introduction

The generation of three-dimensional digital content has become a cornerstone of modern computer vision, graphics, and robotics, driven by the increasing demand for realistic virtual environments, digital twins, and autonomous system simulations. Unlike two-dimensional image synthesis, which has witnessed remarkable progress through deep generative models, 3D shape and scene generation poses fundamentally greater challenges due to the irregular, high-dimensional nature of geometric data and the need to capture both local surface details and global structural coherence. Early generative approaches, including generative adversarial networks and variational autoencoders, laid important groundwork but often struggled with mode collapse, limited diversity, and difficulties in scaling to complex geometries. The emergence of denoising diffusion probabilistic models has reshaped this landscape, offering a principled probabilistic framework that iteratively transforms noise into structured outputs through learned denoising steps. This paradigm has proven remarkably effective for 3D generation, enabling the synthesis of high-fidelity shapes and scenes across diverse representations while maintaining training stability and sample diversity that previous methods could not reliably achieve.

Despite these advances, several critical challenges persist in the application of diffusion models to 3D content generation. The first challenge concerns the choice of geometric representation, as 3D data can be expressed through point clouds, meshes, voxel grids, implicit neural fields, or hybrid formulations, each imposing distinct constraints on the diffusion process and the architectural design of denoising networks. A second challenge involves the computational demands of processing high-resolution 3D data, which grows rapidly with spatial dimensionality and necessitates efficient attention mechanisms and latent-space formulations to remain tractable. Furthermore, the generation of structurally meaningful shapes requires models that respect part-level organization, symmetry constraints, and hierarchical relationships, moving beyond purely statistical pattern matching to produce geometrically and semantically coherent outputs. Finally, the integration of multi-modal conditioning signals—such as images, sketches, or depth maps—into the diffusion pipeline introduces additional complexity in aligning heterogeneous representations while preserving generation quality and diversity.

This survey provides a comprehensive and structured overview of diffusion-based methods for 3D shape and scene generation, systematically examining the key technical dimensions that define this rapidly evolving field. Our primary contribution lies in organizing the extensive body of recent work along three fundamental axes: the underlying 3D representations employed, the methodological innovations in diffusion architectures and training strategies, and the evaluation protocols used to assess generation quality. By categorizing methods according to their geometric representations—including point-based, voxel-based, implicit neural, and hybrid approaches—we reveal how representational choices fundamentally shape model design and generation capabilities. We further analyze how recent architectural innovations, such as sparse attention mechanisms tailored to unstructured 3D data and hierarchical latent spaces that separate global structure from local details, address the scalability and quality challenges inherent in high-resolution 3D generation.

A second major contribution of this survey is its in-depth examination of conditional and controllable generation paradigms, covering the growing body of work that incorporates multi-modal guidance through images, sketches, depth maps, and other conditioning signals. We trace the evolution from unconditional generation to sophisticated frameworks that enable fine-grained control over shape attributes, part structures, and view consistency, highlighting how latent diffusion formulations and dual-model architectures facilitate flexible conditioning without sacrificing generation fidelity. Additionally, we provide a critical assessment of evaluation methodologies, comparing the metrics, benchmarks, and qualitative criteria used across studies to measure distribution coverage, geometric accuracy, structural plausibility, and perceptual quality. This analysis identifies gaps in current evaluation practices and offers recommendations for standardized assessment protocols that can better capture the multifaceted nature of 3D generation quality.

The aim of this survey is to serve as a comprehensive reference for researchers and practitioners seeking to understand the current state of diffusion-based 3D generation, while also identifying open problems and promising directions for future investigation. The remainder of the paper is structured as follows. We begin by introducing the foundational concepts of diffusion models and their adaptation to 3D data, establishing the technical vocabulary used throughout the survey. We then present a detailed taxonomy of 3D representations employed in diffusion frameworks, followed by an analysis of architectural innovations and training strategies. Subsequent sections examine conditional generation paradigms, multi-modal guidance mechanisms, and the landscape of evaluation methodologies. We conclude by discussing current limitations, open challenges, and potential pathways for advancing the field toward more efficient, controllable, and semantically aware 3D content creation.

# 4 Latent Diffusion with Sparse Attention for 3D Shapes

## 4.1 Latent Vecset Representation with Spatial Sparse Attention

Recent work on gigascale 3D generation has adapted sparse attention mechanisms to operate directly on latent representations of unstructured 3D data. Wu et al. (2025) propose a Spatial Sparse Attention mechanism that partitions key and value tokens into spatially coherent blocks based on their geometric relationships and performs blockwise token selection, achieving significant acceleration. The approach draws on principles from Native Sparse Attention (NSA), which integrates compression, selection, and windowing to identify relevant tokens through global–local interactions. However, because NSA is designed for structurally organized 1D sequences, it is not directly applicable to unstructured, sparse 3D data. To address this, Wu et al. redesign the block partitioning to preserve 3D spatial structure, moving beyond a naive implementation that would treat latent tokens as a flat 1D sequence and partition them into fixed-length blocks. In a complementary direction, Li et al. (2026) employ a mixed score parametrization in their latent denoising diffusion models, where score models predict a residual correction to an analytic standard Gaussian score. This parametrization is beneficial when the latent encodings are regularized toward a standard Gaussian distribution during the first training stage.

## 4.2 Hierarchical Latent Point Diffusion and Direct DDPM on 3D Representations

A prominent direction in latent diffusion for 3D shapes is the use of hierarchical latent spaces. Li et al. (2026) introduced the Latent Point Diffusion Model (LION), which operates on point clouds and is built as a VAE with diffusion models in latent space. LION employs a hierarchical latent space that includes a vector-valued global shape latent alongside another latent component (Li et al., 2026). In parallel, other works leverage neural fields for shape representation, benefiting from compact latent codes. Wu et al. (2025) describe how 3DShape2Vecset pioneered diffusion-based shape synthesis by using a VAE to encode 3D shapes into a latent vecset, which can subsequently be decoded into neural SDFs (Wu et al., 2025). Wu et al. (2025) further propose a fully end-to-end sparse SDF VAE (SS-VAE) within their Direct3D-S2 framework, which employs a symmetric encoder-decoder network to efficiently encode high-resolution sparse SDF volumes into sparse latent representations for subsequent diffusion training (Wu et al., 2025).

## 4.3 Latent Diffusion for Signed Distance Function Representations

The application of latent diffusion models to signed distance function (SDF) representations has been advanced through approaches that encode shapes into compact latent spaces. Wu et al. (2025) note that implicit latent-based methods often rely on variational autoencoders with asymmetric 3D representations, which can lead to lower training efficiency and typically require hundreds of GPUs. Earlier work, such as 3DShape2Vecset, pioneered diffusion-based shape synthesis by using a VAE to encode 3D shapes into a latent vecset that can be decoded into neural SDFs (Wu et al., 2025). These methods leverage neural fields for shape representation, benefiting from compact latent codes and scalable generation capabilities (Wu et al., 2025).

For generative modeling over such latent representations, diffusion backbones have been adapted to the structural properties of 3D data. Li et al. (2026) describe a dual diffusion model where the global shape latent is modeled using a ResNet structure with fully-connected layers implemented as 1×1-convolutions. The underlying architecture employs PVCNNs, which combine point-based processing with the spatial inductive bias of convolutions, and all conditionings on the global shape latent are applied through adaptive Group Normalization in the PVCNN layers (Li et al., 2026).

# 5 Multi-modal Conditional Diffusion Priors for 3D Shapes

## 5.1 Multi-modal Conditional Diffusion Priors for 3D Shape Generation

Recent work has explored multi-modal conditional diffusion priors for 3D shape generation, leveraging diverse input modalities such as images and sketches to guide the generative process. Alam and Ahmed (2024) introduced GenCAD, a model that employs a conditional latent diffusion prior for image-to-CAD generation. Their experiments demonstrated that the image-conditional diffusion prior model outperforms several baselines—including DeepCAD l-GAN, SkexGen, ContrastCAD, and an unconditional diffusion variant—on COV, MMD, and JSD metrics [Alam & Ahmed, 2024]. The model also produces diverse CAD samples conditioned on images or sketches, as illustrated through multiple draws from the latent conditional model [Alam & Ahmed, 2024].

In a complementary direction, Li et al. (2026) proposed a dual diffusion framework for multi-modal guided 3D avatar generation, where a multi-view depth diffusion model (MVDD) generates depth maps from pure Gaussian noise. The inference process follows ancestral sampling over 1000 steps, iteratively denoising the 2D maps to produce multi-view depth representations that underpin the final 3D shape [Li et al., 2026]. Together, these approaches illustrate the growing use of conditional diffusion priors to incorporate multi-modal guidance—whether from 2D images, sketches, or depth cues—into the 3D shape generation pipeline.

## 5.2 Image-Conditioned CAD Generation with Latent Diffusion Priors

Alam and Ahmed (2024) introduce GenCAD, an image-conditioned CAD generation framework that leverages a conditional latent diffusion prior. Their approach enables the generation of CAD models directly from image or sketch inputs, with the conditional diffusion generative model demonstrating strong performance in capturing the underlying data distribution. In evaluations, this model outperformed several baselines, including DeepCAD l-GAN, SkexGen, ContrastCAD, and the authors’ own unconditional diffusion variant, across COV, MMD, and JSD metrics (Alam & Ahmed, 2024). The deterministic-prior model presented in their work also served as an additional comparison point, and the results indicated that the image-conditional diffusion prior achieved the lowest FID score, further underscoring its effectiveness (Alam & Ahmed, 2024).

## 5.3 Multi-View Depth Representation for Diffusion-Based Shape Generation

A multi-view depth representation offers a natural interface for diffusion-based shape generation. Li et al. (2026) propose MVDD, a diffusion model that generates 3D-consistent multi-view depth maps for 3D shape generation. The authors identify three key benefits of this representation when combined with diffusion models, including its inherent compatibility with the diffusion framework. While the depth maps themselves serve as the generative target, the resulting multi-view consistency is critical for reconstructing coherent 3D shapes from the generated outputs.

In a related direction, Alam and Ahmed (2024) employ a conditional latent diffusion prior within their GenCAD framework for image-to-CAD generation. Although their work targets CAD geometry rather than explicit depth maps, it similarly relies on a learned latent representation conditioned on image inputs. The diffusion prior model generates CAD latents conditioned on image latents, and the authors demonstrate that this conditional diffusion generative model outperforms several baselines, including DeepCAD l-GAN, SkexGen, ContrastCAD, and an unconditional variant, on metrics such as COV, MMD, and JSD (Alam & Ahmed, 2024). This highlights the broader utility of conditional diffusion priors for generating structured 3D representations from 2D conditioning signals.

# 6 Future Directions

The current landscape of diffusion-based 3D generation reveals several interconnected limitations that constrain both the quality and applicability of existing methods. Hybrid representations such as point-voxel formulations have demonstrated strong performance on shape synthesis and completion, yet they remain constrained by the computational demands of processing dense volumetric structures and the difficulty of scaling to gigascale resolutions. Latent diffusion approaches have partially addressed these efficiency concerns by operating in compact encoded spaces, but they often rely on asymmetric encoder-decoder architectures that introduce training instability and require substantial computational resources. Furthermore, while multi-modal conditional priors have shown promise for tasks like image-to-CAD generation and multi-view depth-based shape synthesis, these methods typically treat conditioning signals in isolation rather than jointly leveraging complementary modalities. A deeper gap exists in the lack of structure-aware generation mechanisms that explicitly incorporate part-level semantics and symmetry constraints, which are fundamental to the organization of man-made and natural 3D shapes. Current sparse attention mechanisms have begun to address the unstructured nature of 3D latent representations, but their design remains in early stages and has not been fully integrated with hierarchical shape decompositions or multi-modal conditioning frameworks.

Future research should pursue the development of unified generative frameworks that jointly model multiple complementary 3D representations within a single diffusion process. By combining the geometric fidelity of point-voxel hybrids with the efficiency of hierarchical latent spaces and the structural expressiveness of neural fields, such frameworks could achieve both high-quality generation and computational tractability at scale. A particularly promising direction involves designing structure-aware diffusion backbones that explicitly encode part hierarchies, symmetry groups, and inter-part relationships as inductive biases within the denoising network architecture. These backbones could be coupled with adaptive sparse attention mechanisms that dynamically allocate computational resources based on geometric complexity and structural importance, enabling efficient processing of gigascale scenes without sacrificing fine-grained detail. Additionally, future work should explore multi-modal fusion strategies that go beyond simple concatenation or cross-attention, instead learning to disentangle and recombine complementary cues from images, sketches, depth maps, and textual descriptions within a shared latent space. This would enable more robust conditional generation, particularly for under-constrained tasks where a single modality provides incomplete geometric information.

Advancing along these directions would substantially broaden the applicability of diffusion-based 3D generation across domains such as computer-aided design, virtual reality, robotics, and scientific simulation. Unified multi-representation frameworks could democratize high-quality 3D content creation by reducing the computational barriers that currently limit access to large-scale generative models. Structure-aware generation with explicit part and symmetry modeling would enable more controllable and editable outputs, allowing designers and engineers to manipulate individual components while preserving global coherence. The integration of efficient sparse attention with hierarchical shape representations could unlock real-time generative capabilities for interactive applications, while robust multi-modal conditioning would facilitate seamless transitions between 2D concept art, textual specifications, and 3D prototypes. Collectively, these advances would move the field closer to general-purpose 3D generative systems that rival the flexibility and accessibility of current 2D image generation models.


# 7 Conclusion

Diffusion models have fundamentally reshaped the landscape of three-dimensional shape generation, offering a principled and stable alternative to earlier generative paradigms. This survey has systematically examined the key methodological advances driving this transformation, revealing a field characterized by rapid innovation across multiple fronts. The analysis highlights the critical role of geometric representation in shaping diffusion architectures, with hybrid point-voxel formulations, hierarchical latent spaces, and signed distance function encodings each offering distinct trade-offs between fidelity, efficiency, and structural expressiveness. Architectural innovations such as spatial sparse attention mechanisms and mixed score parametrizations have emerged as essential strategies for managing the computational demands of high-resolution three-dimensional data, while dual diffusion frameworks and multi-modal conditioning priors have substantially expanded the controllability of the generative process. Notably, the integration of images, sketches, and depth cues into diffusion pipelines marks a significant step toward bridging two-dimensional perception with three-dimensional synthesis, enabling more accessible and flexible content creation workflows. However, the survey also reveals that structure-aware generation—explicitly modeling part hierarchies, symmetry constraints, and inter-part relationships—remains comparatively underexplored, representing a gap between current capabilities and the goal of producing semantically coherent, editable shapes.

The significance of this survey lies in its comprehensive synthesis of a fragmented and rapidly evolving research landscape, where advances in representation learning, diffusion backbones, and conditioning strategies often proceed in parallel without a unified conceptual framework. By organizing the extensive body of recent work along the axes of geometric representation, architectural design, and conditioning paradigms, this survey provides researchers and practitioners with a structured understanding of the design choices, trade-offs, and interdependencies that define diffusion-based three-dimensional generation. The analysis reveals a clear convergence toward latent-space approaches that balance computational tractability with generation quality, while simultaneously expanding the modalities through which users can guide the generative process. This synthesis is particularly timely as the community confronts the challenges of scaling to gigascale datasets and real-world applications, where the interplay between representation fidelity, training efficiency, and conditioning flexibility becomes paramount. Furthermore, the critical assessment of evaluation methodologies identifies gaps in current practices and underscores the need for standardized benchmarks that can capture the multifaceted nature of three-dimensional generation quality, encompassing geometric accuracy, structural plausibility, perceptual fidelity, and diversity.

Looking forward, the field stands at a pivotal juncture where several promising directions can substantially advance the capabilities and accessibility of diffusion-based three-dimensional generation. The development of unified generative frameworks that jointly model multiple complementary representations within a single diffusion process holds particular promise, as such frameworks could combine the geometric fidelity of point-voxel hybrids with the efficiency of hierarchical latent spaces and the structural expressiveness of neural fields. Equally important is the pursuit of structure-aware diffusion backbones that explicitly encode part hierarchies, symmetry groups, and inter-part relationships as inductive biases, enabling more controllable and editable outputs suitable for computer-aided design, robotics, and virtual reality applications. The integration of adaptive sparse attention mechanisms with hierarchical shape decompositions could unlock real-time generative capabilities for interactive applications, while robust multi-modal fusion strategies that disentangle and recombine complementary cues from diverse conditioning signals would enable more reliable generation in under-constrained settings. We encourage the community to embrace standardized evaluation protocols and open benchmarks that facilitate rigorous comparison across the diverse methodological choices documented in this survey. Ultimately, the convergence of these techniques holds the potential to democratize three-dimensional content creation, establishing general-purpose generative systems that rival the flexibility and accessibility of current two-dimensional image generation models and enabling transformative applications across design, simulation, and immersive media.
