# 1 Abstract

Diffusion models have achieved remarkable success in image generation, yet their extension to three-dimensional shape synthesis introduces fundamental challenges arising from the irregular structure, permutation invariance, and binary nature of native 3D representations such as point clouds and voxel grids. This survey provides a systematic review of recent advances in diffusion-based 3D generation, with a particular emphasis on the evolution from combined point-voxel representations to hierarchical latent diffusion and structure-aware architectures. We examine key methodological developments, including point-voxel hybrid models that overcome individual representation limitations, equivariant formulations that leverage geometric symmetry priors, spatial sparse attention mechanisms enabling efficient high-resolution processing, and latent diffusion frameworks operating on compact neural field encodings. Our analysis reveals a clear trajectory toward generation frameworks that respect underlying shape structure while achieving computational efficiency, with latent-space operations and structural priors emerging as critical design principles. The survey further highlights the growing adoption of conditional generation paradigms—such as image-conditioned CAD generation and multi-view depth-based synthesis—that bridge advances in 2D diffusion with 3D content creation. By synthesizing the current state of the field, this review identifies persistent challenges in adapting diffusion models to irregular 3D structures and outlines promising directions for future research in symmetry-guided and efficient 3D generative modeling.


# 2 Introduction

The generation of three-dimensional digital content has become a cornerstone of modern computer graphics, computer vision, and artificial intelligence, driven by an ever-increasing demand for realistic virtual environments, digital twins, and automated design tools. From immersive video games and cinematic visual effects to robotic simulation and computer-aided design, the ability to synthesize high-quality 3D shapes and scenes is essential. Unlike two-dimensional image generation, which operates on regular pixel grids and has seen remarkable progress through deep generative models, 3D content creation must contend with fundamentally more complex data structures. Three-dimensional objects can be represented in numerous forms, including point clouds, voxel grids, polygonal meshes, tetrahedral decompositions, implicit neural fields, and procedural CAD sequences, each possessing unique geometric and topological properties. The recent emergence of diffusion models, which learn to reverse a gradual data-destroying noising process, has opened a transformative new chapter in generative modeling. Extending these powerful probabilistic frameworks from the 2D image domain to the intricate world of 3D geometry represents a natural yet profoundly challenging frontier, promising to unlock unprecedented levels of fidelity, diversity, and controllability in automated 3D synthesis.

The adaptation of diffusion models to 3D shape and scene generation is fraught with fundamental obstacles that distinguish it sharply from the well-trodden path of image synthesis. A primary difficulty lies in the inherent incompatibility between standard diffusion formulations, which assume continuous data distributions on regular grids, and the irregular, discrete, or permutation-invariant nature of common 3D representations. Naively applying diffusion to binary voxel grids ignores the continuous probability densities central to the framework, while point cloud representations demand permutation invariance that is difficult to enforce within a denoising trajectory. Mesh and tetrahedral structures introduce further complications through irregular connectivity and complex topological relationships. Beyond these structural mismatches, the computational burden of processing high-resolution 3D data is immense, as volumetric representations scale cubically with resolution, quickly exhausting memory and compute resources. Moreover, 3D shapes lack a canonical orientation, necessitating geometric equivariance in generative models to ensure that transformations of the input do not arbitrarily alter the output. The sheer diversity of 3D representations also fragments the field, requiring bespoke modeling strategies, conditioning mechanisms, and evaluation protocols for each modality, thereby hindering the development of unified and generalizable generation frameworks.

This survey provides a comprehensive and structured examination of the rapidly evolving landscape of diffusion models for 3D shape and scene generation, with a central focus on the critical interplay between methodological design choices and the underlying data representations. Our primary contribution is the formulation of a clear taxonomy that categorizes existing approaches based on how they handle the fundamental challenges of 3D data. This taxonomy encompasses point-voxel hybrid models that synergistically combine complementary representations to overcome individual limitations, hierarchical latent diffusion frameworks that achieve tractable generation by operating on compact encodings of neural fields, equivariant architectures that embed geometric symmetry priors directly into the denoising process, and multi-view conditioning strategies that bridge the gap between advanced 2D diffusion models and 3D content creation. By systematically analyzing these categories, we elucidate the trade-offs between generation fidelity, sample diversity, and computational efficiency, revealing how the choice of representation dictates architectural constraints and training objectives. This analysis traces a clear trajectory from early, representation-specific models toward more sophisticated frameworks that respect the intrinsic structure of 3D shapes while achieving scalability.

A second key contribution of this work is a thorough investigation of the evaluation methodologies and conditional generation paradigms that are shaping the field. We critically assess the metrics employed to quantify generation quality, such as coverage, minimum matching distance, and Jensen-Shannon divergence, discussing their applicability and limitations across different 3D representations. Furthermore, we explore the diverse conditioning mechanisms that enable controllable synthesis, ranging from image-guided CAD model generation and partial shape completion to multi-view depth-based reconstruction. This examination reveals how different architectural designs support specific downstream tasks and highlights the growing convergence between 2D and 3D generative modeling. In addition to synthesizing the current state of the art, the survey identifies persistent open challenges, including the need for more computationally efficient training paradigms, the development of perceptual evaluation metrics that better align with human judgment, and the deeper integration of structural priors such as part-level relationships and explicit symmetry guidance. By providing this consolidated perspective, the survey serves as both a detailed reference for current methodologies and a roadmap for future research.

The aim of this survey is to provide researchers and practitioners with a structured and insightful overview of diffusion-based 3D generation, and the paper is organized to systematically guide the reader through the field’s foundational concepts, methodological innovations, and future potential. We begin by establishing the necessary background on diffusion models and the diverse landscape of 3D representations. The core of the survey is then dedicated to our proposed taxonomy, with dedicated sections examining point-voxel and mesh diffusion methods, latent diffusion frameworks employing sparse attention mechanisms, and multi-view image-conditioned approaches for CAD and avatar generation. For each category, we analyze representative architectures, discuss their innovations, and compare their performance characteristics. Subsequently, we review the evaluation protocols and benchmark datasets that are standard in the field, offering a critical perspective on current assessment practices. Finally, we conclude by identifying the most pressing open challenges and outlining promising research directions, charting a course toward more efficient, structure-aware, and generalizable 3D generative models.


# 3 Point-Voxel and Mesh Diffusion for 3D Shape Generation

## 3.1 Point-Voxel Diffusion for Shape Generation and Completion

Point-voxel diffusion models have emerged as a compelling approach for 3D shape generation and completion, addressing limitations observed when diffusion models are applied to pure voxel or point representations. Cheng et al. (2022) introduced Point-Voxel Diffusion (PVD), a denoising diffusion probabilistic model designed specifically for 3D point clouds. Their work was motivated by experimental evidence showing that straightforward extensions of diffusion models to either purely voxel-based or purely point-based representations lead to failures, whereas the combined point-voxel representation yields superior generative power and impressive shape completion quality (Cheng et al., 2022). The PVD framework formulates a training objective for unconditional shape generation and further proposes a modified objective tailored for shape completion from partial observations, distinguishing it from deterministic encoder-decoder baselines (Cheng et al., 2022). The broader context for such models is the growing importance of generative modeling for 3D shapes, including point clouds and meshes, where Gaussian diffusion models have demonstrated exceptional performance in image generation and are being adapted to the 3D domain (Wan et al., 2025).

## 3.2 Diffusion on Tetrahedral Grid and Mesh Representations

Applying diffusion models to 3D representations such as tetrahedral grids and meshes presents fundamental challenges distinct from those encountered with 2D images. Wan et al. (2025) note that, unlike pixel data, 3D data is characterized by a complex interplay of structural properties, and they draw an analogy linking the distribution of coordinates in point clouds or meshes to a noise distribution through the diffusion process. This highlights the need for specialized formulations when moving beyond regular grid structures. Similarly, Cheng et al. (2022) emphasize that a direct application of diffusion models to alternative 3D representations is highly nontrivial and results in poor generation quality. They specifically identify that pure voxel representations are binary and therefore unsuitable for the probabilistic nature of diffusion models, while point cloud representations demand permutation invariance, which imposes infeasible constraints on the modeling process. These observations collectively underscore the inherent difficulties in adapting standard diffusion frameworks to the irregular and discrete structures common in tetrahedral mesh and grid-based 3D data.

## 3.3 Structure-Aware Point Cloud Generation with Symmetry Guidance

Recent work on diffusion-based point cloud generation has begun to incorporate structural priors. Cheng et al. (2022) introduced Point-Voxel Diffusion (PVD), a denoising diffusion probabilistic model designed for 3D point cloud generation, reconstruction, and completion. Their formulation supports shape generation and a modified objective for shape completion from partial observations, evaluated on synthetic and real datasets including ShapeNet, PartNet, and single-view depth scans from the Redwood dataset. While the core PVD framework does not explicitly encode symmetry guidance, it establishes a diffusion pipeline that later structure-aware methods can build upon.

In parallel, Wan et al. (2025) proposed an equivariant diffusion model that leverages A5-group neurons to jointly estimate pose and reconstruct shape, facilitating conditional point cloud generation. Their experiments sample point clouds from ShapeNet, which contains 51,127 shapes across 55 categories, and apply standard point cloud generation metrics. The equivariant design inherently respects geometric transformations, a form of structural awareness that aligns with symmetry-guided generation principles. Together, these works illustrate a trajectory toward generation frameworks that respect underlying shape structure, though explicit symmetry-guidance mechanisms remain an area for further development.

# 4 Latent Diffusion with Sparse Attention for 3D Generation

## 4.1 Spatial Sparse Attention in Latent Vecset Diffusion

In the context of latent vecset diffusion, spatial sparse attention has been proposed to address the computational challenges of processing high-resolution 3D representations. Wu et al. (2025) introduce a Spatial Sparse Attention (SSA) mechanism within their image-conditioned diffusion transformer (SS-DiT) to substantially improve training and inference efficiency. The core idea is to partition key and value tokens into spatially coherent blocks based on their geometric relationships, followed by blockwise token selection, which achieves significant acceleration compared to dense attention. A naive implementation of this approach involves treating the latent tokens as a 1D sequence and partitioning them into fixed-length blocks, though the spatially informed grouping is the defining characteristic of the method.

The broader motivation for operating in a compressed latent space is echoed by Li et al. (2026), who note that latent diffusion models benefit from expressivity when a VAE first regularizes latent encodings to approximate a standard Gaussian distribution, simplifying the modeling task for the diffusion process. While Li et al. do not detail a sparse attention mechanism themselves, their framework underscores the value of efficient latent-space operations, which aligns with the efficiency gains sought by spatial sparse attention in architectures like the SS-DiT.

## 4.2 Hierarchical Latent Point Diffusion Models

Hierarchical latent point diffusion models build on the broader paradigm of latent diffusion, which was originally developed for images and has been adapted to 3D shape generation. As noted by Li et al. (2026), this framework first trains a variational autoencoder (VAE) that regularizes latent encodings to approximately follow a standard Gaussian distribution, thereby simplifying the modeling task for the subsequent diffusion process. This approach is characterized by its expressivity, as the diffusion model operates in a more tractable latent space.

In the context of 3D content, methods have leveraged neural fields for shape representation to benefit from compact latent codes and scalable generation. Wu et al. (2025) highlight that 3DShape2Vecset pioneered diffusion-based shape synthesis by employing a VAE to encode 3D shapes into a latent vecset, which can subsequently be decoded into neural signed distance functions. However, Wu et al. (2025) also note that implicit latent-based methods of this kind often depend on VAEs with asymmetric 3D representations, which can lead to lower training efficiency that typically requires hundreds of GPUs.

## 4.3 Latent Diffusion for SDF Representations

Recent work has begun to explore latent diffusion for signed distance function (SDF) representations by operating on compact latent codes derived from neural fields. Wu et al. (2025) note that such approaches benefit from scalable generation capabilities and highlight 3DShape2Vecset as a pioneering method that uses a Variational Autoencoder to encode 3D shapes into a latent vecset, which can subsequently be decoded into neural SDFs. Their own framework further develops this direction by employing a multi-resolution sparse SDF representation, where a sparse SDF variational autoencoder produces sparse latent representations that are then processed by a diffusion model conditioned on image features via cross-attention.

# 5 Multi-View Image-Conditioned Diffusion for CAD and Avatars

## 5.1 Image-Conditioned Latent Diffusion for CAD Generation

Alam and Ahmed (2024) introduced GenCAD, an image-conditioned CAD generation framework that employs a conditional latent diffusion model as a prior network to generate CAD latents conditioned on input image latents. A transformer-based decoder then constructs the CAD program from the generated CAD latent, producing not merely a 3D solid model but a complete CAD representation (Alam & Ahmed, 2024). Their conditional diffusion generative model outperformed several baselines, including DeepCAD l-GAN, SkexGen, ContrastCAD, and their own unconditional diffusion model, as measured by COV, MMD, and JSD metrics (Alam & Ahmed, 2024).

## 5.2 Multi-View Depth Diffusion for Shape Generation

Recent work has explored multi-view depth representations as a bridge between 2D diffusion models and 3D shape generation. Li et al. (2026) propose a diffusion model, MVDD, that generates 3D-consistent multi-view depth maps for shape generation. The authors highlight that a multi-view depth representation is naturally compatible with diffusion models, offering several advantages for the generation process.

# 6 Future Directions

The current landscape of point-voxel diffusion models reveals several persistent limitations that constrain their broader applicability and performance. A primary gap lies in the explicit integration of structural priors, such as symmetry and geometric equivariance, directly into the denoising process. While equivariant architectures have been explored for point cloud generation, their fusion with point-voxel hybrid representations remains underexplored, limiting the ability to generate shapes that are both high-fidelity and structurally coherent under transformations. Furthermore, the computational demands of operating on high-resolution 3D data, even within compressed latent spaces, continue to pose significant efficiency bottlenecks. Existing sparse attention mechanisms offer speedups but are often decoupled from the hierarchical nature of 3D geometry, missing opportunities to model shapes from coarse global structures to fine local details. Finally, the challenge of generating complete, editable 3D representations—such as CAD programs or temporally consistent dynamic shapes—from partial or multi-view observations remains largely unaddressed by current unconditional or single-modality conditional diffusion frameworks.

Future research should pursue the deep integration of symmetry-aware denoising processes within point-voxel architectures. This involves designing diffusion models where the reverse process is guided by learned, group-equivariant operators that explicitly enforce geometric constraints, ensuring that generated shapes respect canonical orientations and part-level relationships without sacrificing the generative diversity offered by hybrid representations. Concurrently, a new class of hierarchical latent diffusion models should be developed that inherently leverage the multi-scale nature of point-voxel structures. By structuring the latent space into a coarse-to-fine hierarchy and pairing it with a geometry-aware sparse attention mechanism, future models can dramatically reduce computational overhead while improving the capture of both macroscopic topology and microscopic surface details. Another critical direction is the advancement of multi-modal conditional generation that moves beyond static shape synthesis toward editable and programmatic 3D content creation. This entails designing unified frameworks where a single diffusion prior can be conditioned on diverse signals—such as multi-view images, text descriptions, and partial scans—to generate not only point clouds but also editable CAD sequences and dynamic 4D representations, bridging the gap between generative modeling and practical design workflows.

The pursuit of these directions promises to fundamentally elevate the role of diffusion models in 3D computer vision and graphics. By embedding structural and hierarchical reasoning directly into the generative process, future models will produce shapes of unprecedented geometric fidelity and semantic consistency, making them viable for downstream tasks in robotics, simulation, and autonomous driving where structural integrity is paramount. The development of efficient, geometry-aware architectures will democratize access to high-quality 3D generation, enabling complex content creation on commodity hardware. Most significantly, unifying diverse conditioning modalities within editable and programmatic generation pipelines will transform diffusion models from tools of mere synthesis into intelligent co-design systems, accelerating workflows in industrial design, architecture, and immersive virtual world creation.


# 7 Conclusion

This survey has systematically examined the evolution of diffusion models for 3D shape generation, tracing the trajectory from early point-voxel hybrid approaches to contemporary latent-space and structure-aware architectures. The analysis reveals that the direct application of standard diffusion frameworks to pure voxel or point representations encounters fundamental obstacles, including the binary nature of voxel grids and the stringent permutation invariance requirements of point clouds. Point-voxel diffusion models successfully circumvent these limitations by unifying complementary representations, thereby achieving superior generative fidelity and enabling robust shape completion from partial observations. The incorporation of geometric priors through equivariant architectures represents a further critical advance, allowing models to inherently respect spatial transformations and structural symmetries. Concurrently, the development of spatial sparse attention mechanisms and hierarchical latent diffusion frameworks operating on compact neural field encodings has substantially mitigated the computational burdens associated with high-resolution 3D generation. The extension of these methods to image-conditioned CAD generation and multi-view depth-based synthesis demonstrates the adaptability of diffusion-based paradigms across diverse 3D modalities, bridging advances in 2D generative modeling with the demands of 3D content creation.

The significance of this survey resides in its structured synthesis of a rapidly maturing field at the intersection of generative modeling and 3D computer vision. By articulating a clear taxonomy that encompasses point-voxel hybrid models, latent diffusion frameworks, equivariant architectures, and multi-view conditioning strategies, the discussion illuminates the critical design decisions that govern the trade-offs between generation quality, computational efficiency, and representational flexibility. This consolidation of methodological advances offers researchers and practitioners a coherent framework for understanding how different representation choices influence model behavior and for identifying underexplored opportunities, such as the explicit integration of symmetry-guidance mechanisms within point-voxel denoising processes and the development of geometry-aware sparse attention for hierarchical latent spaces. The survey further underscores the importance of rigorous evaluation protocols and the need for metrics that better capture perceptual quality and structural coherence, providing a foundation for more principled comparative analyses in future work.

The field now stands at a pivotal juncture where foundational challenges have been clearly delineated and partially resolved, yet substantial opportunities for innovation persist. Future research should prioritize the development of unified generative frameworks that seamlessly integrate structural priors, equivariance properties, and computational efficiency, enabling the synthesis of shapes that are simultaneously high-fidelity, geometrically consistent, and editable. The extension of diffusion-based methods to dynamic 3D content, including temporally coherent mesh sequences and animated point clouds, represents a natural and impactful progression that will broaden the applicability of these techniques to simulation, robotics, and immersive media. Furthermore, the deployment of these models in domains demanding precise geometric constraints—such as computer-aided design, medical imaging, and autonomous perception—requires rigorous validation against standardized benchmarks and the establishment of reproducible evaluation practices. As the community advances toward these goals, a concerted commitment to open-source implementations and transparent reporting will be essential for translating theoretical innovations into practical tools that empower the broader scientific and engineering communities.
