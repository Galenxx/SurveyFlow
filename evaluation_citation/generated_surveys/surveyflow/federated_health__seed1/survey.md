# 1 Abstract

Federated learning has emerged as a transformative paradigm for collaborative model training across distributed data sources, offering a compelling solution to the privacy and regulatory challenges that have traditionally hindered centralized machine learning in sensitive domains such as healthcare. This survey systematically examines recent advances at the intersection of communication efficiency and privacy preservation in federated learning, with a particular focus on techniques tailored to large language models and medical imaging applications. We review layer-skipping strategies that selectively freeze and transmit only subsets of model parameters to drastically reduce bandwidth requirements while preserving pre-trained knowledge, and analyze intention-hiding mechanisms in vertical federated learning that conceal participant identities to prevent the inference of sensitive business or operational intentions. The survey further explores the adaptation of federated learning to multi-institutional chest radiograph analysis, highlighting decentralized frameworks that integrate privacy-preserving protocols directly into collaborative radiological model training, and examines emerging data pricing mechanisms that embed privacy protection into the valuation of client contributions. Our synthesis reveals that parameter-efficient update strategies offer viable trade-offs between communication cost and model fidelity, while intention-hiding protocols and privacy-preserving pricing frameworks address critical gaps in vertical and incentive-driven federated settings. These findings collectively underscore the growing maturity of federated learning techniques for resource-constrained and privacy-sensitive applications, while identifying open challenges in adaptive layer selection, formalized privacy guarantees, and the holistic integration of communication efficiency, privacy protection, and incentive alignment.


# 2 Introduction

The rapid digitization of healthcare systems has led to an unprecedented accumulation of sensitive patient data, ranging from electronic health records and clinical notes to medical imaging and genomic profiles. This wealth of information holds immense potential for advancing clinical decision-making, accelerating drug discovery, and enabling personalized medicine through the application of machine learning and artificial intelligence. However, the inherently sensitive nature of healthcare data imposes stringent regulatory and ethical constraints on its use, with frameworks such as the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act in the United States and the General Data Protection Regulation in Europe mandating rigorous privacy protections. Traditional centralized machine learning paradigms, which require aggregating data in a single repository for model training, are fundamentally at odds with these privacy requirements and often encounter institutional resistance, legal barriers, and logistical challenges in data sharing across hospitals and research centers. Federated learning has emerged as a transformative paradigm that addresses this tension by enabling collaborative model training without the need to transfer raw data across institutional boundaries. In this decentralized approach, only model updates—such as gradients or parameters—are shared between participating clients and a central server, thereby preserving data locality and reducing the risk of privacy breaches. The application of federated learning to healthcare has consequently attracted significant research interest, promising to unlock the potential of distributed clinical data while upholding the highest standards of patient confidentiality.

Despite its promise, the deployment of federated learning in real-world healthcare settings is confronted by several interrelated challenges that span technical, operational, and regulatory domains. One of the most pressing issues is the communication overhead associated with transmitting model updates, particularly when large-scale architectures such as large language models and deep convolutional neural networks are employed for tasks like clinical text analysis and medical image interpretation. The bandwidth constraints typical of hospital networks, combined with the sheer size of modern models, can render naive federated training protocols prohibitively slow and costly. Beyond communication efficiency, the heterogeneity of data distributions across institutions—arising from differences in patient demographics, imaging equipment, and clinical coding practices—poses significant obstacles to model convergence and generalization. Privacy vulnerabilities further complicate the landscape, as sophisticated attacks can potentially infer sensitive patient information from shared model updates, and even the mere participation of an institution in a federated training process may reveal strategic or operational intentions. Additionally, the translation of federated learning from experimental benchmarks to clinically deployed systems requires addressing practical concerns such as incentive design for participating institutions, integration with existing hospital information systems, and the development of robust evaluation frameworks that account for the unique constraints of decentralized training.

This survey makes several distinct contributions to the growing body of literature on federated learning for healthcare. First, it provides a comprehensive and structured taxonomy of the architectural designs that underpin privacy-preserving federated systems, encompassing both horizontal and vertical partitioning schemes as well as decentralized and peer-to-peer topologies. By systematically categorizing existing approaches, the paper clarifies the design space and highlights the trade-offs between model performance, communication cost, and privacy guarantees that practitioners must navigate. Special attention is devoted to intention-hiding mechanisms in vertical federated learning, which protect the identities and participation status of collaborating parties, and to the emerging role of parameter-efficient fine-tuning strategies that selectively freeze model layers to drastically reduce communication overhead without sacrificing the representational power of pre-trained models.

Second, this survey offers an in-depth analysis of communication-efficient training techniques tailored to the healthcare domain, with a particular focus on layer-skipping strategies and selective attention mechanisms. By examining how freezing a subset of model layers during federated fine-tuning can preserve pre-trained knowledge while minimizing bandwidth requirements, the paper illuminates a practical pathway for deploying large language models in resource-constrained clinical environments. The discussion extends to the applicability of these techniques across diverse healthcare tasks, including clinical named entity recognition, summarization of patient notes, and multi-institutional chest radiograph analysis. Furthermore, the survey addresses the critical but often underexplored topic of data valuation and incentive design, presenting frameworks that integrate privacy-preserving protocols into the pricing of client contributions and thereby foster sustainable federated ecosystems.

The primary aim of this survey is to serve as a structured guide for researchers, clinicians, and system architects seeking to understand and implement federated learning in healthcare contexts. The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. We begin by establishing foundational concepts in federated learning and healthcare privacy, after which we present a detailed taxonomy of architectures and communication-efficient methods. Subsequent sections examine clinical deployment considerations, including data heterogeneity, regulatory compliance, and integration pathways, before concluding with a discussion of open challenges and future research directions.


# 3 Layer-Skipping and Selective Attention in Federated Clinical Text Models

## 3.1 Layer-Skipping Strategies for Federated LLMs

Layer-skipping strategies reduce the communication cost of federated learning for large language models by transmitting updates for only a subset of the model’s parameters while keeping other layers frozen. Wen et al. (2024) propose an approach in which a majority of layers in pre-trained LLMs are selectively frozen during federated fine-tuning; only the unfrozen subset of parameters is communicated, which dramatically lowers bandwidth requirements while preserving the knowledge encoded in the frozen layers from pre-training. Their implementation and evaluation use the LLaMA model family. In a related line of work, Tang et al. (2023) include a static layer-skipping baseline, termed Layer-Skipping FL, that freezes the bottom layers of the model and transmits only the remaining updates. This method is compared against standard FedAvg with full model updates, the parameter-efficient FedPepTAO method, and a centralized training upper bound.

## 3.2 Selective Attention Mechanisms for Clinical Text Classification

Tang et al. (2023) suggest that selective attention mechanisms could benefit healthcare tasks in federated learning beyond text classification, such as named entity recognition or summarization of clinical notes, where identifying critical information while ignoring sensitive details is important. However, the provided evidence does not detail specific implementations of selective attention for clinical text classification itself.

# 4 Privacy-Preserving Vertical Federated Learning with Quantum Medical Architectures

## 4.1 Intention-Hiding and Contribution-Aware Vertical Federated Learning

In vertical federated learning (VFL), participants typically know which other parties are involved in the training process, which can leak sensitive business or operational intentions. To address this, Rajagopal et al. (2023) proposed and characterized the concept of intention-hiding vertical federated learning (IHVFL). Compared to traditional VFL, IHVFL satisfies an additional privacy requirement by concealing the identities and participation status of the collaborating parties, thereby preventing the inference of a participant’s intent from the mere fact of their involvement. The authors further enhanced privacy-preserving feature engineering and introduced a new private set intersection (PSI) protocol as part of this framework (Rajagopal et al., 2023). The available evidence does not detail the specific mechanics of the PSI protocol or how contribution awareness is explicitly integrated into the IHVFL design.

## 4.2 Decentralized Frameworks for Multi-Institutional Chest Radiograph Analysis

**4.2 Decentralized Frameworks for Multi-Institutional Chest Radiograph Analysis**

Federated learning (FL) has been applied to medical imaging tasks to enable collaborative model training without centralizing sensitive data. Rajagopal et al. (2023) note that Roth et al. (2020) employed an FL framework for breast density classification, achieving performance superior to standalone learning approaches. While this work demonstrates the feasibility of decentralized learning in radiological contexts, the evidence does not specify the chest radiograph datasets or pathologies involved in that study. In a more targeted effort, Serrano et al. (2026) introduced FedIHRAS, a privacy-preserving FL framework designed explicitly for multi-institutional collaborative radiological analysis. Their framework was evaluated on the ChestX-ray14 dataset, which contains images labeled for 14 common thoracic pathologies using automated natural language processing (Serrano et al., 2026). The available evidence indicates that decentralized frameworks are being extended to chest radiograph analysis with integrated privacy mechanisms, though the specific performance metrics and architectural details of FedIHRAS are not detailed in the provided excerpts.

# 5 Federated Learning for IoT-Enabled ECG Decision Making and Data Pricing

## 5.1 Data Pricing Mechanisms for Privacy-Preserved Valuation

Rajagopal et al. (2023) propose a two-phase data pricing framework that integrates privacy protection directly into the valuation process. The first phase computes a data value score for each client, and to prevent the leakage of sensitive client information during this step, the authors design a privacy-preserving protocol that enables the secured sharing of private distributions with the server. The second phase then determines the actual payment for the selected clients through a budget-constrained pricing mechanism (Rajagopal et al., 2023).

# 6 Future Directions

The current landscape of federated learning for healthcare and large language models reveals several critical limitations. Layer-skipping strategies, while effective in reducing communication overhead, have been explored only in narrow contexts with static freezing policies, leaving adaptive or dynamic layer selection mechanisms entirely unexplored. The potential to balance communication efficiency with model performance across diverse healthcare tasks such as clinical named entity recognition, patient note summarization, and multimodal analysis remains an open question. In vertical federated learning, the intention-hiding framework introduces a novel privacy dimension, yet the underlying mechanisms for contribution awareness and private set intersection protocols lack detailed specification and rigorous security analysis. Decentralized frameworks for chest radiograph analysis have demonstrated feasibility, but the evidence base is fragmented, with limited cross-comparison of privacy mechanisms, performance benchmarks, and architectural choices across different institutions and datasets. Furthermore, the integration of privacy-preserving data pricing with federated training workflows remains in its infancy, with no established methodologies for dynamically valuing contributions while maintaining strict privacy guarantees throughout the entire pipeline.

Future research should pursue several interconnected directions to address these gaps. Adaptive layer-skipping mechanisms should be developed that dynamically determine which layers to freeze or communicate based on task complexity, data heterogeneity, and privacy constraints. Such mechanisms could leverage reinforcement learning or meta-learning to optimize the trade-off between communication cost and model utility in real time, extending beyond text classification to named entity recognition, clinical note summarization, and multimodal healthcare tasks. The intention-hiding vertical federated learning framework requires rigorous formalization, including complete specification of the private set intersection protocol, threat models, and contribution awareness mechanisms. Researchers should also investigate how intention hiding interacts with differential privacy guarantees and explore composability theorems that quantify cumulative privacy loss when multiple protection mechanisms are combined. Comprehensive benchmarking efforts are needed for decentralized chest radiograph analysis, establishing standardized evaluation protocols that compare heterogeneous federated learning architectures, self-supervised pretraining strategies, and privacy-preserving techniques across diverse multi-institutional datasets. Finally, the two-phase data pricing framework should be extended to support dynamic and fair valuation in streaming healthcare environments, where data contributions evolve over time and clients may join or leave the federation. Integrating pricing mechanisms directly with adaptive layer-skipping and intention-hiding protocols could yield holistic frameworks that simultaneously optimize communication efficiency, privacy protection, and incentive alignment.

The proposed research directions hold transformative potential for real-world healthcare federated learning deployments. Adaptive layer-skipping could enable resource-constrained hospitals and clinics to participate in collaborative model training without requiring high-bandwidth infrastructure, democratizing access to advanced AI capabilities. Formalized intention-hiding protocols would allow competing healthcare institutions to collaborate on sensitive tasks such as rare disease diagnosis without revealing their strategic interests or patient populations, fostering trust and broader participation. Standardized benchmarks for chest radiograph analysis would accelerate progress by enabling reproducible comparisons and identifying which privacy-utility trade-offs are acceptable in clinical practice. Integrating privacy-preserving data pricing with federated training would create sustainable ecosystems where data contributors are fairly compensated, incentivizing long-term participation and high-quality data sharing. Collectively, these advances could bridge the gap between theoretical federated learning research and practical, privacy-compliant healthcare AI systems deployed at scale.


# 7 Conclusion

This survey has systematically examined the convergence of communication-efficient and privacy-preserving techniques in federated learning, with a particular emphasis on their application to large language models and healthcare domains. The analysis of layer-skipping strategies reveals that selectively freezing and transmitting only subsets of model parameters offers a viable pathway for drastically reducing bandwidth requirements while preserving the representational power of pre-trained architectures. These techniques, ranging from static freezing of bottom layers to more adaptive configurations, demonstrate that communication efficiency need not come at the expense of model fidelity, particularly in resource-constrained clinical environments. In parallel, the review has traced the evolution of privacy paradigms from conventional vertical federated learning to intention-hiding frameworks that conceal participant identities, thereby addressing a critical and often overlooked vulnerability wherein the mere fact of collaboration can leak sensitive strategic or operational information. The survey further documents the successful adaptation of federated learning to multi-institutional chest radiograph analysis, where decentralized frameworks integrate privacy mechanisms directly into the collaborative training pipeline, and highlights the nascent integration of privacy-preserving data pricing models that embed valuation and incentive alignment into the federated workflow. Collectively, these findings illustrate a field in transition, moving from isolated technical contributions toward more holistic and interdependent design philosophies.

The significance of this survey resides in its integrated treatment of three dimensions that are frequently addressed in isolation: communication efficiency, privacy enhancement, and domain-specific deployment. By drawing explicit connections between layer-skipping methodologies, intention-hiding protocols, and practical healthcare applications, the paper offers a unified perspective on the challenges that must be navigated to realize federated learning at scale. The synthesis reveals that meaningful progress depends not on optimizing any single axis in isolation but on the co-design of model architecture, privacy guarantees, and incentive structures. For the healthcare domain in particular, the documented shift from generic federated averaging to specialized, privacy-preserving frameworks for radiological analysis signals the maturation of the field from theoretical exploration to domain-adapted engineering. This integrated view provides researchers and system architects with a structured understanding of the trade-offs involved and a foundation upon which to build more robust, trustworthy, and sustainable federated systems for sensitive and high-stakes applications.

Looking ahead, the community must move decisively beyond proof-of-concept demonstrations toward rigorous, reproducible benchmarks on diverse and heterogeneous real-world datasets. The gaps identified in this survey call for concerted effort: adaptive layer-skipping mechanisms that respond dynamically to task complexity and data heterogeneity, formalized intention-hiding protocols with complete threat models and composability guarantees, and comprehensive evaluation frameworks for decentralized medical imaging that enable meaningful cross-comparison of privacy-utility trade-offs. The integration of privacy-preserving data pricing with adaptive communication strategies represents a particularly promising frontier, one that could foster equitable and enduring multi-institutional collaborations. Ultimately, the successful translation of federated learning into widespread clinical and commercial practice will require treating communication efficiency, privacy preservation, and fair incentive alignment not as separate research threads but as interdependent pillars of a single, resilient collaborative learning ecosystem. It is our hope that this survey serves as both a map of the current landscape and a call to action for the rigorous, holistic research that the field now demands.
