# 1 Abstract

Federated learning has emerged as a transformative paradigm for collaborative machine learning, enabling model training across decentralized data sources while preserving data privacy, with particularly significant applications in healthcare, clinical text analysis, and cross-institutional radiological imaging. This survey provides a comprehensive examination of recent advances in privacy-preserving federated learning frameworks, focusing on novel architectures and mechanisms designed to address the inherent challenges of data heterogeneity, communication efficiency, and security in distributed environments. We systematically review intention-hiding vertical federated learning, which introduces enhanced privacy guarantees by concealing participant query intent, alongside layer-skipping strategies that substantially reduce communication overhead for large language models by selectively transmitting only a subset of model parameters during federated fine-tuning. Additionally, we analyze selective attention mechanisms for clinical text classification that prioritize informative document segments without exposing raw data, integrated pricing and privacy-preserving data valuation frameworks that resolve conflicts between accurate contribution assessment and confidentiality, and triple-shuffle mechanisms within contribution-aware serial federated learning designed to simultaneously improve privacy, fairness, and model performance in cross-silo healthcare settings. Our analysis reveals persistent trade-offs between formal privacy guarantees, such as those offered by differential privacy and homomorphic encryption, and practical considerations including model accuracy degradation and computational overhead. These findings highlight the critical need for holistic frameworks that jointly optimize privacy protection, communication efficiency, and learning performance in real-world federated deployments.


# 2 Introduction

Federated learning has emerged as a transformative paradigm for collaborative machine learning, enabling model training across decentralized data sources while preserving data privacy, with particularly significant applications in healthcare, clinical text analysis, and cross-institutional radiological imaging. The proliferation of digital health technologies and the widespread adoption of electronic health records have generated vast repositories of clinical data with immense potential to advance medical research and improve patient outcomes. Machine learning, particularly deep learning, has demonstrated remarkable capabilities in analyzing complex medical data, from radiological images to unstructured clinical text, enabling tasks such as disease classification, early diagnosis, and treatment recommendation. However, the sensitive nature of healthcare data imposes stringent regulatory and ethical constraints on data sharing and centralization, creating a fundamental tension between the need for large-scale, diverse datasets to train robust models and the imperative to protect patient confidentiality. Federated learning offers a pathway to harness the collective intelligence of multi-institutional healthcare data while upholding privacy principles by distributing computation to where data resides, thereby enabling collaborative model training without requiring raw data to leave local premises.

Despite its conceptual promise, the translation of federated learning from theoretical frameworks to real-world clinical deployment is fraught with multifaceted challenges. A primary concern lies in the inherent heterogeneity of healthcare data across institutions, which manifests as non-identically distributed data distributions, varying feature spaces, and inconsistent labeling protocols, severely degrading model convergence and generalization when conventional federated averaging algorithms are naively applied. Beyond statistical heterogeneity, the privacy guarantees of standard federated learning are increasingly recognized as insufficient, as model updates and gradients can inadvertently leak sensitive patient information through sophisticated inference attacks, motivating the development of advanced techniques such as differential privacy, homomorphic encryption, and intention-hiding mechanisms. Concurrently, the communication efficiency of federated training remains a critical bottleneck, particularly when deploying large-scale models such as transformers and large language models in bandwidth-constrained clinical environments, with strategies such as layer-skipping and selective attention mechanisms proposed to mitigate these costs. Furthermore, the question of fair and privacy-preserving data valuation in collaborative healthcare networks introduces additional complexity, as institutions require equitable compensation for their data contributions without compromising the confidentiality of their patient populations. These interconnected challenges collectively underscore the need for holistic frameworks that jointly optimize privacy protection, communication efficiency, and learning performance in real-world federated deployments.

This survey provides a comprehensive and structured examination of the key architectural innovations, communication-efficient strategies, and deployment considerations that define the current landscape of federated learning for privacy-preserving healthcare. The first major contribution is a systematic taxonomy of federated learning architectures tailored to clinical contexts, spanning horizontal, vertical, and hybrid configurations, and their extensions to address specific healthcare privacy requirements. We analyze intention-hiding vertical federated learning as a notable advancement that conceals the purpose behind participant queries, extending privacy guarantees beyond data protection to encompass the concealment of computational intent in competitive or adversarial collaborative environments. The survey further examines triple-shuffle mechanisms within contribution-aware serial federated learning designed to simultaneously improve privacy, fairness, and model performance in cross-silo healthcare settings with segmented medical data. We dissect the application of these architectures across diverse clinical domains, including chest radiograph analysis, breast density classification, and clinical text mining, highlighting how federated methods demonstrate particular benefits for smaller datasets in non-identically distributed settings and how selective attention frameworks can focus computation on informative document segments without compromising local data sovereignty.

The second major contribution is an in-depth analysis of communication efficiency and privacy-utility trade-offs that govern practical federated deployment. We critically evaluate layer-skipping strategies that freeze subsets of pre-trained model layers to dramatically reduce bandwidth requirements, as demonstrated with large language models on clinical named entity recognition and discharge summary benchmarks, preserving the knowledge encoded in foundation models while substantially lowering communication overhead. The survey synthesizes evidence on the interplay between differential privacy guarantees and model accuracy degradation, particularly under heterogeneous data conditions, and contrasts these with the computational costs of cryptographic approaches such as homomorphic encryption. We further address the emerging challenge of privacy-preserving data valuation, examining integrated frameworks that assess client data quality and contribution to federated training while maintaining confidentiality, thereby resolving the inherent conflict between accurate contribution assessment and robust privacy protection. By consolidating insights from both algorithmic innovations and empirical evaluations, this survey identifies persistent gaps between theoretical privacy assurances and practical clinical requirements, offering a roadmap for researchers and practitioners seeking to navigate the complex design space of federated healthcare systems.

The aim of this survey is to equip researchers, clinicians, and system architects with a holistic understanding of the state-of-the-art in federated learning for healthcare, bridging the gap between privacy research and clinical informatics. The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. We first establish foundational concepts in federated learning and healthcare privacy, then present our taxonomy of architectures and their clinical instantiations, including intention-hiding vertical federated learning and contribution-aware serial frameworks. Subsequent sections delve into communication-efficient training paradigms, with detailed examinations of layer-skipping strategies and selective attention mechanisms for clinical text classification, followed by an analysis of privacy-preserving mechanisms, their trade-offs, and data valuation frameworks. We conclude with a critical discussion of open challenges and future directions for translating federated learning into routine clinical practice, emphasizing the need for integrated approaches that jointly address privacy, fairness, efficiency, and equitable data valuation.

# 4 Communication-Efficient Federated Training of Clinical LLMs

## 4.1 Layer-Skipping Strategies for Federated LLMs

Layer-skipping strategies reduce the communication cost of federated learning for large language models by transmitting updates for only a subset of model parameters while keeping the remaining layers frozen. Wen et al. (2024) proposed an approach that selectively freezes a majority of layers in pre-trained LLMs during federated fine-tuning, communicating updates for only the unfrozen subset. This method preserves the knowledge encoded in the frozen layers from pre-training while dramatically reducing bandwidth requirements; the authors implemented and evaluated their approach using LLaMA models (Wen et al., 2024).

A related static layer-skipping baseline is described by Tang et al. (2023), who include a "Layer-Skipping FL" method that freezes bottom layers during federated training. In their experimental comparisons for medical data, this static layer-skipping approach is benchmarked against standard FedAvg with full model updates and a state-of-the-art parameter-efficient method (FedPepTAO), alongside centralized training as an upper bound (Tang et al., 2023). The evidence indicates that freezing a fixed set of early layers constitutes a straightforward strategy within the broader design space of layer-skipping for federated LLMs.

## 4.2 Selective Attention Federated Learning for Clinical Text Classification

The application of federated learning to clinical text classification must contend with the heterogeneous and often redundant nature of real-world medical notes. Wen et al. (2024) evaluated their federated approach on standard clinical NLP benchmarks, including the i2b2 2010 Clinical Concept Extraction task for medical named entity recognition and MIMIC-III discharge summaries, demonstrating the practical viability of distributed training on de-identified clinical text. Building on such settings, Tang et al. (2023) introduced a Selective Attention Federated Learning framework that aligns with the privacy philosophy of keeping sensitive user-generated text on-device, as previously shown in systems that predicted depression and anxiety from smartphone keyboard and speech data without central data collection. Their proposed method aims to improve efficiency while preserving this privacy guarantee, suggesting that selective attention mechanisms can be integrated into federated training to focus computation on the most informative parts of clinical documents without requiring raw text to leave local sites.

# 5 Privacy-Preserving Data Valuation and Serial Federated Deployment

## 5.1 Privacy-Preserved Data Valuation and Pricing Mechanisms

A central tension in data markets is balancing accurate data valuation with privacy protection. Rajagopal et al. (2023) explicitly aim to address this by designing an integrated pricing and privacy-preserving framework that resolves the conflict between data privacy and data pricing. Their approach evaluates individual client data based on its contribution to federated learning training, using a score function to assess data quality before training commences. While the specific privacy mechanism is not detailed in the provided evidence, the stated goal is a unified solution that simultaneously handles valuation and privacy.

The broader federated learning literature highlights the trade-offs inherent in privacy-preserving techniques that would underpin such valuation frameworks. Tang et al. (2023) note that methods like differential privacy, as used in systems such as PrivateFL, offer formal privacy guarantees but often cause substantial degradation in model accuracy, particularly in highly heterogeneous data environments. Similarly, they observe that cryptographic approaches like homomorphic encryption provide protection but consistently incur high computational costs and complexity. These observations underscore the practical challenges that any privacy-preserved data pricing mechanism must navigate to remain both effective and efficient.

## 5.2 Triple-Shuffle and Contribution-Aware Serial Federated Learning

Tang et al. (2023) propose a triple-shuffle and contribution-aware serial federated learning framework designed for segmented healthcare data in cross-silo environments. The framework aims to simultaneously enhance privacy, fairness, and learning performance. A core component is a triple-shuffle mechanism that randomizes data representations, though further specifics are not detailed in the provided evidence. This work is situated within the broader context of federated learning, which Rajagopal et al. (2023) describe as a mainstream privacy-preserving paradigm that enables machine learning model training without exposing private data, with applications spanning medical information systems, financial data analysis, and cross-border corporate data integration.

# 6 Future Directions

The current landscape of federated learning for healthcare is constrained by several persistent limitations that hinder its translation from research prototypes to routine clinical deployment. Privacy-preserving mechanisms remain trapped in a fundamental trade-off, where formal guarantees from differential privacy consistently degrade model accuracy under heterogeneous data conditions, while cryptographic approaches such as homomorphic encryption impose computational overheads that are prohibitive for resource-constrained clinical environments. Intention-hiding vertical federated learning has been conceptually introduced but lacks mature protocol implementations, formal security proofs, and rigorous empirical validation across diverse medical use cases, leaving a critical gap in scenarios where query patterns themselves reveal sensitive institutional priorities. Data valuation frameworks that simultaneously address pricing and privacy protection are still nascent, with no established methodology for fairly compensating data contributors while maintaining robust confidentiality guarantees. Furthermore, the integration of parameter-efficient techniques such as layer-skipping with selective attention mechanisms for clinical text remains underexplored, particularly regarding how these combined strategies perform under extreme data heterogeneity and across different medical specialties. The complete absence of quantum federated learning architectures in the current medical literature represents a significant unexplored frontier, leaving unexamined the potential advantages that quantum computing could bring to distributed healthcare model training.

Future research should prioritize the development of integrated frameworks that jointly optimize privacy, fairness, and valuation within a unified operational paradigm. A promising direction involves designing adaptive privacy budgets that dynamically adjust based on real-time assessments of data heterogeneity levels and contribution quality, potentially reconciling the accuracy-privacy tension that currently limits deployment in heterogeneous clinical networks. Extending intention-hiding mechanisms beyond their initial conceptualization into fully operational protocols, complete with formal security proofs and comprehensive empirical benchmarks on real-world medical datasets, would address pressing needs in competitive or adversarial collaborative environments where computational intent itself constitutes sensitive information. The convergence of parameter-efficient fine-tuning with selective attention mechanisms warrants systematic investigation, particularly exploring whether attention-based filtering can identify and prioritize clinically salient text segments while simultaneously reducing communication overhead, and evaluating how such combined approaches perform under varying degrees of data heterogeneity across radiology, pathology, and clinical note domains. Another vital avenue is the exploration of quantum federated learning architectures tailored to medical applications, investigating whether quantum neural networks can offer advantages in modeling complex biomedical data distributions, accelerating convergence in non-IID settings, or providing stronger privacy guarantees through quantum cryptographic primitives. Additionally, the establishment of standardized benchmarks and evaluation protocols that realistically simulate the non-IID, resource-constrained, and institutionally fragmented environments characteristic of real-world healthcare deployments is essential for reproducible progress.

Advancing these research directions could fundamentally transform collaborative healthcare artificial intelligence by enabling institutions to jointly train models on sensitive data with unprecedented privacy assurances and computational efficiency. Integrated privacy-valuation frameworks would unlock data markets where hospitals and research centers are fairly incentivized to participate while maintaining patient confidentiality, accelerating the development of robust diagnostic models that generalize across diverse populations and demographic groups. Adaptive privacy mechanisms that respond to data heterogeneity would make federated learning viable for smaller institutions with limited computational resources, democratizing access to high-quality medical AI and particularly benefiting underrepresented patient cohorts that stand to gain the most from collaborative learning paradigms. Quantum federated approaches, if successfully realized, could potentially solve currently intractable privacy and optimization challenges, opening new frontiers in multi-institutional biomedical research and enabling analyses at scales previously considered infeasible. Collectively, these advances would bridge the persistent gap between theoretical privacy research and practical clinical requirements, establishing federated learning as a trusted and routine methodology for collaborative healthcare intelligence that respects the inviolable primacy of patient data sovereignty.


# 7 Conclusion

This survey has systematically examined the evolving landscape of privacy-preserving federated learning, with a particular focus on its applications in healthcare and the emerging strategies designed to address persistent challenges in communication efficiency, data heterogeneity, and security. The analysis reveals a clear trajectory from conventional horizontal and vertical federated learning paradigms toward more sophisticated frameworks that incorporate intention-hiding mechanisms, selective parameter updating, and contribution-aware aggregation. Key findings indicate that layer-skipping strategies, such as freezing pre-trained model components during federated fine-tuning, offer a practical and effective means of reducing communication overhead for large language models without sacrificing the representational power encoded in foundation models. Concurrently, the integration of selective attention mechanisms into federated training demonstrates the potential to focus computational resources on the most salient portions of clinical documents, thereby improving efficiency while maintaining the fundamental privacy guarantee of keeping raw patient data on local devices. The survey also highlights the critical tension between accurate data valuation and robust privacy protection, a conflict that demands unified frameworks capable of simultaneously assessing data quality and enforcing formal privacy guarantees. Furthermore, the exploration of advanced shuffling mechanisms and serial training protocols in cross-silo healthcare environments underscores the field's commitment to reconciling the competing demands of fairness, model performance, and stringent data confidentiality.

The significance of this survey lies in its comprehensive synthesis of cutting-edge techniques that collectively define the next generation of federated learning systems for sensitive domains. By bridging the gap between theoretical privacy mechanisms and practical deployment in medical imaging, clinical text analysis, and multi-institutional radiological studies, this work provides a structured understanding of the trade-offs that practitioners must navigate. The discussion of intention-hiding vertical federated learning extends the privacy discourse beyond data protection to encompass the concealment of computational intent, a critical consideration in competitive or adversarial collaborative environments. Similarly, the examination of data pricing frameworks that operate without compromising privacy addresses a pressing need in data marketplaces, where equitable compensation for data contributions must coexist with uncompromising confidentiality. These insights are particularly consequential for the healthcare sector, where the promise of collaborative model training across institutions has long been tempered by regulatory constraints and the inherent sensitivity of patient information. The survey thus serves as both a roadmap and a critical reference for researchers and system designers seeking to implement federated learning solutions that are not only technically sound but also ethically and legally compliant.

As the field advances, the research community must prioritize the development of holistic frameworks that seamlessly integrate efficiency, fairness, and verifiable privacy into a unified operational paradigm. Future efforts should move beyond treating these objectives as isolated optimization targets and instead pursue architectures where privacy-preserving data valuation, adaptive communication reduction, and robust aggregation are co-designed from inception. The persistent accuracy degradation observed with formal privacy mechanisms such as differential privacy, particularly in heterogeneous data settings, signals an urgent need for novel algorithmic approaches that can deliver rigorous protection without prohibitive performance costs. Equally important is the need for standardized benchmarks and evaluation protocols that realistically simulate the non-IID, resource-constrained, and institutionally fragmented environments characteristic of real-world healthcare deployments. Only through such concerted and interdisciplinary effort can federated learning fulfill its transformative potential as a mainstream paradigm for collaborative intelligence that respects the inviolable primacy of data privacy.
