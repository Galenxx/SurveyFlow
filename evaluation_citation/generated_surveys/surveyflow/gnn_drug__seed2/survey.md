# 1 Abstract

The integration of artificial intelligence into drug discovery has fundamentally reshaped virtual screening and de novo molecular design, enabling rapid candidate identification and property optimization through advanced computational strategies. This survey provides a comprehensive examination of graph neural network architectures and molecular featurization techniques employed in virtual screening pipelines, with particular emphasis on how molecular topology is encoded, processed, and leveraged for predictive modeling. We systematically review directed message passing frameworks, including Directed Graph Attention Networks, which incorporate bond directionality and attention mechanisms to enhance atom- and molecule-level representations, alongside expert-based models that integrate domain-specific structural knowledge to improve classification accuracy and interpretability. Our analysis reveals a persistent tension between the convenience of linearized topological descriptors and their capacity to capture global molecular properties such as symmetry and long-range dependencies, highlighting key limitations in current graph-level classification approaches that treat molecular graphs as undifferentiated wholes. These findings underscore the critical importance of substructure-aware representation learning and directional information for improving generalization in molecular property prediction tasks.


# 2 Introduction

The discovery and development of new therapeutic agents remains one of the most challenging and resource-intensive endeavors in modern science, traditionally requiring over a decade of research and billions of dollars in investment to bring a single drug to market. In response to these formidable demands, the integration of computational methods into the drug discovery pipeline has fundamentally reshaped how researchers approach the identification and optimization of novel bioactive compounds. Among the most transformative advances is the application of artificial intelligence, which now enables predictive models capable of estimating molecular properties, forecasting synthetic feasibility, and prioritizing promising candidates with remarkable efficiency. These computational strategies have become indispensable in virtual screening, where large libraries of existing molecules are evaluated against biological targets, and in de novo drug design, where entirely new chemical entities are generated to meet predefined pharmacological criteria. Central to these advances is the challenge of representing molecular structures as mathematical objects suitable for machine learning, a task that directly influences the performance, interpretability, and generalization capacity of predictive models. Graph neural networks have emerged as particularly powerful tools in this context, offering a natural framework for encoding molecular topology and learning from the rich structural information inherent in chemical compounds.

Despite the remarkable progress achieved through AI-driven approaches, several persistent challenges continue to limit their full potential in drug discovery applications. A fundamental tension exists between the convenience of molecular representations and their capacity to capture the full complexity of chemical phenomena. While molecular topology provides an intuitive foundation for graph-based representations, conventional encodings often struggle to preserve essential structural features such as symmetry, reversibility, and long-range dependencies that are critical for accurate property prediction. Many graph neural network architectures treat molecular graphs as homogeneous entities, failing to account for the distinct functional roles played by different substructures and thereby limiting their ability to generalize to novel chemical spaces. The design of message passing algorithms, which govern how information propagates through molecular graphs, remains a critical bottleneck, as standard implementations may overlook directional information inherent in chemical bonds and the varying importance of different molecular regions. Furthermore, the comparative performance of graph neural networks against classical molecular featurization techniques in specific tasks such as quantitative structure-activity relationship modeling and activity-cliff prediction remains an area requiring systematic investigation. These interconnected challenges underscore the pressing need for a comprehensive examination of how graph neural networks can be refined, evaluated, and optimally deployed for molecular property prediction.

This survey makes several contributions to the understanding and advancement of graph neural networks for drug discovery and molecular property prediction. First, it provides a systematic synthesis of recent methodological developments, with particular emphasis on architectures that address the limitations of conventional graph representations. The survey examines how directed message passing frameworks, such as Directed Graph Attention Networks, incorporate bond directionality and attention mechanisms to enhance the fidelity of molecular representations. By representing chemical bonds as distinct directed edges and applying scaled dot-product attention to update both atom and bond states, these models offer a more nuanced treatment of molecular topology that better reflects the underlying chemistry. The survey further explores how attention-based readout functions aggregate layer-wise representations for molecule-level prediction, highlighting the architectural choices that influence model expressivity and performance on the small molecular graphs typical of drug discovery applications. Through this detailed examination, the survey illuminates the trade-offs between representational power and computational efficiency that practitioners must navigate when selecting graph neural network architectures for specific tasks.

Second, this survey critically evaluates the integration of domain-specific structural knowledge into graph-level classification frameworks, an increasingly important direction for improving both accuracy and interpretability. It examines expert-based modeling strategies, including weighted combinations of expert outputs and scaffold alignment techniques, which have demonstrated enhanced performance in molecular property prediction by embedding chemical intuition directly into model architectures. By contrasting these approaches with purely data-driven methods, the survey illuminates the trade-offs between model complexity, computational efficiency, and predictive power, while also highlighting the potential for hybrid strategies that combine the strengths of both paradigms. The discussion extends to the comparative landscape of molecular featurization, situating graph neural networks within the broader context of classical descriptors and fingerprint-based methods. Through this analysis, the survey identifies key areas where graph neural networks offer distinct advantages, such as their ability to learn task-specific representations directly from molecular topology, as well as scenarios where traditional approaches may remain competitive, thereby providing practical guidance for practitioners navigating the increasingly diverse toolkit of computational drug discovery.

The aim of this survey is to offer a structured and critical overview of the current state of graph neural networks in drug discovery, with a focus on molecular property prediction and virtual screening applications. The paper is organized as follows. The opening sections establish foundational concepts in molecular representation and the theoretical underpinnings of graph neural networks, including message passing frameworks and expressivity considerations that determine what structural information can be captured and propagated. Subsequent sections delve into specific architectural innovations, including directed and attention-based models, and examine how these advances address known limitations of earlier approaches, particularly regarding the preservation of directional bond information and the handling of molecular symmetry. The survey then transitions to a comparative analysis of graph neural networks and classical featurization techniques, drawing on available computational studies to assess relative performance across key benchmarks and task types. The final sections discuss open challenges and promising research directions, including the development of substructure-aware architectures, the integration of three-dimensional conformational information, and the need for rigorous benchmarking frameworks, offering a forward-looking perspective on how graph neural networks can continue to evolve as indispensable tools in the pursuit of novel therapeutics.

# 4 Message Passing and Meta-Learning in Molecular Prediction

## 4.1 Bond-Based Directed Message Passing with Attention

Gong and Maday (2023) proposed Directed Graph Attention Networks (D-GATs), a model that incorporates a scaled dot-product attention mechanism for updating both bond states and atom states. In this framework, a chemical bond between two atoms is represented as two distinct directed edges within the backbone of the architecture. For molecule-level prediction tasks, an attention-based readout function is applied at the end of each layer.

## 4.2 Graph-Level Classification for Molecular Topological Structures

Graph-level classification for molecular topological structures has been advanced through architectures that explicitly account for directional information in molecular graphs. Gong and Maday (2023) introduced Directed Graph Attention Networks (D-GATs), a model that performs directed message passing based on attention mechanisms and combines the resulting representations with a linear classifier for downstream tasks. The approach is specifically designed for small-size graphs, such as those encountered in most molecular property prediction settings, and the authors highlight its potential as a powerful tool for these tasks, while noting that it is less efficient than undirected graph models.

Complementary strategies leverage expert-based modeling to capture finer-grained structural information. Nguyen et al. (2020) survey approaches within molecular property prediction where expert-based models have demonstrated this capability. They describe GraphDIVE, which utilizes weighted combinations of expert outputs to improve graph classification, and TopExpert, which employs scaffold alignment strategies to enhance both interpretability and accuracy. These methods illustrate the trend toward incorporating domain-specific structural knowledge into graph-level classification frameworks for molecular topologies.

## 4.3 Cross-Graph Inter-Message Passing for Molecular Representation

The design of message passing algorithms is central to the performance of graph neural networks for molecular representation, as models generally follow the Message Passing Neural Network (MPNN) framework (Gong & Maday, 2023). A key limitation of many GNN-based approaches is their tendency to treat molecular graphs as undifferentiated wholes, overlooking the distinct roles and contributions of different substructures, which in turn reduces the model’s capacity for generalization (Nguyen et al., 2020). Gong and Maday (2023) address message passing design by applying a bond-based message passing algorithm inspired by Directed MPNN.

# 6 Future Directions

The current landscape of AI-driven virtual screening and molecular design, while remarkably advanced, is constrained by several interrelated limitations. Graph neural networks have become indispensable tools for molecular representation, yet their reliance on topological encodings often fails to capture essential structural features such as molecular symmetry, reversibility, and long-range dependencies. This shortcoming is compounded by the tendency of many message passing architectures to treat molecular graphs as homogeneous entities, neglecting the distinct functional roles of specific substructures and thereby limiting generalization to novel chemical spaces. Furthermore, directed graph approaches, though promising for capturing bond-level directional information, remain computationally less efficient than their undirected counterparts, creating a practical barrier to their widespread adoption in large-scale screening campaigns. The integration of expert-based structural knowledge has shown potential to mitigate some of these issues, but systematic frameworks for combining domain-specific insights with learned representations are still in their infancy.

Future research should prioritize the development of hybrid architectures that reconcile the efficiency of undirected graph models with the expressive power of directional and substructure-aware mechanisms. One promising direction involves designing hierarchical message passing schemes that dynamically allocate computational resources to functionally critical molecular regions, such as pharmacophores or reactive centers, while maintaining lightweight representations for less informative substructures. This could be achieved through attention-based gating mechanisms that learn to identify and emphasize salient topological features without incurring the full cost of directed message passing across the entire molecular graph. In parallel, novel encoding strategies that explicitly incorporate three-dimensional conformational information and symmetry-aware descriptors into graph-based frameworks could address the persistent gap in capturing global molecular properties. The systematic integration of expert-derived molecular scaffolds and alignment strategies into end-to-end differentiable architectures represents another underexplored avenue, potentially yielding models that are both more interpretable and more robust to distribution shifts in chemical space. Finally, comprehensive benchmarking studies that rigorously compare graph neural networks against classical featurization techniques across diverse prediction tasks, including activity-cliff identification, are urgently needed to establish clear guidelines for model selection and to identify failure modes that remain hidden in aggregate performance metrics.

The pursuit of these research directions holds the potential to fundamentally reshape the virtual screening pipeline by delivering models that combine computational tractability with a deeper, more chemically meaningful understanding of molecular structure. Improved representations that capture symmetry, long-range interactions, and substructure-specific contributions could dramatically enhance the accuracy of property prediction and the reliability of generative molecular design, ultimately reducing the time and cost required to advance candidates from computational screens to experimental validation. Moreover, establishing rigorous comparative frameworks between classical and deep learning approaches will provide the community with evidence-based guidance, fostering greater reproducibility and accelerating the translation of methodological innovations into tangible therapeutic discoveries.


# 7 Conclusion

This survey has systematically examined the role of graph neural networks in virtual screening and molecular property prediction, revealing a landscape characterized by both remarkable progress and persistent methodological tensions. The analysis demonstrates that directed message passing frameworks, particularly Directed Graph Attention Networks, address critical limitations of conventional graph representations by explicitly encoding bond directionality and employing attention mechanisms to refine atom- and bond-level representations. These architectural innovations enable more faithful modeling of molecular topology, yet the survey also highlights that many current approaches continue to treat molecular graphs as homogeneous entities, overlooking the distinct functional contributions of specific substructures. Expert-based modeling strategies, including scaffold alignment and weighted expert outputs, have shown considerable promise in improving classification accuracy and interpretability, suggesting that the integration of domain-specific structural knowledge represents a productive direction for future development. Furthermore, the comparative analysis underscores that while graph neural networks offer distinct advantages in capturing topological relationships, classical featurization techniques remain competitive in certain prediction tasks, and systematic benchmarking across diverse chemical spaces remains insufficiently addressed in the existing literature.

The significance of this survey derives from its integrated examination of molecular representation, architectural design, and practical deployment considerations within a unified analytical framework. By tracing the evolution from foundational message passing paradigms to contemporary directed and attention-based models, this work clarifies how specific design choices influence model expressivity, generalization capacity, and computational efficiency. The critical assessment of linearized topological descriptors and their failure to preserve global properties such as symmetry and long-range dependencies provides researchers with a nuanced understanding of the trade-offs inherent in current representational strategies. Moreover, the survey contextualizes graph neural networks within the broader ecosystem of molecular featurization methods, offering practical guidance for practitioners who must navigate an increasingly complex array of computational tools. This synthesis is particularly valuable as the field grapples with the challenge of translating methodological advances into robust, reproducible improvements in hit identification and lead optimization workflows.

The path forward demands concerted efforts to develop molecular representations that reconcile representational fidelity with computational tractability. Researchers should prioritize the design of hierarchical architectures that dynamically allocate modeling resources to pharmacologically relevant substructures while maintaining efficiency across large-scale screening campaigns. The systematic integration of three-dimensional conformational information and symmetry-aware descriptors into graph-based frameworks represents a critical frontier for capturing global molecular properties that remain elusive to current methods. Equally important is the establishment of rigorous, standardized benchmarking protocols that enable fair comparisons between graph neural networks and classical featurization techniques, particularly in challenging scenarios such as activity-cliff prediction where subtle structural variations produce disproportionate functional consequences. Collaborative initiatives that bring together computational scientists, medicinal chemists, and structural biologists will be essential to ensure that future innovations are grounded in chemical reality and directly address the practical demands of drug discovery. Through sustained commitment to both theoretical advancement and empirical validation, the field can realize the transformative potential of graph neural networks in accelerating the development of novel therapeutics.
