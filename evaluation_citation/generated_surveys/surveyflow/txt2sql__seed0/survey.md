# 1 Abstract

The translation of natural language questions into executable SQL queries remains a fundamental challenge in natural language processing, with schema linking—the alignment of textual references to database tables and columns—serving as a critical component of text-to-SQL systems. This survey systematically examines recent advances in schema linking and query generation, focusing on the paradigm shifts introduced by large language models and their implications for robust, schema-aware SQL generation. We review bidirectional schema linking strategies that decompose retrieval into complementary perspectives, data augmentation techniques that address the scarcity of dedicated training sets, and modular reasoning architectures that employ Monte Carlo Tree Search and compounding operator frameworks to manage complex queries. Our analysis reveals a notable tension between explicit schema linking mechanisms and the implicit schema understanding capabilities of large language models, while also highlighting the growing importance of iterative refinement, in-context learning optimization, and agent-based decomposition for handling real-world query complexity. We further discuss emerging concerns at the intersection of model security and efficiency, including the effects of quantization on code generation models and their vulnerability to adversarial attacks. This survey provides a comprehensive overview of the evolving landscape of text-to-SQL research, offering insights into both established methodologies and promising future directions for building reliable and secure systems.


# 2 Introduction

The accurate translation of natural language questions into executable SQL queries, commonly referred to as text-to-SQL generation, stands as a foundational challenge at the intersection of natural language processing and database systems. This task empowers non-technical users to interact with structured data through intuitive natural language interfaces, eliminating the need for expertise in formal query languages. Over the past decade, the field has undergone a remarkable transformation, evolving from early rule-based systems and carefully engineered pipelines to sophisticated deep learning architectures that leverage sequence-to-sequence models and graph-based neural networks. The recent emergence of large language models has further revolutionized this landscape, introducing models capable of processing entire database schemas within their context windows and generating complex SQL queries with unprecedented fluency and accuracy. These models have not only pushed performance boundaries on established benchmarks but have also fundamentally altered how researchers conceptualize the core sub-tasks of text-to-SQL generation, particularly the role of schema understanding and the strategies for handling query complexity.

Despite these significant advances, several critical challenges continue to impede the development of robust and reliable text-to-SQL systems. A central tension exists around the role of schema linking, the process of identifying which tables, columns, and relationships within a database schema are relevant to a given natural language question. While earlier architectures treated schema linking as an explicit, dedicated component, the powerful contextual reasoning capabilities of large language models have raised fundamental questions about whether such explicit mechanisms remain necessary. Evidence suggests that well-reasoned models can implicitly perform schema mapping when the full schema fits within their context window, yet this implicit approach often proves insufficient for complex queries involving large, domain-specific schemas or ambiguous references. Compounding this challenge is the scarcity of dedicated training datasets with explicit schema annotations, forcing researchers to rely on heuristically parsed ground truth that introduces noise and limits the fidelity of learned alignments. Beyond schema linking, the robustness of text-to-SQL systems faces emerging threats from adversarial manipulations, including backdoor attacks that can persist through model compression techniques such as quantization. Additionally, the inherent complexity of multi-step reasoning over extensive schemas demands new architectural strategies that decompose query generation into manageable sub-problems while maintaining coherence and guarding against compounding errors across stages.

This survey makes two primary contributions to the understanding of text-to-SQL generation in the era of large language models. First, it provides a comprehensive examination of schema linking strategies, tracing the conceptual evolution from explicit architectural components in early graph-based models to the implicit capabilities of modern large language models. We analyze bidirectional schema linking frameworks that decompose retrieval into complementary table-first and column-first perspectives, capturing deeper relationships between question tokens and schema elements through structured graph representations. Our analysis extends to data augmentation techniques that address the scarcity of labeled schema linking data, including methods that parse existing SQL statements to extract ground-truth schema references and approaches that automatically generate authentic question-SQL pairs with minimal annotation cost. We further investigate modular reasoning architectures that employ compounding operators and Monte Carlo Tree Search to manage schema selection and query generation through structured decomposition, highlighting how these strategies navigate the trade-off between exploration and exploitation in complex reasoning spaces.

Second, this survey offers an in-depth analysis of robustness challenges in text-to-SQL systems, with particular attention to security vulnerabilities and the effects of model compression techniques. We examine the complex interplay between quantization and adversarial attacks, revealing that while quantization can reduce vulnerability to certain jailbreaking attempts, it may also enable new information recovery threats and fail to eliminate backdoor trojans in code generation models. The survey further investigates test-time scaling strategies and retrieval-aware optimization methods that enhance robustness during SQL generation under constrained context windows, as well as the role of in-context learning with schema-infused prompts for improving selection accuracy. By synthesizing findings across schema linking, benchmark evaluation, and robustness research, we identify critical gaps in current approaches and outline promising directions for building more reliable, secure, and schema-aware text-to-SQL systems.

The aim of this survey is to provide researchers and practitioners with a structured overview of the current state of text-to-SQL generation with large language models, focusing on the interconnected themes of schema linking, benchmarks, and robustness. The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. We begin by reviewing the evolution of schema linking techniques, examining the shift from explicit methods to implicit approaches enabled by large language models, and analyzing bidirectional, view-based, and modular strategies for schema-aware query generation. We then explore fine-tuning strategies for code language models, including the effects of quantization on adversarial robustness and data augmentation techniques for addressing training data scarcity. Subsequently, we investigate modular compound generation frameworks that leverage Monte Carlo Tree Search and compounding operators for iterative SQL refinement, before discussing schema-infused in-context learning approaches. We conclude with a forward-looking discussion of open challenges and future research directions, emphasizing the need for unified frameworks that jointly learn schema linking and query generation while maintaining robustness against adversarial threats.


# 3 Context-Aware Schema Linking for Text-to-SQL

## 3.1 Bidirectional Schema Linking and Retrieval

Câmara et al. (2024) observe that schema linking is often treated as a one-shot filtering step, and they propose a bidirectional strategy to address this limitation. Their approach decomposes the retrieval process into two complementary perspectives: table-first and column-first schema linking, aiming to help the LLM more accurately contextualize and interpret the underlying database schema. The effectiveness of this bidirectional schema linking framework is evaluated in terms of schema retrieval performance and its downstream impact on SQL generation (Câmara et al., 2024).

A separate challenge for schema linking is the absence of dedicated training sets. Vichev and Marchev (2024) note that existing text-to-SQL training datasets provide (question, schema context)–SQL pairs without explicitly indicating the chosen schema. To obtain ground-truth tables and columns for schema linking, they parse the SQL statement associated with a given question.

## 3.2 View-Based Schema Linking

The role of explicit schema linking in text-to-SQL systems has been reexamined with the emergence of large language models. Câmara et al. (2024) note that the advent of LLMs has reignited debate on the necessity of explicit schema linking, observing that well-reasoned LLMs may implicitly perform schema linking, especially when the full schema fits within the model’s context window. This perspective challenges earlier approaches that incorporated schema linking as an explicit architectural component, such as RAT-SQL (Câmara et al., 2024).

A practical challenge for training explicit schema-linking models lies in the nature of available datasets. Vichev and Marchev (2024) point out that existing text-to-SQL training datasets provide data in the form of question, schema, and SQL statement triples without indicating which parts of the schema were actually chosen for the query. To address this absence of a dedicated training set for schema linking, they parse the SQL statement related to a given question to obtain the ground-truth tables and columns used (Vichev & Marchev, 2024).

## 3.3 Robust Pre-processing for Schema Linking

The emergence of large language models (LLMs) has reignited debate on the necessity of explicit schema linking, with some arguing that well-reasoned LLMs may implicitly perform schema linking, especially when the full schema fits within the model’s context window (Câmara et al., 2024). This shift in perspective has prompted researchers to reconsider how schema information is prepared and presented to models, moving beyond traditional one-shot filtering approaches.

Câmara et al. (2024) introduce a bidirectional schema linking strategy that decomposes the retrieval process into two complementary perspectives: table-first and column-first schema linking. This approach allows the LLM to more accurately contextualize and interpret the underlying database schema by capturing deeper relationships between question tokens and schema elements through schema-linking graphs.

To improve the robustness of schema linking models, Vichev and Marchev (2024) employ data enhancement techniques on the training dataset. Their approach expands the original text-to-SQL training data, which consists of triplets containing the input text query, the complete database schema, and the target SQL statement, thereby strengthening the model's ability to handle diverse schema linking scenarios.

# 5 Modular Compound Text-to-SQL Generation

## 5.1 Modular Reasoning and MCTS-Based Candidate Selection

Recent work has explored modular reasoning architectures that decompose complex data query tasks into manageable sub‑problems, combined with Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) to guide candidate selection. Liu et al. (2024) propose an agent-based approach in which MCTS progressively decomposes a problem into candidate reasoning trajectories. This formulation is designed to alleviate the difficulty that moderate‑scale large language models encounter when attempting to generate complete reasoning chains in a single step. The authors note that, unlike beam search or self‑consistency methods, MCTS explicitly balances exploration and exploitation over the search space of possible reasoning paths.

In a related direction, Maamari et al. (2025) introduce a compounding operator framework for enterprise text‑to‑SQL. Their system begins with an initial operator that selects relevant task examples—pairs of natural language queries and SQL statements—from a knowledge set. The selected examples subsequently inform the choice of relevant instructions, which are natural language hints or term definitions, and these in turn guide the selection of pertinent schema elements. While this work does not employ MCTS, it illustrates a modular, multi‑stage candidate selection process where the output of one operator conditions the input of the next, similarly aiming to manage complexity through structured decomposition and informed retrieval.

## 5.2 Compounding Operators and Iterative SQL Refinement

The refinement of SQL queries through iterative and multi-step processes has emerged as a key strategy for handling complex text-to-SQL tasks. Liu et al. (2024) note that multi-agent and iterative reasoning have been explored to address challenges where computation is implicit, rewards become coarse in math-intensive queries, and performance is unstable under tight budgets. In a complementary direction, Maamari et al. (2025) introduce the concept of compounding operators within their GenEdit system, where SQL generation is decomposed into a sequence of operators. In this design, the output of each prior operator is fed into the subsequent one, as exemplified by a context expansion step that retrieves relevant contextual information for the input query before generation proceeds.

## 5.3 Schema-Infused In-Context Learning for SELECT Operations

Schema-infused in-context learning for SELECT operations has been advanced through modular, agent-based approaches that explicitly optimize how schema information is selected and utilized within constrained context windows. Liu et al. (2024) describe retrieval-aware optimization strategies that mitigate schema context cost, noting that in-context reinforcement learning combined with retrieval-augmented generation improves database and table selection under limited context. Their work further highlights test-time scaling strategies, as explored in Agentar-Scale-SQL (Wang et al., 2025a), which allocate inference budgets to enhance robustness during SQL generation.

A complementary perspective is offered by Maamari et al. (2025), who frame the selection problem as a compounding sequence of operators within the GenEdit framework. In their approach, an initial operator selects relevant task examples—pairs of natural language queries and SQL—and this example selection subsequently informs the choice of relevant instructions, such as hints or term definitions. The selection of both examples and instructions then guides the identification of pertinent schema elements, creating a cascading refinement process that infuses schema knowledge into the in-context prompt in a targeted manner.

# 6 Future Directions

Current text-to-SQL research faces several interconnected limitations that hinder the development of truly robust and practical systems. Schema linking remains a fragmented practice, oscillating between explicit preprocessing pipelines and complete reliance on implicit reasoning within large language models, yet neither approach consistently resolves ambiguity when confronting large, evolving, or domain-specific schemas. The scarcity of dedicated, high-quality schema linking datasets forces researchers to rely on heuristically parsed ground truth from SQL statements, introducing noise that degrades the fidelity of learned alignments. Furthermore, while modular reasoning architectures and iterative refinement strategies show promise for handling complex queries, they introduce significant orchestration overhead and remain susceptible to compounding errors across multiple stages. The interplay between efficiency techniques like model quantization and adversarial vulnerabilities, such as backdoor attacks, remains critically underexplored in the text-to-SQL pipeline, leaving open questions about the security of deployed systems.

Future research should move beyond the explicit-implicit dichotomy by developing unified frameworks that jointly learn schema linking and SQL generation through multi-task objectives. Such frameworks could leverage bidirectional schema linking graphs as dynamic intermediate representations that are iteratively refined during query construction, enabling the model to revise its initial schema interpretation as it builds the SQL statement. To address data scarcity, researchers should develop automated curation pipelines that construct high-quality schema linking datasets through consensus-based parsing across multiple SQL dialects and database systems, reducing the noise inherent in single-source heuristic extraction. On the architectural front, integrating Monte Carlo Tree Search with compounding operator pipelines offers a compelling direction, where search strategies guide the selection and ordering of modular operators while dynamically balancing exploration of alternative schema interpretations against inference budget constraints. This integration should be complemented by adaptive test-time scaling strategies that allocate additional computation to ambiguous schema elements or particularly complex query segments. Critically, future work must systematically investigate the relationship between data augmentation, model quantization, and adversarial robustness, ensuring that efficiency gains do not inadvertently amplify vulnerabilities to poisoned schema elements or maliciously crafted natural language inputs.

Advancing these research directions would yield text-to-SQL systems with substantially greater reliability in enterprise environments where database schemas evolve continuously and domain-specific terminology is pervasive. Unified schema linking and generation would eliminate the brittle failure modes that arise when initial schema selection proves incorrect, enabling systems to recover gracefully through iterative refinement rather than producing irrecoverable errors. The integration of search-based reasoning with modular decomposition would democratize access to complex analytical queries, empowering non-technical users to engage in sophisticated data exploration without SQL expertise. Moreover, establishing robustness guarantees against adversarial manipulation and ensuring stability under model compression would build essential trust for deployment in sensitive domains such as healthcare, finance, and legal analytics, where query accuracy and data confidentiality are non-negotiable requirements. Collectively, these advances would transform text-to-SQL from a promising research direction into a dependable technology for real-world decision-making.


# 7 Conclusion

This survey has examined the evolving landscape of text-to-SQL generation through three interconnected lenses: schema linking strategies, data augmentation and fine-tuning methodologies, and modular reasoning architectures. A central finding is the paradigm shift from explicit, architectural schema linking components toward the implicit schema understanding capabilities of large language models, a transition that has fundamentally altered how researchers approach the alignment between natural language questions and database structures. Bidirectional schema linking frameworks, which decompose retrieval into complementary table-first and column-first perspectives, demonstrate that even within implicit paradigms, structured preprocessing can enhance contextualization and downstream SQL accuracy. Concurrently, the persistent scarcity of dedicated schema linking datasets has spurred innovative data augmentation techniques, including SQL parsing for ground-truth extraction and bidirectional generation of authentic question–SQL pairs, which collectively address the training data bottleneck. The emergence of modular, agent-based reasoning systems that employ Monte Carlo Tree Search and compounding operator pipelines further reflects a growing recognition that complex queries demand structured decomposition and iterative refinement rather than monolithic end-to-end generation. These developments collectively indicate a field in active transition, balancing the raw generative power of large language models with the need for interpretable, controllable, and robust query construction mechanisms.

The significance of this survey resides in its synthesis of these seemingly disparate research threads into a coherent narrative about the current state and future trajectory of text-to-SQL systems. By juxtaposing schema linking, data preparation, and reasoning architecture, the survey reveals critical interdependencies that are often obscured in isolated studies: the effectiveness of modular reasoning is contingent upon the fidelity of schema grounding, which in turn depends on the quality and representativeness of training data. This holistic perspective is particularly valuable for practitioners deploying text-to-SQL systems in enterprise environments, where schemas are large, domain-specific, and continuously evolving, and where the cost of incorrect query generation can be substantial. The survey also brings to the forefront underappreciated robustness concerns, including the interaction between model quantization and adversarial vulnerabilities, highlighting that efficiency gains must be carefully weighed against potential security compromises. By mapping the terrain from explicit schema linking to implicit reasoning, and from single-pass generation to iterative, search-guided decomposition, this work provides a structured framework for understanding both the achievements and the unresolved tensions in contemporary text-to-SQL research.

Moving forward, the research community should prioritize the development of unified evaluation frameworks that simultaneously assess schema linking accuracy, reasoning trace correctness, and end-to-end query generation quality, as progress in one dimension can mask regressions in others. The integration of data augmentation directly into modular reasoning pipelines, where synthetic examples are generated dynamically to support specific sub-task operators, offers a promising avenue for reducing annotation costs while improving generalization to unseen domains. Additionally, adaptive strategies that dynamically select between explicit and implicit schema linking based on query complexity and schema size warrant deeper investigation, as rigid adherence to a single paradigm limits robustness across diverse deployment scenarios. As text-to-SQL systems become increasingly embedded in high-stakes applications across healthcare, finance, and legal domains, the community must also intensify efforts to understand and mitigate adversarial threats, ensuring that the pursuit of accuracy and efficiency does not come at the cost of reliability and trustworthiness.
