# 1 Abstract

The accurate translation of natural language questions into executable SQL queries remains a central challenge in text-to-SQL systems, with schema linking serving as a critical component for aligning user intent with database structure. Recent advances in large language models have fundamentally reshaped this landscape, prompting a reevaluation of whether explicit schema linking mechanisms remain necessary when models can process entire database schemas within their expanded context windows. This survey provides a systematic examination of emerging schema linking paradigms and associated preprocessing techniques, while also investigating complementary developments in supervised fine-tuning and modular reasoning frameworks that collectively define the modern text-to-SQL pipeline.

Our analysis categorizes schema linking approaches into bidirectional retrieval methods, view-based two-stage frameworks, and schema-aware prompt engineering strategies, revealing a clear trend toward structured, multi-phase processes that balance retrieval accuracy with computational efficiency. We further synthesize findings on data augmentation pipelines, joint loss tuning with confusion-aware noisy schema sampling, and the integration of Monte Carlo Tree Search for decomposing complex query generation tasks. The survey highlights the model-dependent nature of hybrid encoding strategies and identifies persistent challenges including training data scarcity, robustness to adversarial perturbations, and the underexplored interaction between model quantization and text-to-SQL performance. By connecting these diverse research threads, this survey offers a comprehensive overview of the evolving schema linking landscape and its integration into contemporary text-to-SQL architectures.


# 2 Introduction

The accurate translation of natural language questions into executable SQL queries, commonly referred to as Text-to-SQL generation, has long been a central challenge in natural language processing and database research. This task bridges the gap between human linguistic expression and structured data retrieval, enabling non-expert users to interact with relational databases without requiring specialized query language knowledge. Early approaches relied heavily on rule-based systems and template matching, which gradually evolved into neural sequence-to-sequence models that learned direct mappings between questions and queries. More recently, the emergence of large language models has fundamentally transformed the landscape, introducing unprecedented capabilities in semantic understanding, reasoning, and code generation. These models have demonstrated remarkable proficiency in interpreting complex natural language instructions and generating syntactically correct SQL, even in zero-shot and few-shot settings. However, the integration of large language models into Text-to-SQL pipelines has also surfaced new challenges and reopened longstanding debates regarding the optimal design of intermediate processing stages, the role of explicit schema representation, and the robustness of generated outputs under varying conditions.

Despite the impressive advances brought by large language models, several critical problems persist in the Text-to-SQL domain. A primary challenge lies in schema linking, the process of aligning natural language question elements with corresponding database schema components such as tables and columns. The research community remains divided on whether explicit schema linking mechanisms are still necessary when large language models can potentially perform this alignment implicitly within their context windows. Furthermore, the effectiveness of different retrieval and encoding strategies for schema elements varies significantly across model architectures, complicating the development of universal solutions. Beyond schema linking, the scarcity of high-quality training data poses a substantial obstacle for supervised fine-tuning approaches, particularly for specialized SQL generation models. Additional concerns have emerged regarding the robustness of Text-to-SQL systems, including their susceptibility to adversarial perturbations, the impact of model compression techniques such as quantization on performance stability, and the challenges of decomposing complex reasoning tasks into manageable sub-problems. These interconnected issues highlight the need for a comprehensive examination of current methodologies and their limitations.

This survey makes several contributions to the understanding of Text-to-SQL generation with large language models. First, it provides a systematic analysis of schema linking strategies, categorizing approaches into bidirectional retrieval methods, view-based two-stage processes, and schema-aware prompt pre-processing techniques. The survey examines how these methods address the fundamental tension between explicit and implicit schema linking, and evaluates their effectiveness across different model scales and architectures. By synthesizing findings from recent studies on hybrid encoding strategies that combine semantic and traditional retrieval methods, the survey offers insights into the model-dependent nature of schema linking performance and identifies conditions under which specific approaches prove most beneficial. This analysis extends to the practical deployment considerations of schema reduction techniques, which are particularly relevant for smaller language models operating under computational constraints.

Second, the survey advances the discourse on training methodologies and robustness challenges in Text-to-SQL systems. It examines supervised fine-tuning strategies, including data augmentation pipelines and specialized loss functions designed to improve model resilience to schema variations. The survey also investigates emerging concerns at the intersection of model compression and security, particularly how quantization techniques interact with the persistence of adversarial behaviors in language models used for SQL generation. Additionally, the survey explores modular reasoning frameworks that employ structured search methods, such as Monte Carlo Tree Search, to decompose complex query generation tasks into manageable reasoning trajectories. By connecting these diverse threads of research, the survey provides a unified perspective on the current state of Text-to-SQL technology and identifies critical gaps in the literature that warrant further investigation.

The aim of this survey is to provide researchers and practitioners with a structured overview of the key developments, challenges, and open questions in Text-to-SQL generation using large language models. # 3 Context-Aware Schema Linking via In-Context Learning

## 3.1 Bidirectional Retrieval for Schema Linking

Bidirectional retrieval for schema linking addresses the limitation of treating schema linking as a single filtering step. Câmara et al. (2024) introduce a bidirectional strategy that decomposes the retrieval process into two complementary perspectives: table-first and column-first schema linking. This approach is designed to help the LLM more accurately contextualize and interpret the underlying database schema. The evidence from Câmara et al. (2024) also notes that the emergence of large language models has reignited debate on the necessity of explicit schema linking, with some researchers arguing that well-reasoned LLMs may implicitly perform schema linking when the full schema fits within the model’s context window.

## 3.2 View-Based Two-Stage Schema Linking


View-based two-stage schema linking addresses the challenge of aligning natural language questions with database schema elements through a structured, multi-phase process. Câmara et al. (2024) propose a methodology consisting of two primary stages: Question Augmentation and Bidirectional Schema Linking. The initial stage enriches the natural language query, while the subsequent bidirectional linking stage establishes connections between the augmented question and the schema. This approach is situated within a broader context where the role of explicit schema linking is being reevaluated. As noted by Câmara et al. (2024), the emergence of large language models has reignited debate on whether explicit schema linking remains necessary, with some arguing that well-reasoned LLMs may perform schema linking implicitly when the full schema fits within the model's context window.

The effectiveness of schema linking can be further influenced by the underlying retrieval and encoding strategies employed. Sergeev et al. (2025) demonstrate that combining semantic encoders with traditional tf-idf encoders can lead to significant improvements in accuracy for search tasks relevant to database interaction. Their experiments show performance gains of +0.028 for the e5-large model and +0.024 for the te-3-large model when using the combined approach over the semantic model alone. However, this benefit is not universal, as combining the bge-m3 model with tf-idf did not yield the same positive results, highlighting the model-dependent nature of such hybrid encoding strategies in schema-related retrieval.

## 3.3 Schema-Aware Prompt Pre-processing for In-Context Learning

Schema-aware prompt pre-processing can improve in-context learning by reducing the complexity of the database context passed to a language model. Câmara et al. (2024) propose a fully prompt-based approach that employs multi-stage retrieval to efficiently reduce schema size and the number of LLM calls, which enhances practical deployment. By simplifying the schema context, this strategy may notably boost the performance of smaller LLMs, enabling accurate SQL generation even in constrained settings (Câmara et al., 2024). Separately, Sergeev et al. (2025) discuss how various prompt engineering techniques, such as few-shot prompting, can be applied to enhance task performance. Few-shot prompting allows the inclusion of multiple transformation examples within the instruction and helps specify edge cases that refine the primary instruction (Sergeev et al., 2025).

## 4.1 SQL-Centric Data Augmentation for Supervised Fine-Tuning

A central challenge in supervised fine-tuning for SQL tasks is the scarcity of high-quality training data. Câmara et al. (2024) address this issue directly by augmenting the training data before fine-tuning their CodeS models, a series of language models ranging from 1B to 15B parameters designed specifically for SQL. The augmentation step is positioned as a prerequisite for effective fine-tuning, after which the models are evaluated on standard test sets.

The broader landscape of supervised fine-tuning for Text-to-SQL reveals increasingly specialized strategies. Neogi et al. (2024) note that while early approaches relied on direct language model fine-tuning, later methods have decomposed the problem into distinct stages. For instance, DTS-SQL and DB-Explore treat schema linking as a separate fine-tuning stage, and the ROUTE framework further refines this modular approach. These developments highlight a trend toward structured fine-tuning pipelines, though the specific data augmentation techniques employed within these pipelines are not detailed in the available evidence.

The approach builds on a next-token prediction loss defined over the SQL query tokens, where the model is conditioned on the prefix, schema, and previously generated query tokens. The authors introduce a noisy schema sampling mechanism that is guided by confusion awareness, aiming to make the model more robust to schema variations during fine-tuning. The integration of Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) into modular reasoning frameworks offers a structured approach to decomposing complex problems. Liu et al. (2024) propose using MCTS to progressively break down a problem into candidate reasoning trajectories. This method specifically addresses the limitations of moderate-scale large language models, which often struggle to generate complete reasoning chains in a single step. A key advantage of this MCTS-based formulation over alternatives such as beam search or self-consistency is its explicit balancing of exploration and exploitation across the search space (Liu et al., 2024).

# 6 Future Directions

The current landscape of schema linking and text-to-SQL generation reveals several important limitations that warrant further investigation. Bidirectional and view-based schema linking strategies have shown promise, yet their evaluation remains largely confined to standard benchmarks with clean schemas and well-formed questions, leaving their robustness to ambiguous, incomplete, or adversarial queries underexplored. The effectiveness of hybrid encoding strategies, such as combining semantic and sparse retrievers, appears highly model-dependent, and no systematic framework exists to predict when such combinations will yield improvements. Data augmentation and fine-tuning techniques, including confusion-aware noisy schema sampling and joint loss tuning, are described only at a high level, with insufficient detail on implementation choices, noise injection strategies, or loss weighting schemes. Additionally, the interaction between model compression techniques like quantization and the reliability of fine-tuned text-to-SQL models remains poorly understood, particularly regarding how parameter approximation affects schema understanding and query generation fidelity. Finally, while modular reasoning frameworks employing Monte Carlo Tree Search have been introduced, their application to iterative SQL refinement with compounding operations has not been addressed, leaving open questions about how structured search can improve complex, multi-step query construction.

Future research should prioritize the development of comprehensive evaluation protocols that test schema linking methods under realistic conditions, including ambiguous natural language, evolving database schemas, and queries requiring implicit contextual reasoning. A systematic investigation into the conditions under which hybrid encoding strategies succeed or fail would provide valuable guidance for practitioners, potentially leading to adaptive retrieval mechanisms that dynamically select encoding combinations based on query and schema characteristics. The fine-tuning pipeline for text-to-SQL models would benefit from detailed ablation studies that isolate the contributions of data augmentation, loss design, and schema sampling strategies, enabling the community to establish best practices and reproducible configurations. Further work should also examine the interplay between model quantization and text-to-SQL performance, specifically investigating whether quantization disproportionately affects schema linking accuracy or introduces subtle degradations in query correctness that are not captured by standard metrics. Extending Monte Carlo Tree Search frameworks to support iterative SQL refinement with compounding operators represents another promising direction, where the search process could explicitly reason about intermediate query states, operator composition, and incremental schema alignment.

Advancing these research directions would significantly strengthen the practical deployment of text-to-SQL systems in real-world environments. Robust evaluation protocols would reveal failure modes that currently remain hidden, guiding the development of more resilient models capable of handling the messiness of actual user queries and database schemas. A deeper understanding of hybrid encoding and adaptive retrieval would enable more efficient schema linking, reducing computational overhead while maintaining or improving accuracy, which is critical for resource-constrained settings. Establishing clear guidelines for fine-tuning pipelines and their interaction with model compression would lower the barrier to entry for organizations seeking to deploy specialized text-to-SQL models without extensive trial-and-error experimentation. Finally, incorporating structured search into iterative query refinement could unlock the ability to generate complex, multi-step SQL that mirrors the compositional reasoning processes of expert database users, ultimately making database interaction more accessible to non-technical stakeholders and broadening the impact of natural language interfaces to structured data.


# 7 Conclusion

The accurate translation of natural language questions into executable SQL queries remains a central challenge in text-to-SQL systems, and this survey has systematically examined the evolving landscape of schema linking and its integration into modern architectures. Our analysis reveals a decisive shift away from monolithic, single-step schema linking toward structured, multi-phase approaches that balance retrieval accuracy with computational efficiency. Bidirectional retrieval mechanisms, which decompose schema linking into table-first and column-first perspectives, offer more robust contextualization of database structures within language model prompts. View-based two-stage frameworks further enhance this process by enriching natural language queries before establishing schema connections, while schema-aware prompt preprocessing techniques reduce context complexity and improve performance for smaller models operating under computational constraints. The investigation of hybrid encoding strategies demonstrates that combining semantic encoders with traditional tf-idf methods can yield measurable accuracy gains, though their effectiveness remains highly model-dependent. In the domain of supervised fine-tuning, data augmentation pipelines and confusion-aware noisy schema sampling have emerged as promising solutions to the persistent challenge of training data scarcity, while joint loss tuning strategies enhance model resilience to schema variations. Furthermore, the integration of Monte Carlo Tree Search into modular reasoning frameworks provides a structured method for decomposing complex queries, explicitly balancing exploration and exploitation to overcome the limitations of moderate-scale models that struggle with single-step reasoning chain generation.

The significance of this survey lies in its synthesis of recent, fragmented advances into a coherent overview that captures both the progress made and the persistent challenges defining contemporary text-to-SQL research. By bringing together developments in bidirectional schema linking, view-based preprocessing, hybrid encoding, and structured fine-tuning, the paper illuminates the fundamental tension between explicit and implicit schema linking strategies—a debate that carries substantial implications for system design and resource allocation in the era of large language models with expansive context windows. The survey further broadens its relevance by connecting text-to-SQL performance to wider concerns in model deployment and trustworthiness, particularly through the examination of how quantization techniques interact with adversarial robustness and the persistence of trojan behaviors. For practitioners, the surveyed techniques offer actionable insights into building more accurate and efficient systems, while for researchers, the identified gaps point to promising directions for future investigation. The comprehensive analysis of training methodologies, including data augmentation strategies and specialized loss functions, provides a foundation for establishing reproducible best practices in an area where implementation details are often underspecified.

The text-to-SQL community must now push beyond incremental improvements by confronting the fundamental limitations that remain underexplored. Rigorous benchmarking of schema linking strategies across diverse database schemas, ambiguous natural language queries, and evolving data structures is essential to reveal failure modes that current evaluation protocols obscure. Researchers should prioritize the development of open, high-quality training datasets that capture the full spectrum of real-world SQL challenges, including nested queries, domain-specific jargon, and implicit contextual reasoning. Equally pressing is the need to investigate iterative refinement mechanisms that allow models to correct and compound partial results through structured search processes, an area that remains notably underdeveloped despite its potential to mirror the compositional reasoning of expert database users. The interaction between model compression and text-to-SQL fidelity warrants deeper examination to ensure that deployment efficiency does not come at the cost of subtle degradations in query correctness. By pursuing these directions with methodological rigor, the community can advance toward text-to-SQL systems that are not only accurate on standard benchmarks but also robust, interpretable, and secure in practical deployment scenarios, ultimately making database interaction more accessible to non-technical stakeholders and broadening the impact of natural language interfaces to structured data.
