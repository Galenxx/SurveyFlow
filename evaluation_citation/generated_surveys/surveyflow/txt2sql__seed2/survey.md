# 1 Abstract

Schema linking, the task of aligning natural language queries with relevant database tables and columns, is a fundamental component that critically shapes the performance of modern text-to-SQL systems. This survey provides a comprehensive examination of recent advances in schema linking and its evolving role within broader text-to-SQL pipelines, with a particular focus on modular, bidirectional, and robustness-oriented approaches. We review the shift from treating schema linking as a one-shot filtering step toward more sophisticated strategies, including bidirectional retrieval that decomposes the task into table-first and column-first perspectives, and confusion-aware noisy schema sampling that enhances model robustness against ambiguous linking outputs. We further analyze the integration of schema linking into compounding operator pipelines and Monte Carlo tree search frameworks, which structure SQL generation into iterative, modular sub-tasks to improve candidate quality. Additionally, we explore complementary directions such as schema augmentation with data-driven statistics, in-context learning under constrained context windows, and the implications of model quantization on adversarial robustness in code-focused large language models. By synthesizing these developments, this survey highlights the maturation of schema linking from a peripheral preprocessing step into a central, multi-faceted optimization point for reliable and accurate text-to-SQL generation.


# 2 Introduction

Text-to-SQL generation, the task of automatically translating natural language questions into executable SQL queries, has become a pivotal technology for democratizing access to relational databases. By allowing non-expert users to interact with data through everyday language, these systems eliminate the traditional barrier of requiring deep technical knowledge of database schemas and query languages. The rapid evolution of large language models has dramatically accelerated progress in this field, enabling the generation of increasingly sophisticated and accurate SQL queries across diverse domains. However, deploying these models in real-world enterprise environments introduces challenges that extend far beyond basic query generation. Systems must contend with complex, often ambiguous database schemas containing hundreds of tables, maintain reliable performance under noisy or adversarial conditions, and operate efficiently within practical computational constraints. As the field matures, the research community has increasingly recognized that the path to robust text-to-SQL systems requires careful attention to the entire generation pipeline, from initial schema interpretation through final query synthesis.

Despite significant advances, several fundamental problems continue to challenge the text-to-SQL community. Schema linking, the critical process of aligning user queries with relevant tables and columns, remains a primary bottleneck that directly determines downstream generation accuracy. The research community remains divided on whether explicit schema linking mechanisms are necessary in an era where large language models can potentially perform this alignment implicitly when entire schemas fit within context windows. Beyond this debate, questions of robustness loom large: how do text-to-SQL systems behave when confronted with noisy or ambiguous schema linking outputs, adversarial perturbations, or varying inference configurations such as model quantization? The emergence of modular architectures and compounding operator pipelines raises additional questions about how to optimally structure the generation process to balance accuracy with computational efficiency, particularly when handling mathematically complex queries or operating under constrained inference budgets. These interconnected challenges underscore the need for a systematic examination of how schema linking strategies, robustness techniques, and modular reasoning frameworks collectively shape system performance.

This survey makes two primary contributions to the understanding of text-to-SQL generation with large language models. First, it delivers a comprehensive and structured analysis of schema linking strategies, tracing their evolution from traditional one-shot filtering approaches to sophisticated bidirectional and view-based two-stage methods. We examine how recent frameworks decompose schema linking into complementary perspectives—such as table-first and column-first retrieval—and critically assess the ongoing debate regarding the necessity of explicit schema linking in the context of increasingly capable language models. By synthesizing findings from multiple research threads, we offer a nuanced perspective on when and how explicit schema linking mechanisms contribute to overall system performance, and how these mechanisms interact with other pipeline components including data augmentation and in-context learning. This analysis reveals that schema linking has matured from a peripheral preprocessing step into a central, multi-faceted optimization point that fundamentally shapes the reliability of text-to-SQL generation.

Second, this survey provides an in-depth examination of robustness challenges and modular architectures that define the current frontier of text-to-SQL system design. We analyze how techniques such as confusion-aware noisy schema sampling, Monte Carlo tree search-based modular reasoning, and compounding operator pipelines address the brittleness that emerges in complex query scenarios. The survey explores the intersection of model quantization with security and robustness, presenting empirical evidence on how precision reduction affects both performance and vulnerability profiles in code-focused large language models. Additionally, we investigate complementary directions including schema augmentation with data-driven statistics and retrieval-aware optimization for constrained context windows. By covering these interconnected topics within a unified analytical framework, we provide researchers and practitioners with a holistic view of the current technological landscape, highlighting both established best practices and promising directions for future investigation.

The aim of this survey is to equip readers with a systematic understanding of the key technical challenges and solutions in modern text-to-SQL generation, with particular emphasis on schema linking, benchmarking practices, and robustness considerations. The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. We begin by establishing foundational concepts and tracing the evolution of text-to-SQL systems from early template-based approaches to contemporary large language model-driven architectures. Subsequent sections provide detailed examinations of schema linking methodologies, including bidirectional and view-based strategies, followed by an analysis of preprocessing pipelines and robustness techniques. We then explore modular reasoning frameworks and compounding operator architectures, before turning to data augmentation and in-context learning approaches for schema-aware generation. The survey concludes with a discussion of open challenges and future research directions, offering guidance for both academic researchers and industry practitioners seeking to build reliable, scalable text-to-SQL systems.


# 3 Schema Linking with Bidirectional Retrieval for Text-to-SQL

## 3.1 Context-Aware Bidirectional Retrieval for Schema Linking

Schema linking, which aligns user queries with relevant tables and columns, is a critical component that directly influences the effectiveness of text-to-SQL systems (You et al., 2024). While traditionally treated as a one-shot filtering step, Câmara et al. (2024) introduced a bidirectional strategy that decomposes the retrieval process into two complementary perspectives: table-first and column-first schema linking. This approach is designed to help a large language model more accurately contextualize and interpret the underlying database schema.

## 3.2 View-Based Two-Stage Schema Linking

View-based two-stage schema linking decomposes the complex text-to-SQL task into two sequential steps: first linking the natural language question to relevant database schema elements, and then synthesizing the logical SQL structure from those linked components (You et al., 2024). This decomposition treats schema linking as a distinct optimization point, where the goal is to accurately connect user queries to corresponding fields in the database.

Câmara et al. (2024) propose a bidirectional schema linking approach as part of their SQL-to-text generation framework. Their methodology consists of two primary stages: question augmentation and bidirectional schema linking, with the initial stage enriching the natural language query before schema connections are established. The emergence of large language models has introduced debate about whether explicit schema linking remains necessary, as some work suggests that well-reasoned LLMs may perform schema linking implicitly when the full schema fits within the model's context window (Câmara et al., 2024).

## 3.3 Bidirectional Schema Linking with Preliminary SQL

Recent work has increasingly treated schema linking as a distinct and critical stage within the text-to-SQL pipeline. You et al. (2024) describe a decomposition-based approach that separates the task into schema linking and logical synthesis, noting that prior research commonly focuses on schema linking as a key optimization point for accurately connecting user queries to the corresponding database fields. Building on this recognition of schema linking as an independent problem, Câmara et al. (2024) argue that treating it as a one-shot filtering step is insufficient. Instead, they introduce a bidirectional strategy that decomposes the retrieval process into two complementary perspectives: table-first and column-first schema linking. In the table-first direction, relevant tables are retrieved first, followed by column selection within those tables; in the column-first direction, columns are retrieved first, followed by table selection. This bidirectional framework is designed to help the large language model more accurately contextualize and interpret the underlying database schema.

# 4 Robust Text-to-SQL via Noisy Schema Sampling

## 4.1 Backdoor Trojans and Model Quantization in Code LLMs

Recent work has begun to examine how model quantization interacts with adversarial vulnerabilities in large language models, including code-focused systems. Hussain et al. (2025) investigate the effects of quantization on backdoor trojans in code LLMs, specifically using Llama 2 models. Their study provides empirical evidence on how reducing model precision influences the persistence or suppression of trojan behaviors in code generation contexts. In related work on foundation models, Kumar et al. (as cited in Hussain et al., 2025) found that quantization significantly reduced LLMs’ vulnerability to jailbreaking attacks across models such as Mistral, Llama, and MosaicML, while Zhang et al. (as cited in Hussain et al., 2025) demonstrated that quantization can enable the recovery of sensitive information. These findings collectively suggest that quantization introduces complex trade-offs between security, robustness, and privacy in code LLMs, though the precise mechanisms by which quantization affects backdoor trojans remain an open area of investigation.

## 4.2 Confusion-Aware Noisy Schema Sampling

Neogi et al. (2024) examined confusion-aware noisy schema sampling (Confusion-aware NSS) as one of three core components in an ablation study of a text-to-SQL fine-tuning pipeline, alongside schema Local Bidirectional Attention and schema Selective Attention. The ablation analysis measured the contribution of Confusion-aware NSS to overall model performance. The evidence does not provide further implementation details or quantitative outcomes specific to this component.

In a separate line of work, Hussain et al. (2025) employed the sql-create-context dataset for fine-tuning code LLMs, formatting each sample with a natural language question, a context containing SQL table structures, and an output. Although their study addresses schema-related data formatting, the available evidence does not establish a direct connection to confusion-aware noisy schema sampling.

## 4.3 Robust Pre-Processing Pipeline with Enhanced Schema Linking

**4.3 Robust Pre-Processing Pipeline with Enhanced Schema Linking**

Neogi et al. (2024) propose a pre-processing pipeline that diverges from conventional multi-stage fine-tuning by jointly training schema linking and SQL generation with a unified loss, thereby eliminating the overhead of separate stages. To further strengthen the pipeline against noisy schema linking outputs, they introduce Confusion-aware Noisy Schema Sampling, a technique that strategically guides SQL generation to improve robustness when faced with redundant or ambiguous schema linking results. In a related investigation of inference pipeline robustness, Hussain et al. (2025) examined how weight quantization affects model behavior and observed that reducing the precision level of the weights prior to inference did not significantly impact the performance of Llama-2, a trend that held across experiments replicated with different dataset splits and seeds.

# 5 Modular Reasoning for Enterprise Text-to-SQL Generation

## 5.1 MCTS-Based Modular Reasoning for SQL Candidate Generation

Recent work has explored Monte Carlo tree search (MCTS) as a framework for modular reasoning in text-to-SQL, decomposing the generation process into structured sub-tasks that guide candidate exploration. Liu et al. (2024) introduced IESR, an MCTS-based approach that organizes SQL generation into modular reasoning steps, and evaluated it across multiple model backbones including Qwen2.5-Coder, XiYanSQL, OmniSQL, and Seed-Coder at the 7B–8B scale. On the LogicCat and Archer-Dev benchmarks, IESR achieved execution accuracy scores that substantially outperformed earlier decomposition-based baselines such as Cot-sql, Din-sql, and DAIL-sql, with the strongest results observed on the Archer-Dev dataset where execution accuracy reached 34.41% under the XiYanSQL 8B configuration (Liu et al., 2024). These results suggest that MCTS-guided modular search can improve SQL candidate quality over simpler chain-of-thought or direct decomposition methods.

A related modular philosophy appears in the GenEdit framework proposed by Maamari et al. (2025), which splits SQL generation into two operators to reduce reliance on extensive LLM reasoning. Their method first produces an initial chain-of-thought plan—a step-by-step natural language description of the target SQL query, where each step describes a sub-statement, clause, or subquery in both natural language and SQL—and then uses this plan in a subsequent generation stage (Maamari et al., 2025). While GenEdit does not employ MCTS, its operator-based decomposition shares the broader motivation of structuring SQL candidate generation into manageable sub-problems, complementing the search-driven modular reasoning demonstrated in IESR.

## 5.2 Compounding Operators for Continuous SQL Improvement

Recent work has shifted attention from one-shot SQL generation toward architectures that compound multiple operators into a continuous improvement loop. Maamari et al. (2025) introduce GenEdit, a framework that organizes the inference phase into a pipeline of compounding operators including retrieval, query reformulation, schema linking, instruction selection, example selection, external knowledge integration, materialized view generation, chain-of-thought generation with pseudo-SQL, SQL generation, and self-correction. This operator chain enables the system to iteratively refine its output rather than relying on a single generation step, directly addressing the brittleness observed in enterprise text-to-SQL settings.

The motivation for such compounding approaches is reinforced by the challenges identified in complex query scenarios. Liu et al. (2024) note that multi-agent and iterative reasoning strategies have been explored to overcome the limitations of implicit computation, where rewards become coarse in mathematically intensive queries and unstable under tight computational budgets. By decomposing the generation process into modular, sequentially applied operators, systems like GenEdit create opportunities for targeted improvement at each stage, allowing corrections to propagate through the pipeline and yielding progressively higher-quality SQL outputs.

## 5.3 Data Augmentation and In-Context Learning for Schema-Aware Generation

**5.3 Data Augmentation and In-Context Learning for Schema-Aware Generation**

Schema-aware generation can be strengthened by augmenting schema representations with data-driven statistics. Maamari et al. (2025) describe a practical strategy in which the schema, obtained directly from the database or from data catalog documentation, is enriched with possible attribute values. Specifically, they append the top-5 most frequent values per attribute to the schema information, providing the model with concrete exemplars that ground abstract column definitions in actual data distributions.

In parallel, in-context learning techniques have been developed to cope with the cost of large schema contexts. Liu et al. (2024) note that retrieval-aware optimization can mitigate schema context cost, pointing to work on in-context reinforcement learning with retrieval-augmented generation that improves database and table selection under constrained context windows (Toteja et al., 2025). The same source highlights test-time scaling strategies that allocate inference budgets to enhance robustness, as explored in Agentar-Scale-SQL (Wang et al., 2025a). Together, these approaches illustrate two complementary directions: augmenting the schema itself with informative data samples, and optimizing how schema information is retrieved and used within limited context windows.

# 6 Future Directions

While recent advances have significantly improved text-to-SQL systems, several critical limitations persist that constrain their deployment in real-world enterprise environments. Schema linking remains fundamentally brittle when confronted with ambiguous column names, domain-specific terminology, and complex multi-table relationships that exceed the reasoning capacity of even the most capable large language models. Current bidirectional and view-based strategies operate under the assumption that schema elements can be unambiguously matched to query fragments, an assumption that frequently breaks down in databases containing hundreds of tables with overlapping semantics and inconsistent naming conventions. The compounding operator pipelines that have demonstrated promise on structured benchmarks introduce cascading failure modes, where errors in early stages such as schema linking or chain-of-thought planning propagate through subsequent operators and resist correction, undermining the reliability of the entire generation pipeline. Furthermore, the interaction between model quantization, adversarial robustness, and schema-aware generation remains poorly understood, with conflicting evidence about whether precision reduction suppresses or amplifies vulnerabilities in code generation contexts. Existing approaches to schema augmentation with data statistics and retrieval-aware context optimization have been studied largely in isolation, without a unified framework for balancing the richness of schema representations against the practical constraints of limited context windows.

Future research should pursue a tighter integration between robust schema linking and self-correcting generation pipelines through the development of uncertainty-aware mechanisms that explicitly model confidence scores for each table and column match. Such mechanisms would enable downstream operators to request clarification or re-ranking when confidence falls below a threshold, preventing the silent propagation of linking errors through the SQL generation process. These confidence estimates could be calibrated using confusion-aware sampling strategies that expose systems to deliberately noisy schema linking outputs during training, thereby hardening the entire pipeline against cascading failures. A complementary direction involves developing adaptive schema representation learning techniques that dynamically adjust the level of detail provided to the model based on query complexity. For simple lookup queries, compact representations with minimal attribute exemplars would suffice, while complex analytical queries involving joins, aggregations, and nested subqueries would trigger the inclusion of value distributions, foreign key paths, and materialized view candidates. Such adaptive compression could be guided by lightweight classifiers or retrieval-augmented selection policies trained to predict the minimal schema context required for a given query type. Systematic investigation is also needed into how quantization and other model compression techniques interact with schema linking fidelity, particularly in multi-tenant deployments where a single compressed model must serve queries across diverse database schemas without catastrophic forgetting or trojan reactivation.

Advancing these research directions would yield substantial practical benefits for enterprise text-to-SQL adoption. Uncertainty-aware and adaptively compressed schema linking would reduce the rate of silent failures where plausible but incorrect SQL is generated from misidentified columns, a failure mode that severely undermines user trust in production analytics interfaces and limits the adoption of natural language querying tools. Hardening the compounding operator pipeline against cascading errors would enable more ambitious decomposition strategies, including Monte Carlo tree search-guided exploration over operator sequences, without incurring the brittleness that currently restricts such approaches to narrow benchmarks. Clarifying the robustness implications of model quantization would provide engineering teams with actionable guidelines for trading off inference latency, memory footprint, and query accuracy, accelerating the deployment of text-to-SQL systems in resource-constrained environments such as on-premise data warehouses and edge analytics platforms. Collectively, these advances would transform text-to-SQL from a promising research technology into a dependable infrastructure component capable of serving diverse enterprise needs with the reliability that production systems demand.


# 7 Conclusion

This survey has examined the evolving landscape of schema linking and modular reasoning in text-to-SQL systems, revealing a decisive shift from treating schema linking as a simple one-shot filtering step toward more sophisticated, multi-stage architectures. The reviewed literature demonstrates that bidirectional schema linking strategies, which decompose retrieval into complementary table-first and column-first perspectives, enable large language models to more accurately contextualize database schemas and establish precise connections between natural language queries and their corresponding structural elements. Furthermore, the integration of confusion-aware noisy schema sampling into unified pre-processing pipelines has emerged as an effective mechanism for improving robustness against redundant or ambiguous schema linking outputs, while eliminating the computational overhead associated with traditional multi-stage fine-tuning. Parallel investigations into Monte Carlo tree search and operator-based decomposition frameworks, exemplified by IESR and GenEdit respectively, illustrate the growing recognition that modular reasoning and compounding operator chains can substantially improve SQL generation quality by enabling iterative refinement and targeted optimization at each stage of the inference process. Additionally, the survey has highlighted important considerations regarding the interplay between model quantization, adversarial robustness, and schema-aware generation, suggesting that precision reduction introduces complex trade-offs that merit careful evaluation in production deployments.

The significance of this survey lies in its synthesis of disparate yet interconnected research threads that collectively define the current frontier of text-to-SQL system design. By systematically examining bidirectional linking, confusion-aware sampling, modular search strategies, and schema augmentation techniques within a unified analytical framework, this work provides researchers and practitioners with a coherent understanding of how individual methodological innovations contribute to the broader goal of robust and accurate natural language database querying. The survey bridges gaps between otherwise isolated contributions, revealing that the most promising directions for future advancement lie not in isolated component optimization but in the thoughtful integration of complementary techniques—such as combining MCTS-guided candidate exploration with data-driven schema enrichment or coupling operator-based decomposition with quantization-aware deployment strategies. This integrative perspective is particularly valuable given the increasing complexity of enterprise text-to-SQL applications, where systems must simultaneously contend with noisy inputs, constrained computational budgets, and diverse database schemas.

Moving forward, the field stands at a critical juncture where continued progress will depend on rigorous empirical investigation into how these modular components interact under realistic deployment conditions. Researchers are encouraged to prioritize the development of standardized benchmarks that capture the multifaceted challenges of schema linking, including ambiguous column names, redundant table structures, and cross-domain schema variations. Equally important is the need for systematic studies examining how quantization, adversarial robustness, and schema linking accuracy jointly influence end-to-end system performance, as the current evidence base remains fragmented. The community should also explore the potential for learned schema linking policies that dynamically adapt their retrieval strategies based on query complexity and schema characteristics, potentially leveraging reinforcement learning or meta-learning paradigms. Ultimately, the path toward truly reliable text-to-SQL systems requires sustained collaboration across the subfields of natural language processing, database systems, and machine learning security, with the shared objective of building systems that are not only accurate but also robust, efficient, and trustworthy in real-world applications.
